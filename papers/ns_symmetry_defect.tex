\begin{abstract}
We give a sufficient regularity criterion when the angular mean of a
three-dimensional Navier--Stokes velocity satisfies prescribed anisotropic
bounds. The additional hypothesis is finiteness of the critical
$L^2_tL^\infty_x$ norm of the angular fluctuation. Its Reynolds stress
vanishes in the anisotropic ancient limit without differentiating the
fluctuation. A circulation barrier for that limit eliminates the swirl;
a cylindrical stretching identity then closes the full-velocity enstrophy
estimate using small mean derivatives. We also give a separate quantitative
gradient-history estimate and exact finite-energy examples of both signs
of kinetic transfer between the mean and the fluctuation.
The proof adapts the axisymmetric argument of Constantin, Ignatova, and
Vicol. This is an analytic conditional criterion, not a Lean theorem or
an arbitrary-data regularity proof.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement}

Let $Q=B(1)\times(-1,0)$ and let $(u,p)$ be a suitable weak solution of
\[
 u_t+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\Delta u+\nabla p=0,\qquad \diver u=0,
\]
smooth on compact subsets of $Q$. Assume the usual local energy class
$u\in L^\infty_tL^2_x\cap L^2_tH^1_x$ and $p\in L^{3/2}(Q)$.
For rotations $Q_\phi$ about the fixed third axis put
\[
 \PP u(x)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}Q_\phi u(Q_\phi^{-1}x)\dd\phi,
 \qquad v=\PP u,\qquad w=u-v.
\]
The same notation denotes the induced action on tensors. Write $r=|x'|$,
$z=x_3$, and $\tau=-t$. Unless a smaller ball is specified, suprema are on $B(1)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Suppose $0<h<1/2$ and, for every $a+b\leq2$,
\begin{align}
 |\partial_r^a\partial_z^b v_r|
 &\leq C\tau^{-1/2-a/2-(1/2-h)b},\label{eq:meanr}\\
 |\partial_r^a\partial_z^b v_z|+|\partial_r^a\partial_z^b v_\theta|
 &\leq C\tau^{-1/2-h-a/2-(1/2-h)b}.\label{eq:meanz}
\end{align}
Set $K(t)=\norm{w(t)}_\infty$. If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:history}
 \int_{-1}^0 K(t)^2\dd t<\infty,
\end{equation}
then $(0,0)$ is a regular point of $u$.
\end{theorem}

The mean bounds are those used in \cite{CIV}. Their validity and
\eqref{eq:history} are hypotheses, not consequences of the energy inequality.
The axis and the ball are fixed; the theorem makes no assertion about
propagating approximate symmetry from a shrinking core. No derivative or
torque integral of $w$ is assumed. The proof uses the full second-order
swirl bounds in \eqref{eq:meanz}; it does not extend the meridional-only
variant of \cite[Remark~5.2]{CIV} to this setting.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:powers}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, either of the following bounds near zero
suffices in place of \eqref{eq:history}:
\[
 K(t)\leq C\tau^{-\beta},\quad 0\leq\beta<\tfrac12;
 \qquad
 K(t)\leq C\tau^{-1/2}\log(e/\tau)^{-p},\quad p>\tfrac12.
\]
\end{corollary}

These implications follow by integrating $K^2$ and restricting to a
final cylinder. In particular no
gradient estimate is inferred from an amplitude bound. A stronger
weighted amplitude condition does pay a gradient history in the
whole-space setting; that independent estimate is in
Section~\ref{sec:smoothing}.

\section{The mean equation and its rescaled stress}

Rotation covariance and $\PP w=0$ give the exact equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean}
 v_t+(v\cdot\nabla)v-\Delta v+\nabla\PP p=F,
 \qquad F=-\PP[(w\cdot\nabla)w]=-\diver\PP(w\otimes w).
\end{equation}
The proof retains this divergence form and does not require an
$L^\infty$ bound on $F$.

The original solution has a regular spherical annulus inside any
prescribed interval of radii. Indeed, partial regularity makes the
top-slice singular set have zero $\mathcal H^1$ measure. Its image under
$x\mapsto|x|$ has measure zero, so some intervening sphere is regular.
Compactness and interior estimates give an annulus on which $u$,
$\nabla u$, and $\nabla^2u$ are bounded through the endpoint, as in the
first part of \cite[Lemma~3.2]{CIV}. Its rotational invariance gives the
same bounds for $v$ and $w$. This argument uses suitability of $u$;
it does not require a partial-regularity theorem for \eqref{eq:mean}.

Write the meridional entries of $\PP(w\otimes w)$ in the cylindrical
frame as $a=R_{rr}$, $b=R_{\theta\theta}$, $c=R_{zz}$, and $d=R_{rz}$.
Their absolute values are at most $K^2$. Direct differentiation yields
\begin{align}
 F_r&=-a_r-(a-b)/r-d_z,& F_z&=-d_r-d/r-c_z,\label{eq:force}\\
 \frac{(\curl F)_\theta}{r}
 &=\left(\partial_{rr}+\frac3r\partial_r-\partial_{zz}\right)\frac d r
   +\frac1r\partial_{rz}(c-a)-\frac1{r^2}\partial_z(a-b).
 \label{eq:lift}
\end{align}
The quantities here are smooth at the axis in the appropriate lifted
variables. For a distributional estimate it suffices that $d=O(r)$.

Consider the rescaling used in \cite[Section~4]{CIV}: for $t_n\uparrow0$,
\[
 \tau_n=-t_n,\quad \lambda_n=\tau_n^{1/2},\quad
 \delta_n=\tau_n^h,\quad \mu_n=\lambda_n/\delta_n,\quad
 r=\lambda_n R,\quad z=z_n+\mu_n Z,\quad t=\tau_n s.
\]
The mean components are normalized as
$V_n=\lambda_n v_r$, $W_n=\lambda_n\delta_n v_z$,
$S_n=\lambda_n\delta_n v_\theta$, and the potential vorticity as
$\Omega_n=\lambda_n^3\delta_n(\curl v)_\theta/r$.
For a stress component $a$, let $a_n$ denote its composition with this
change of variables, without an amplitude normalization.

Lift $R=|X|$ to four radial dimensions. Since
\[
 \frac1R\partial_R H
   =\diver_X\left(\frac{XH}{R^2}\right)-\frac{2H}{R^2},
\]
the rescaled forcing in the potential-vorticity equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled}
\begin{split}
 G_n=\lambda_n^2\delta_n\bigg[&
 (\Delta_X-\delta_n^2\partial_{ZZ})(d_n/R)\\
 &+\delta_n\partial_Z\left\{
  \diver_X\frac{X(c_n-a_n)}{R^2}
  -\frac{2(c_n-a_n)+(a_n-b_n)}{R^2}\right\}\bigg].
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Both $R^{-1}$ and $R^{-2}$ are locally integrable in $\R^4$.
The divergence identity has no axis-supported mass: the flux through
$R=\epsilon$ is $O(\epsilon^2)$ for bounded $H$.
Move all derivatives in \eqref{eq:scaled} onto a compactly supported
smooth test function with time support $J\Subset(-\infty,0)$.
The resulting absolute value is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weaksmall}
 C_J\lambda_n^2\delta_n\int_J K(\tau_n s)^2\dd s
 =C_J\delta_n\int_{\tau_nJ}K(t)^2\dd t\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Thus no derivative of the fluctuation is needed in this part of the
limiting argument. In a recentered zoom with $r=\lambda_n(A_n+R)$ and
$A_n\to\infty$, the azimuthal-vorticity force has the same prefactor
$\lambda_n^2\delta_n$ multiplying at most two derivatives of the stress;
its additional coefficients $(A_n+R)^{-1}$ and $(A_n+R)^{-2}$ are
uniformly bounded on compact sets. Estimate~\eqref{eq:weaksmall}
therefore applies there too.

\section{Eliminating the ancient swirl}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ancientswirl}
Let $S$ on $[0,\infty)\times\R\times(-\infty,-1]$ have uniform
spatial $W^{2,\infty}$ bounds on compact time intervals, be continuous
through the upper time, and satisfy $S(0,Z,s)=0$.
The drifts $V,W$ may be only measurable in time, but are uniformly bounded
and spatially Lipschitz on each such interval. Suppose, for $R>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ancientswirl}
 S_s+V S_R+W S_Z+(V/R)S
                    =S_{RR}+R^{-1}S_R-R^{-2}S
\end{equation}
in distributions. If $h>0$ and
\[
 |S(R,Z,s)|\leq C(-s)^{-1/2-h},\qquad
 |V(R,Z,s)|\leq C_r(-s)^{-1/2},
\]
then $S=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $\Gamma=RS$. Its equation on $R>0$ is
\[
 \mathcal L\Gamma:=\Gamma_s+V\Gamma_R+W\Gamma_Z
                      -\Gamma_{RR}+R^{-1}\Gamma_R=0.
\]
For $L>1$ let $B_L=CL^{-1/2-h}$ and
\[
 \Phi_L(R,s)=B_LR+C_rB_L\int_{-L}^s(-q)^{-1/2}\dd q.
\]
It dominates $|\Gamma|$ at $s=-L$, and
\[
 \mathcal L\Phi_L=B_L\big[C_r(-s)^{-1/2}+V+R^{-1}\big]\geq0.
\]
Comparison is valid on the unbounded spatial domain. On a fixed
slab $[-L,s_1]$, perturb $\Phi_L$ by
\[
 \eta e^{k(s+L)}(1+R^2+Z^2).
\]
The radial diffusion
of $R^2$ is zero for this operator, so $k$ can be chosen in terms of
the bounded drifts to make the added function a supersolution.
Its quadratic growth dominates the linear growth of $\Gamma$ at infinity.
Near the axis, $\Gamma=O(R^2)$ uniformly on this slab, while
$\Phi_L\geq B_LR$. Thus one can excise a sufficiently small axis
cylinder, on whose boundary comparison already holds. On the remaining
bounded region the positive-part energy argument applies: spatial
Lipschitz bounds control the divergence of the drift, and no diffusion
in $Z$ is needed. The distributional equation and spatial second-derivative
bounds give local bounded time derivatives, so the time traces used in
this argument are legitimate. This also justifies the argument for
time-measurable drifts, as in \cite[Lemma~3.3]{CIV}.
Let $\eta\downarrow0$ and apply the comparison to both signs of $\Gamma$.
For a fixed $(R,Z,s)$ it yields
\[
 |\Gamma(R,Z,s)|\leq CRL^{-1/2-h}+2CC_rL^{-h}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(L\longrightarrow\infty).
\]
The upper time is included by continuity.
\end{proof}

To apply the lemma, first take a limit of the actual mean swirl equation.
Write $e=R_{r\theta}$ and $f=R_{z\theta}$ for the corresponding
entries of $\PP(w\otimes w)$. They obey $|e|+|f|\leq2K^2$, and
\[
 F_\theta=-\partial_r e-2e/r-\partial_z f.
\]
In the preceding rescaling the swirl equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaledSwirl}
\begin{split}
 (S_n)_s+V_n(S_n)_R+W_n(S_n)_Z+(V_n/R)S_n
  &=(S_n)_{RR}+R^{-1}(S_n)_R-R^{-2}S_n
                       +\delta_n^2(S_n)_{ZZ}+H_n,\\
 H_n&=-\lambda_n^2\delta_n
       [\partial_R e_n+2e_n/R+\delta_n\partial_Z f_n].
\end{split}
\end{equation}
For a rectangle $D\Subset\{R>0\}$, a closed time slab
$J=[-T,-1]$, and finite $p$, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:swirlforce}
 \norm{H_n}_{L^1(J;W^{-1,p}(D))}
       \leq C_{D,J}\delta_n\int_{\tau_nJ}K(t)^2\dd t
       \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The mean hypotheses uniformly bound $V_n,W_n,S_n$ in spatial $C^2$
on this slab. All other terms of \eqref{eq:scaledSwirl} are bounded
in a negative Sobolev norm. Thus the time increments of $S_n$ in that
norm are bounded by $C|s-s'|+\epsilon_n$, where $\epsilon_n\to0$.
Interpolation with the spatial bounds gives asymptotic equicontinuity
in $C^0$; spatial interpolation then gives locally uniform convergence
of the first spatial derivatives too. Finitely many initial members
are smooth, so this yields the usual compactness after a subsequence.
The selected time $s=-1$ is included. The bounds $S_n(0,Z,s)=0$ and
$\norm{\partial_R S_n}_{\infty}\leq C_J$ extend the convergence
to the axis. Also $\partial_ZS_n(0,Z,s)=0$ and the mixed second
derivative is uniformly bounded.

The drifts have weak-* limits, with the same global spatial Lipschitz
bounds on each compact time slab. The strong convergence of $S_n$ and
its first derivatives permits passage to their products with these
drifts. Equations \eqref{eq:scaledSwirl}--\eqref{eq:swirlforce}
give \eqref{eq:ancientswirl} in the limit. The backward bounds are
\[
 |S(R,Z,s)|\leq C|s|^{-1/2-h},\qquad
 |V(R,Z,s)|\leq C|s|^{-1/2}.
\]
Their constants are independent of the compact spatial set.
Lemma~\ref{lem:ancientswirl} therefore makes this limit zero.

For a recentered zoom with $r=\lambda_n(A_n+R)$ and $A_n\to\infty$,
replace every curvature factor $1/R$ in \eqref{eq:scaledSwirl} by
$1/(A_n+R)$. The force estimate still holds on each fixed rectangle,
and the limiting equation on $\R^2$ is simply
\[
 S_s+V S_R+W S_Z=S_{RR}.
\]
Its bounded solution tends uniformly to zero in the remote past, so
\cite[Lemma~3.3]{CIV} makes it zero as well.

In particular, on every smaller original ball,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:axialSwirlSmall}
 (-t)\norm{\partial_z v_\theta(t)}_\infty\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Otherwise select points and times where this fails and use the same
finite-axis or receding-axis alternatives. At the selected point
$\partial_ZS_n=\lambda_n^2\partial_zv_\theta$ tends to zero.
If its scaled radial coordinate tends to zero, the conclusion instead
follows directly from $\partial_ZS_n(0,Z,s)=0$ and the mixed
second-derivative bound. This proves \eqref{eq:axialSwirlSmall}.

\subsection{A necessary stress input on late time intervals}

The same compactness argument also gives a requirement for sustaining a
nonzero rescaled mean swirl, without assuming \eqref{eq:history}.
It concerns the actual radial and axial fluxes of angular momentum.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:stressgap}
Fix $0<b<1$, $\kappa>0$, $0<h<1/2$, and constants $C,F_0\geq0$.
Consider smooth divergence-free solutions on $B(1)\times(-1,0)$
whose mean satisfies \eqref{eq:meanr}--\eqref{eq:meanz} with constant
$C$, allowing an original force of supremum at most $F_0$.
Let $e=\PP(w_rw_\theta)$, $f=\PP(w_zw_\theta)$ and define
\[
 \mathcal B_\tau(T)=\tau^h\int_{-T\tau}^{-\tau}
       \big(\norm{e(t)}_\infty+\tau^h\norm{f(t)}_\infty\big)\dd t.
\]
There are $T>1$, $\varepsilon>0$, and $0<\tau_0<1/T$, depending
only on the listed constants, such that, for every such solution and
$0<\tau<\tau_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stressgap}
 \tau^{1/2+h}\norm{v_\theta(-\tau)}_{L^\infty(B(b))}\geq\kappa
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
       \mathcal B_\tau(T)\geq\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
The stress norms are over $B(1)$. Infinite integrals satisfy the
conclusion automatically. No local energy or suitability hypothesis is
needed for this swirl statement.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If no such constants existed, choose solutions and scales with
$T_n=n$, $\tau_n<n^{-2}$, and
$\mathcal B_{\tau_n}(n)<1/n$, while the left side of
\eqref{eq:stressgap} is at least $\kappa$. Choose spatial points
realizing at least half this supremum. On every fixed rescaled time
slab the Reynolds source in \eqref{eq:scaledSwirl} then tends to zero
in $L^1_tW^{-1,p}_x$ away from the scaled axis. The original force
contributes at most $\lambda_n^3\delta_n F_0$, which also vanishes.
The uniform mean bounds give precisely the spatial compactness,
time increment bounds, and weak-* drift limits used above.

If the selected radii divided by $\lambda_n$ stay bounded, the
limit is the ancient swirl of Lemma~\ref{lem:ancientswirl}, hence zero.
A selected scaled radius tending to zero is already excluded by
$S_n(0,Z,s)=0$ and the uniform radial derivative bound.
If those radii tend to infinity, recenter there; the limiting bounded
scalar equation is the receding-axis equation above, whose ancient
solution is also zero. In either case convergence at $s=-1$
contradicts the selected value of size at least $\kappa/2$.
The fixed distance of $B(b)$ from the boundary permits every compact
rescaled spatial set in these limits.
\end{proof}

Since $|e|,|f|\leq K^2$, persistent nonzero rescaled mean swirl therefore
requires, on every sufficiently late interval of this fixed ratio,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stresscost}
 \int_{-T\tau}^{-\tau}K(t)^2\dd t
       \geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\tau^{-h}.
\end{equation}
Its root-mean-square defect amplitude on that interval is at least a
constant times $\tau^{-1/2-h/2}$. This is an interval estimate;
it permits intermittency and gives no pointwise lower bound at every time.

The signed quantity preceding this norm estimate has a useful exact form.
Write $\Gamma_n=RS_n$ and let $\psi$ be a smooth test function with
compact spatial support away from $R=0$. Set
\[
 \mathcal A_n^*\psi=\psi_{RR}+\delta_n^2\psi_{ZZ}
       +\partial_R\big((V_n+R^{-1})\psi\big)+\partial_Z(W_n\psi).
\]
For $s_0<s_1$, with spatial integrals in $\dd R\dd Z$, the circulation
equation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dualwork}
\begin{split}
 \left[\int\Gamma_n\psi\right]_{s_0}^{s_1}
 -\int_{s_0}^{s_1}\!\!\int
       \Gamma_n(\psi_s+\mathcal A_n^*\psi)
 =\lambda_n^2\delta_n\int_{s_0}^{s_1}\!\!\int
 \big[e_n(R\psi_R-\psi)+\delta_n f_nR\psi_Z\big].
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Here the original force is zero; otherwise its circulation work is added
on the right. Both sides retain the signs of the actual stress.
The identity follows by integrating
$-R\partial_Re_n-2e_n-\delta_nR\partial_Zf_n$ against $\psi$.
For a backward adjoint solution the bulk mean term would cancel;
\eqref{eq:dualwork} itself needs no such kernel construction.
Neither this identity nor \eqref{eq:stresscost} estimates the recurrent
work from initial data.

\section{Meridional smallness}

Define, on a smaller ball,
\[
 G_v(t)=\norm{|\partial_r v_r|+|v_r/r|
                    +|\partial_z v_z|+|\partial_z v_r|}_\infty.
\]
We verify the two changes needed in the proof of
\cite[Proposition~1.5]{CIV} to conclude
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gsmall}
 (-t)G_v(t)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
First, the preceding swirl argument gives $S_n\to0$ on compact
rescaled time slabs. The mean bounds also give $|S_n/R|\leq C$
on those slabs, including the axis.
Splitting at $R=\epsilon$ shows that $S_n^2/R^2$ tends to zero locally
in $L^1$ in the lifted space. In the receding-axis case the source
is $\partial_Z(S_n^2/(A_n+R))$, which likewise tends to zero
in distributions.

Second, the force is small in a negative Sobolev norm integrated in
time, rather than uniformly small as a function. Away from the axis,
\eqref{eq:scaled} and \eqref{eq:weaksmall} give
\[
 \norm{G_n}_{L^1(J;W^{-2,p}(D))}\longrightarrow0
\]
for a fixed rectangle $D$ and any finite $p$. The analogous assertion
holds in the receding-axis case. The other terms in the rescaled
vorticity equation are uniformly bounded in this negative norm.
Together with the uniform spatial Lipschitz bound supplied by
\eqref{eq:meanr}--\eqref{eq:meanz}, this gives an increment bound
$C|s-s'|+\epsilon_n$ with $\epsilon_n\to0$. Interpolation gives
asymptotic uniform time equicontinuity, also at the selected upper
time. Finitely many initial members are smooth, so Arzel\`a--Ascoli
still applies. Boundedness and the small volume of $R<\epsilon$ give
convergence across the axis.

The remaining ancient-limit argument is unchanged: the force and swirl
source vanish, and the mean bounds supply the backward decay and
bounded Lipschitz drift required by \cite[Lemma~3.3]{CIV}. The limiting
vorticity vanishes. At the selected time, incompressibility and
boundedness of the limiting meridional velocity then contradict a
nonzero selected derivative, in either axis case. This proves
\eqref{eq:Gsmall}. No bound on $\partial_r v_z$ is asserted.

\section{Full-velocity enstrophy}

Choose a radial cutoff $\chi$ whose derivative is supported in a regular
annulus, and write
\[
 Y=\norm{\chi\curl u}_2^2,\quad
 M=\norm{\chi\nabla\curl u}_2^2,\quad
 A_v=\norm{\partial_z v_\theta}_\infty.
\]
Here $A_v$ and $G_v$ are taken on a ball containing the support of $\chi$,
with its boundary in the regular annulus; $K$ is still taken on $B(1)$.
All cutoff errors are uniformly bounded. Weighted $L^2$ contraction
of angular averaging, including its tensor action on gradients, gives
$Y_v\leq Y$ and $M_v\leq M$. Local div--curl estimates consequently
bound the first derivatives of $u,v$ by $C(Y+1)^{1/2}$ and their second
derivatives by $C(M+1)^{1/2}$ in these weighted norms.

For $\Pi_\chi(q)=\int\chi^2(\curl q\cdot\nabla)q\cdot\curl q$, put
$\omega_q=\curl q$. There is an exact telescoping identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:telescope}
\begin{split}
 \Pi_\chi(u)-\Pi_\chi(v)=\int\chi^2\big[&
 (\omega_w\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\omega_u
 +(\omega_v\cdot\nabla)w\cdot\omega_u\\
 &+(\omega_v\cdot\nabla)v\cdot\omega_w\big].
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Integrate the derivative on $w$ off that factor in each term. The
remaining factors have derivative orders one and two, apart from
bounded cutoff errors. Hence, for every $\epsilon>0$,
\begin{align*}
 |\Pi_\chi(u)-\Pi_\chi(v)|
 &\leq CK(Y+1)^{1/2}(M+1)^{1/2}+C\\
 &\leq\epsilon M+C_\epsilon(K^2+1)(Y+1).
\end{align*}
For the mean's stretching, write
$a=(\omega_v)_r=-\partial_z v_\theta$,
$b=(\omega_v)_\theta=\partial_z v_r-\partial_r v_z$, and
$c=(\omega_v)_z=\partial_r v_\theta+v_\theta/r$.
The cylindrical gradient identity underlying \cite[Equation~(5.7)]{CIV}
can be rewritten exactly as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cylindricalStretch}
\begin{split}
 (\omega_v\cdot\nabla)v\cdot\omega_v
 ={}&a^2\partial_r v_r+b^2v_r/r+c^2\partial_z v_z
                    +2ac\partial_z v_r-abc-2ab\,v_\theta/r.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
In particular the large derivative $\partial_r v_z$ has been replaced
using the vorticity relation, and the last two terms have the coefficient
$a$, whose supremum is $A_v$.

A local radial integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialHardy}
 \norm{\chi v_\theta/r}_2^2
 \leq\norm{\chi(\omega_v)_z}_2^2+C.
\end{equation}
Indeed, after expanding $(\partial_r v_\theta+v_\theta/r)^2r$,
its cross term is $\partial_r(v_\theta^2)$. The axis boundary term
vanishes because $v_\theta=O(r)$; the cutoff error is
$2\int\chi\partial_r\chi\,v_\theta^2\dd r\dd z\dd\theta$.
It is bounded on the regular annulus, including its intersection with
the axis, where $\partial_r\chi/r$ is bounded.
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to the last two terms of
\eqref{eq:cylindricalStretch}, and using \eqref{eq:radialHardy}, yields
\[
 \Pi_\chi(v)\leq C(G_v+A_v)(Y_v+1).
\]
Testing the original unforced vorticity equation and absorbing the
small dissipation term from the full-velocity difference now gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 Y'+cM\leq C_*[G_v+A_v+K^2+1](Y+1).
\end{equation}

Fix $\eta>0$. Equations \eqref{eq:Gsmall} and
\eqref{eq:axialSwirlSmall} allow a final interval on which
$G_v+A_v\leq\eta/\tau$. Since $K\in L^2_t$, Gronwall in
\eqref{eq:energy} gives
\[
 Y(t)+1\leq C_\eta(-t)^{-C_*\eta}.
\]
Here $C_*$ was fixed before $\eta$. Choose $\eta$ with $2C_*\eta<1$.
Local Sobolev now places $u$ in $L^4_tL^6_x$ on a smaller cylinder.
The local criterion of \cite[Theorem~1.1(i)]{GKT}, applied on backward
cylinders ending at zero, makes $(0,0)$ regular. This finishes the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{A separate quantitative gradient-history estimate}
\label{sec:smoothing}

Regularity in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} requires only \eqref{eq:history}.
For applications which additionally require an absolute gradient or
torque history, the actual defect equation gives the following stronger
estimate. The whole-space assumptions here control the pressure produced
by localization; they are not assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:amplitude}
Let $u$ be a smooth unforced solution on $\R^3\times(-1,0)$ with
$u\in L^\infty((-1,0);L^2(\R^3))$ and canonical pressure, whose
restriction to $Q$ satisfies the local suitability assumptions above.
For some $0<h<1/2$, assume only the following mean bounds on $B(1)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localMeanLip}
 \norm{v(-\tau)}_\infty\leq C_M\tau^{-1/2-h},\qquad
 \norm{\nabla v(-\tau)}_\infty\leq C_N\tau^{-1-h},
\end{equation}
with Cartesian derivatives. Suppose, for some $0<a<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:amplitude}
 \int_0^a \tau^{-h/2}\sqrt{\log(e/\tau)}\,
               \norm{w(-\tau)}_{L^\infty(B(1))}^2\dd\tau<\infty.
\end{equation}
Then, on a smaller ball and final time interval,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gradienthistory}
 \int\sqrt{-t}\,\norm{w(t)}_\infty\norm{\nabla w(t)}_\infty\dd t<\infty.
\end{equation}
It also bounds the same weighted integral of
$\norm{(\PP[(w\cdot\nabla)w])_\theta}_\infty$.
For this additional conclusion, the amplitude power
$\norm{w(-\tau)}_\infty\leq C\tau^{-\beta}$ suffices when
$\beta<1/2-h/4$. At equality, a factor $\log(e/\tau)^{-p}$ suffices
for $p>3/4$. If the full mean geometry of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
also holds, regularity already follows from the weaker condition
\eqref{eq:history}.
\end{theorem}

The theorem does not assert \eqref{eq:amplitude} from initial data.
Its proof has a whole-space smoothing estimate and a localization step.
On a sufficiently short final interval it bounds
\eqref{eq:gradienthistory} by a constant times the integral in
\eqref{eq:amplitude}, plus a bounded annular-source contribution.
Write $\mathbb L$ for the Leray projection, to distinguish it from
angular averaging $\PP$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:window}
Let a smooth divergence-free $U$ on $\R^3\times[0,d]$ satisfy
\[
 U_t-\Delta U=-\mathbb L\diver(U\otimes U)+g,
 \qquad V=\PP U,\quad W=U-V,\quad G=(1-\PP)g.
\]
Assume the fields and the spatial derivatives used below are bounded on
compact time prefixes, and that
$\norm V_\infty\leq M$, $\norm{\nabla V}_\infty\leq N$,
$\norm G_\infty\leq G_0$ there, with $M,N>0$.
Put $K(q)=\norm{W(q)}_\infty$, $L(q)=\norm{\nabla W(q)}_\infty$, and
\[
 Z_d=\sup_{0<q\leq d}\{K(q)+\sqrt q L(q)\},
 \qquad \log_+x=\max(\log x,0).
\]
There are universal constants such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window}
 Z_d\leq C_0(K(0)+dG_0)
 +C_1Nd\left[1+\log_+\frac{M}{N\sqrt d}\right]Z_d
 +C_2\sqrt d Z_d^2.
\end{equation}
Consequently $Z_d\leq2C_0(K(0)+dG_0)$ if the linear coefficient
is at most $1/4$ and $\sqrt d(K(0)+dG_0)$ is sufficiently small.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Rotation covariance gives the exact homogeneous-in-$W$ equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect}
 W_t-\Delta W=-\mathbb L\diver\big[V\otimes W+W\otimes V
                          +(1-\PP)(W\otimes W)\big]+G.
\end{equation}
In particular the complete defect self-interaction is retained.
For the heat kernel $H_s$, the kernel of $e^{s\Delta}\mathbb L\diver$ is
\[
 (B_s)_{ijk}=\delta_{ij}\partial_k H_s
                         +\int_s^\infty\partial_{ijk}H_b\dd b.
\]
Differentiating the Gaussian and integrating in $b$ gives
\begin{align*}
 |B_s(y)|&\leq C(\sqrt s+|y|)^{-4},&
 |\nabla B_s(y)|&\leq C(\sqrt s+|y|)^{-5},\\
 \norm{B_s}_1&\leq C s^{-1/2},&
 \int |y|\,|\nabla B_s(y)|\dd y&\leq C s^{-1/2}.
\end{align*}
The first absolute moment of $B_s$ is not integrable at infinity.
Splitting the radial integral at $\sqrt s$ and $M/N$ instead gives
\begin{align}
 \int |B_s(y)|\min(N|y|,2M)\dd y
 &\leq CN\left[1+\log_+\frac{M}{N\sqrt s}\right],\label{eq:oseenlog}\\
 \int |\nabla B_s(y)|\min(N|y|,2M)\dd y
 &\leq CNs^{-1/2}.\label{eq:oseengrad}
\end{align}

For each endpoint $(x,q)$ follow the backward characteristic
$z'(s)=V(z(s),s)$, $z(q)=x$, and set $a(s)=V(z(s),s)$.
Move $a(s)\cdot\nabla W$ to the left side of \eqref{eq:defect};
its two linear tensors now contain $V-a$ instead of $V$, since
$\diver W=0$. This identity holds everywhere with this one
spatially constant $a(s)$. The translated Duhamel kernels at the
chosen endpoint are centered at $z(s)$, where
\[
 |V(y,s)-a(s)|\leq\min(N|y-z(s)|,2M).
\]
For the gradient estimate, differentiate that representation with
$a(s)$ held fixed. No derivative of the selected characteristic is
introduced: the same representation is valid at every nearby endpoint
with this fixed $a$. Estimates \eqref{eq:oseenlog}--\eqref{eq:oseengrad}
bound the linear amplitude and gradient terms.

For the quadratic gradient term move the derivative onto the tensor:
$\norm{\nabla(1-\PP)(W\otimes W)}_\infty\leq CKL$.
Angular averaging and its tensor action are contractions in the
Euclidean sup norms. The time integral
$\int_0^q(q-s)^{-1/2}s^{-1/2}\dd s=\pi$ then bounds this term
by $CZ_d^2$, before multiplication by $\sqrt q$.
The quadratic amplitude term is bounded by $C\sqrt q Z_d^2$.
The initial term and the divergence-free source use the ordinary
translated heat kernel. Finally
\[
 \int_0^q\left[1+\log_+\frac{M}{N\sqrt s}\right]\dd s
 \leq Cq\left[1+\log_+\frac{M}{N\sqrt q}\right].
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:window}. The estimate holds on every shorter
prefix, whose linear coefficient is no larger. A continuity bootstrap
gives the final claim; smoothness supplies the initial continuity.
If $K(0)+dG_0=0$, use uniqueness or a positive bootstrap bound tending
to zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:amplitude}]
First suppose a globally bounded defect equation as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:window} has been obtained, with $G$ uniformly bounded and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:meanlip}
 \norm{V(-\tau)}_\infty\leq C_M\tau^{-1/2-h},\qquad
 \norm{\nabla V(-\tau)}_\infty\leq C_N\tau^{-1-h}.
\end{equation}
Let $\ell(\tau)=\log(e/\tau)$ and choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dwindow}
 d(\tau)=\frac{c\tau^{1+h}}{\ell(\tau)},\qquad 0<c<1.
\end{equation}
On $[-\tau-d,-\tau]$, the linear coefficient in
\eqref{eq:window} is at most $C c(1+|\log c|+h)$: here
$Nd\leq C_Nc/\ell$ and $M/(N\sqrt d)$ is bounded by a
constant times $c^{-1/2}\tau^{-h/2}\sqrt\ell$.
Take $c$ small enough in terms of the mean bounds.

Write $K=\norm W_\infty$, $L=\norm{\nabla W}_\infty$, and
\[
 E(\tau)=\int_\tau^{\tau+d(\tau)} K(-s)^2\dd s.
\]
Condition \eqref{eq:amplitude}, with the localized global norm,
implies $K\in L^2_t$ and $E(\tau)\to0$.
There is a time $s_0\in[-\tau-d,-\tau-d/2]$ at which
$K(s_0)^2\leq2E(\tau)/d$.
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:window} on $[s_0,-\tau]$ is therefore
legitimate for all sufficiently small $\tau$; its small parameter is
at most $C\sqrt{E(\tau)}+d^{3/2}G_0\to0$.
We obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smoothing}
 K(-\tau)+\sqrt d L(-\tau)
 \leq C\big[d^{-1/2}\sqrt{E(\tau)}+dG_0\big].
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product}
 \sqrt\tau K(-\tau)L(-\tau)
 \leq C\sqrt\tau d^{-3/2}E(\tau)
                         +C\sqrt\tau d^{3/2}G_0^2.
\end{equation}
The second term is integrable. For the first, Tonelli gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tonelliwindow}
 \int_0^{a/2}\sqrt\tau d(\tau)^{-3/2}E(\tau)\dd\tau
 \leq C\int_0^a s^{-h/2}\sqrt{\ell(s)}K(-s)^2\dd s.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\tau\leq s\leq\tau+d(\tau)$ implies
$s/(1+c)\leq\tau\leq s$. Since $d$ increases, these $\tau$ lie
in an interval of length at most $d(s)$, while the integrand's
coefficient is at most $Cs^{-1-3h/2}\ell(s)^{3/2}$.
This proves \eqref{eq:tonelliwindow}, hence the gradient history on a
final interval. The angularly averaged torque is at most $KL$.

It remains to construct the localization used above. Choose a radial
cutoff $\chi$, equal to one on an interior ball and with its transition
inside a regular annulus of the original solution. Put
\[
 \Psi=(-\Delta)^{-1}\curl u,\qquad
 U=\curl(\chi\Psi)=\chi u+b,\qquad b=\nabla\chi\times\Psi.
\]
The $L^2$ hypothesis and incompressibility give $\curl\Psi=u$;
the potential is well defined by its order-minus-one kernel.
On the annulus, its separated kernel and every required derivative
are bounded against $\norm u_2$, while its local part is controlled
by interior spatial regularity. For canonical pressure, the same
decomposition for its order-zero transform of $u\otimes u$ bounds
$p$ and its spatial derivatives on the annulus. Likewise
\[
 \Psi_t=-\curl u-(-\Delta)^{-1}\curl\diver(u\otimes u)
\]
has bounded spatial derivatives there: its bilinear operator is of
order zero, with separated kernels controlled by $\norm u_2^2$.
These statements use only annular regularity and the uniform energy
bound, not regularity of the interior through time zero.

With pressure $\chi p$, the exact force of $U$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoffforce}
\begin{split}
 f_U={}&b_t-2\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla u-(\Delta\chi)u-\Delta b
                       +p\nabla\chi\\
      &+(U\cdot\nabla)U-\chi(u\cdot\nabla)u.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
It is supported in the regular annulus and uniformly bounded in
spatial $C^1$. Its Leray projection is uniformly bounded, as follows
by subtracting $f_U(x)$ in the local principal value and using its
$L^1$ norm for the far part. Thus the equation for $U$ has
$g=\mathbb L f_U$ and bounded $G=(1-\PP)g$, including pressure.

Rotations preserve the interior ball, so $U=u$, $\PP U=v$, and
$U-\PP U=w$ there. The transition region contributes bounded fields
and derivatives; outside the cutoff $U=0$. The mean assumptions
therefore imply \eqref{eq:meanlip} globally. The global weighted amplitude integral
is bounded by \eqref{eq:amplitude} plus the finite contribution of a
bounded function. Equations \eqref{eq:smoothing}--\eqref{eq:tonelliwindow}
now supply \eqref{eq:gradienthistory} on a smaller cylinder of the
original flow. The power and
logarithmic consequences follow by integrating
$\tau^{-h/2-2\beta}\ell(\tau)^{1/2-2p}$.
\end{proof}

\section{The sign of kinetic mean--defect transfer}

For a smooth finite-energy whole-space unforced flow, orthogonality of
angular averaging and integration by parts give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kinetictransfer}
 \frac12\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\norm w_2^2
       +\nu\norm{\nabla w}_2^2=-T,
 \qquad
 \frac12\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\norm v_2^2
       +\nu\norm{\nabla v}_2^2=T,
 \quad T=\int w\cdot(w\cdot\nabla)v.
\end{equation}
We display viscosity explicitly in this section. Defect self-advection
and pressure do no kinetic work on $w$; the averaging correction is
orthogonal to $w$. In particular the signed net kinetic transfer obeys
\[
 -\int_0^t T(s)\dd s
 =\tfrac12\norm{v(0)}_2^2-\tfrac12\norm{v(t)}_2^2
                         -\nu\int_0^t\norm{\nabla v}_2^2\dd s
 \leq\tfrac12\norm{v(0)}_2^2.
\]
This does not bound its positive variation or the critical amplitude
integral \eqref{eq:history}. The following example also excludes an automatic
viscous damping argument based just on small relative angular defect.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:transfer}
For every $\nu>0$, $C>0$, and $0<\epsilon\leq1$, there are real
Schwartz solenoidal data $u_+$ and $u_-$ with $\norm{u_\pm}_2=1$
and the same angular mean $v$. Their defects $w_\pm=u_\pm-v$ have
equal squared $L^2$ and gradient norms, and
$\norm{w_\pm}_2=(3\epsilon/2)\norm v_2$.
Their nonlinear kinetic transfers have opposite signs, and the
unforced solution from $u_+$ satisfies at its initial time
\[
 \frac12\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\norm{w_+(t)}_2^2
                         >C\nu\norm{\nabla w_+}_2^2.
\]
The mean satisfies the spatial bounds
\eqref{eq:meanr}--\eqref{eq:meanz} at a time $t=-\tau_\lambda$,
with $h=1/6$ and a constant independent of the concentration parameter
$\lambda$ and of $\epsilon\in(0,1]$.
This last assertion concerns that time slice, not an entire evolution.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Set $\rho=x^2+y^2+z^2$, $r^2=x^2+y^2$, and, for $\sigma=\pm1$,
\[
 V=\curl(e^{-\rho}e_z),\qquad
 W_\sigma=\curl\big((x^2-y^2+2\sigma xy r^2)e^{-\rho}e_z\big).
\]
Both fields are horizontal, divergence free and Schwartz;
$\PP V=V$ and $\PP W_\sigma=0$. Write $g_a=(\pi/a)^{3/2}$.
Gaussian moments give
\begin{align*}
 \norm V_2^2&=2g_2,& \norm{W_\sigma}_2^2&=\tfrac92 g_2,\\
 \norm{\nabla_h W_\sigma}_2^2&=30g_2,&
 \norm{\partial_z W_\sigma}_2^2&=\tfrac92 g_2,\\
 \int W_\sigma\cdot(W_\sigma\cdot\nabla)V
       &=-\sigma\tfrac{16}{9}g_3.
\end{align*}
For example, the radial--azimuthal entry of
$\PP(W_\sigma\otimes W_\sigma)$ is
$2\sigma r^4e^{-2\rho}$, while
$\partial_r V_\theta-V_\theta/r=-4r^2e^{-\rho}$;
their product directly gives the last integral.

Let $\mu=\lambda^{2/3}$, $\lambda\geq1$, and put
\[
 C_\epsilon=g_2(2+\tfrac92\epsilon^2),\qquad
 A=\frac{\lambda\sqrt\mu}{\sqrt{C_\epsilon}},\qquad
 u_\sigma(x,y,z)=A(V+\epsilon W_\sigma)(\lambda x,\lambda y,\mu z).
\]
The absence of an axial component preserves incompressibility under
this anisotropic scaling. Orthogonality proves the stated unit norm
and relative defect norm. The positive nonlinear injection $I_+=-T_+$
and its viscous cost $\nu D_+$ obey
\[
 \frac{I_+}{\nu D_+}
 =\frac{A\lambda(16g_3/9)}
        {\nu g_2(30\lambda^2+\tfrac92\mu^2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Indeed this ratio grows as a positive constant times
$\lambda^{1/3}/\nu$, with $\epsilon$ fixed. Choose it larger than
$C+1$ and apply \eqref{eq:kinetictransfer}. For $\sigma=-1$ the
injection changes sign while the dissipation is unchanged. These are
jets of actual local unforced solutions from Schwartz data; no imposed
strain or truncated source is used. Continuity also gives a nonzero
initial interval with the strict inequality, but no uniform length
of that interval is asserted.

Finally take $\tau_\lambda=\lambda^{-2}$. The mean has only an
azimuthal component, whose $(a,b)$ derivative is bounded by
\[
 C_{ab}\lambda^{4/3+a+(2/3)b}
 =C_{ab}\tau_\lambda^{-1/2-1/6-a/2-(1/2-1/6)b}.
\]
The constants are uniform for $0<\epsilon\leq1$ because
$C_\epsilon\geq2g_2$. This proves the asserted spatial bounds.
\end{proof}

The example refutes zero-allowance viscous absorption of kinetic
mean--defect transfer, even with small relative defect and the stated
spatial geometry. It does not refute a data-dependent history allowance,
control of repeated interactions, or Theorems~\ref{thm:main}
and~\ref{thm:amplitude}. In particular these transfers are kinetic
energy transfers, not vorticity stretching integrals.

There is also an exact circulation test. The initial mean circulation
$\Gamma=r v_\theta$ has its global maximum at $r=1/\lambda$, $z=0$,
with value $2A/(e\lambda)$. For the base Gaussian defect,
\[
 rF_\theta=4\sigma r^4(2r^2-3)e^{-2\rho},
 \qquad F=-\PP[(W_\sigma\cdot\nabla)W_\sigma].
\]
At that maximum the radial and axial diffusion of the base circulation
are $-8/e$ and $-4/e$. Consequently the actual initial mean equation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussiancirculation}
 \Gamma_t(1/\lambda,0)
 =-\frac{4\nu A}{e\lambda}(2\lambda^2+\mu^2)
                         -\frac{4\sigma\epsilon^2A^2}{e^2}.
\end{equation}
For $\sigma=-1$, choose $\lambda$ still larger so that
$\epsilon^2 A/(\nu\lambda)>e[2+(\mu/\lambda)^2]$.
The value at the old maximum then increases initially, and hence exceeds
the initial global maximum. Its energy comes from the defect in
\eqref{eq:kinetictransfer}. Thus the maximum principle for exactly
axisymmetric circulation cannot be extended by dropping the Reynolds
torque, even for arbitrarily small relative angular defect. This is an
initial growth example, not repeated spin-up or singularity formation.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:inwardwindow}
There are constants $c_0,c_1,c_2,C_0>0$ with the following property.
Fix $\nu>0$, $0<\epsilon\leq1$, and $0<h\leq1/6$. Set
\[
 \mu=\lambda^{1-2h},\qquad A=\lambda^{1+2h}/\sqrt{C_\epsilon},
 \qquad u_\lambda(x_h,z)=A(V+\epsilon W_-)(\lambda x_h,\mu z).
\]
For every sufficiently large $\lambda$, the actual unforced solution
from $u_\lambda$ exists at least until
\[
 t_\lambda=\frac{c_0}{A\lambda}.
\]
These data have $\norm{u_\lambda}_2^2=\lambda^{-1+6h}\leq1$
and relative $L^2$ defect $3\epsilon/2$. For $h=1/6$ they are the
unit-energy data $u_-$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}.
Its mean circulation has a smooth branch of local maxima in the
meridional variables, at $(r,z)=(r_\lambda(t),0)$, starting at
$(\lambda^{-1},0)$. Along this branch the radius strictly decreases
and the circulation strictly increases. At the last time,
\begin{align}
 r_\lambda(t_\lambda)&\leq\lambda^{-1}(1-c_1\epsilon^2),
 \label{eq:inwardradius}\\
 \Gamma(r_\lambda(t_\lambda),0,t_\lambda)
 &\geq\frac{2A}{e\lambda}(1+c_1\epsilon^2).
 \label{eq:inwardgain}
\end{align}
On this whole interval the mean satisfies
\eqref{eq:meanr}--\eqref{eq:meanz} with this $h$, constant $C_0$,
and $\tau=\lambda^{-2}-t>0$. Furthermore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actualhistorylower}
 \int_0^{t_\lambda}\norm{u(t)-\PP u(t)}_{L^\infty(B(1))}^2\dd t
                      \geq c_2\epsilon^2\lambda^{2h}.
\end{equation}
All constants are independent of $\nu,\epsilon,h,\lambda$; the required
lower threshold on $\lambda$ may depend on $\nu,\epsilon,h$.
The number $\lambda^{-2}$ specifies a comparison scale, not an asserted
singular time.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $y_h=\lambda x_h$, $Z=\mu z$, $s=A\lambda t$,
$U=u/A$, and
\[
 \delta=\mu/\lambda=\lambda^{-2h},\qquad
 \eta=\nu\lambda/A,\qquad
 D_\delta=(\partial_{y_1},\partial_{y_2},\delta\partial_Z),\qquad
 \Delta_\delta=\partial_{y_1y_1}+\partial_{y_2y_2}
                                      +\delta^2\partial_{ZZ}.
\]
The full projected equation, including pressure, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anisotropicNS}
 U_s=-\mathbb P_\delta[(U\cdot D_\delta)U]
                  +\eta\Delta_\delta U,\qquad
 D_\delta\cdot U=0,
\end{equation}
with initial field $V+\epsilon W_-$. Here $\mathbb P_\delta$ has
Fourier multiplier $I-k_\delta\otimes k_\delta/|k_\delta|^2$,
$k_\delta=(k_1,k_2,\delta k_3)$. It is an orthogonal projection of
norm at most one in every isotropic Sobolev space and commutes with
derivatives. No estimate of an inverse power of $\delta$ is used.

For $0<\delta\leq1$ and $0\leq\eta\leq1$, differentiated energy
estimates for \eqref{eq:anisotropicNS} give
\[
 \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm ds}\norm U_{H^{10}}^2
                         \leq C\norm U_{H^{10}}^3.
\]
Indeed the leading transport cancels by $D_\delta\cdot U=0$,
the commutators have the usual Sobolev product bounds with
$|k_\delta|\leq|k|$, and the diffusion is nonpositive in the energy.
Friedrichs regularization, this estimate, and the corresponding
difference estimate construct a common interval $[0,s_0]$ with
$\norm U_{H^{10}}\leq M$, uniformly in these parameters and
$0<\epsilon\leq1$. The initial norm is bounded by
$\norm V_{H^{10}}+\norm{W_-}_{H^{10}}$.
Higher derivative estimates propagate smoothness on that interval.
The equation also bounds $U_s$ in $H^8$ and, after one time
derivative, $U_{ss}$ in $H^6$, uniformly. Sobolev embedding therefore
gives uniform spatial bounds for the needed time derivatives.

The fluctuation $w^*=U-\PP U$ satisfies the exact equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaledDefect}
 w^*_s=-\mathbb P_\delta\big[(v^*\cdot D_\delta)w^*
       +(w^*\cdot D_\delta)v^*
       +(1-\PP)((w^*\cdot D_\delta)w^*)\big]
       +\eta\Delta_\delta w^*,\qquad v^*=\PP U.
\end{equation}
Angular averaging is an orthogonal projection in isotropic $H^8$,
commutes with the isotropic Sobolev multiplier and with
$\mathbb P_\delta$, and annihilates $w^*$. Thus the mean correction
in the quadratic term does no $H^8$ work on $w^*$.
Transport cancellation and product bounds, using the uniform $H^{10}$
bound on $U$, give
\[
 \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm ds}\norm{w^*}_{H^8}^2
        \leq C_M\norm{w^*}_{H^8}^2,\qquad
 \norm{w^*}_{H^8}+\norm{w^*_s}_{H^6}\leq C\epsilon
                         \quad(0\leq s\leq s_0).
\]
The time-derivative estimate follows directly from
\eqref{eq:scaledDefect}. In particular the stress
$\PP(w^*\otimes w^*)$ and its time derivative have spatial $C^3$
norm at most $C\epsilon^2$ on any fixed annulus.

Let $\widetilde\Gamma=R(\PP U)_\theta$, where $R=|y_h|$.
Initially $\widetilde\Gamma=2R^2e^{-R^2-Z^2}$. At $(1,0)$ its
meridional Hessian is diagonal with entries $-8/e$ and $-4/e$.
The actual mean equation and the Gaussian torque above give exactly
\begin{align*}
 \widetilde\Gamma_s(1,0,0)
       &=4\epsilon^2/e^2-4\eta(2+\delta^2)/e,\\
 \widetilde\Gamma_{sR}(1,0,0)
       &=-16\epsilon^2/e^2+16\eta/e.
\end{align*}
Reflection in $Z$, with the axial velocity component reflected too,
is a symmetry of \eqref{eq:anisotropicNS} and its initial data.
Uniqueness keeps $\widetilde\Gamma$ even in $Z$.
The implicit function theorem and the uniform derivative bounds
therefore give a uniform short interval with a nearby meridional
local maximum at $(R_*(s),0)$, $R_*(0)=1$. Its Hessian remains
negative definite, with $|\widetilde\Gamma_{RR}|\leq12/e$ there.

Axisymmetry and the mean's actual divergence constraint give
\[
 v^*_r(R,Z)=-\frac\delta R\int_0^R q\partial_Zv^*_z(q,Z)\dd q.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and the $H^1_Z$ trace bound into $L^2_{y_h}$
bound this by $C\delta$ uniformly in space. With
$f=\partial_Zv^*_z$ its first two radial derivatives are
\[
 \partial_Rv^*_r=-\delta f(R)+\delta\int_0^1qf(qR)\dd q,
 \qquad
 \partial_{RR}v^*_r=-\delta f_R(R)
                              +\delta\int_0^1q^2f_R(qR)\dd q.
\]
The same estimates after axial derivatives and the uniform Sobolev
bounds give $|\partial_R^a\partial_Z^b v^*_r|\leq C\delta$
for $a+b\leq2$. The other two components have uniformly bounded
cylindrical derivatives of these orders. Applying the same argument
to $v^*_s$, bounded in $H^8$, also gives an $O(\delta)$ bound on
the first two derivatives of $v^*_{r,s}$.

On the fixed rectangle about $(1,0)$, the mean circulation equation is
\[
 \widetilde\Gamma_s+v^*_r\widetilde\Gamma_R
        +\delta v^*_z\widetilde\Gamma_Z
 =\eta(\widetilde\Gamma_{RR}-R^{-1}\widetilde\Gamma_R
                     +\delta^2\widetilde\Gamma_{ZZ})
       -R e_R-2e-\delta R f_Z,
\]
where $e=\PP(w^*_rw^*_\theta)$ and $f=\PP(w^*_zw^*_\theta)$.
The preceding bounds, and then one time derivative of this equation,
give
\[
 \norm{\widetilde\Gamma_s}_{C^2}
       +\norm{\widetilde\Gamma_{ss}}_{C^1}
                         \leq C(\delta+\eta+\epsilon^2).
\]
For the diffusive term after that derivative, the uniform $H^8$ bound
on $U_s$ supplies the required derivatives of
$\widetilde\Gamma_s$. Hence, if $\lambda$ is large enough that
$\delta+\eta\leq\epsilon^2/(12e)$, the two displayed initial rates,
evaluated along the maximum branch, change by at most $C\epsilon^2s$.
After reducing a universal $c_0>0$, for every $0\leq s\leq c_0$,
\[
 \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm ds}
       \widetilde\Gamma(R_*(s),0,s)\geq2\epsilon^2/e^2,
 \qquad
 R_*'(s)=-\frac{\widetilde\Gamma_{sR}}
                    {\widetilde\Gamma_{RR}}
                              \leq-\frac{2\epsilon^2}{3e}.
\]
Integrating and using $r_\lambda=R_*/\lambda$ and
$\Gamma=(A/\lambda)\widetilde\Gamma$ proves
\eqref{eq:inwardradius}--\eqref{eq:inwardgain} for some universal
$c_1>0$. The branch starts at the global circulation maximum and
its value exceeds that initial maximum. It need not be identified
with the global maximizer at later times.

For the stated physical mean geometry, the radial estimates and
$A\delta=\lambda/\sqrt{C_\epsilon}$ give exactly the
claimed mean bounds at scale $\tau_\lambda=\lambda^{-2}$.
For large $\lambda$, $t_\lambda<\tau_\lambda/2$; using
$\tau_\lambda-t$ only enlarges their right-hand sides.

Finally $W_-(1,0,0)=(-2/e,0,0)$ and the bound on $w^*_s$ gives
\[
 \norm{U(s)-\PP U(s)-\epsilon W_-}_\infty\leq C\epsilon s.
\]
Reduce $c_0$ once more so that the defect at this fixed point has
magnitude at least $\epsilon/e$ throughout the interval.
Its physical point $(\lambda^{-1},0,0)$ belongs to $B(1)$.
Consequently the integral in \eqref{eq:actualhistorylower} is at least
\[
 \frac{A^2\epsilon^2}{e^2}\,
       \frac{c_0}{A\lambda}
 =\frac{c_0\epsilon^2}{e^2\sqrt{C_\epsilon}}\lambda^{2h}.
\]
The uniform upper bound on $C_\epsilon$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

This is a finite concentrating event in the actual unforced equation,
with all nonlinear feedback retained. It excludes a rule that such
feedback always prevents inward motion and growth of a mean circulation
peak. It also excludes a bound on the critical defect history depending
only on kinetic energy, viscosity and an upper bound for the length of
the smooth interval: the interval here tends to zero, whereas
\eqref{eq:actualhistorylower} tends to infinity for fixed $\epsilon$.
The initial higher norms grow with $\lambda$. No infinite cascade from
one fixed datum, data-dependent history obstruction, or singularity
follows from this family.

\subsection{Fast axial seeds and the mean geometry}

The preceding example has slow axial variation in both its mean and its
defect. Fast axial oscillations need not remove its finite inward growth.
Their phase relation matters, however, for the regularity of the angular
mean. In cylindrical coordinates write
\[
 \Psi_\alpha(R,\phi,Z)=e^{-R^2-Z^2}
       \big[R^2\cos(2\phi+\alpha)-R^4\sin(2\phi+\alpha)\big],
 \qquad W_\alpha=\curl_h(\Psi_\alpha e_z).
\]
Here $\curl_h$ takes only the two horizontal derivatives and has zero
axial component. These expressions extend smoothly across $R=0$;
$W_0=W_-$. Two defects with a phase variable $\alpha$ are
\[
 W^{\rm st}(y_h,Z,\alpha)=W_0(y_h,Z)\cos\alpha,
 \qquad W^{\rm hel}(y_h,Z,\alpha)=W_\alpha(y_h,Z).
\]
The first is a standing axial wave, while the second rotates its
horizontal pattern with its axial phase.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fastseeds}
Fix $\nu>0$, $0<\epsilon\leq1$, and $0<h\leq1/6$. With
$\delta=\lambda^{-2h}$, $\mu=\lambda^{1-2h}$ and
$A=\lambda^{1+2h}/\sqrt{C_\epsilon}$ as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:inwardwindow}, set
\[
 u^j_\lambda(x_h,z)
   =A\big[V(\lambda x_h,\mu z)
       +\epsilon W^j(\lambda x_h,\mu z,\lambda z)\big],
 \qquad j\in\{{\rm st},{\rm hel}\}.
\]
Both are real Schwartz solenoidal data, with the same angular mean,
squared energy at most one and relative $L^2$ defect at most
$3\epsilon/2$. Their initial means obey
\eqref{eq:meanr}--\eqref{eq:meanz} with $\tau=\lambda^{-2}$ and
a constant independent of $\lambda$ and $\epsilon$.

For all sufficiently large $\lambda$, the helical data have all the
conclusions of Proposition~\ref{prop:inwardwindow}, with universal
constants, except that the maximum branch may also move axially:
it is $(r_\lambda(t),z_\lambda(t))$, initially $(\lambda^{-1},0)$.
In particular the mean geometry persists on $0\leq t\leq c_0/(A\lambda)$,
the branch moves inward and gains circulation by relative amounts
at least $c_1\epsilon^2$, and its actual defect history is at least
$c_2\epsilon^2\lambda^{2h}$. Its squared energy is exactly one
when $h=1/6$.

For the standing data, there instead exist universal $c_3,c_4>0$
such that, at $t_*=c_3\epsilon^2/(A\lambda)$ and
$\tau_* =\lambda^{-2}-t_*>0$, the actual unforced solution satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fastMeanCurvature}
 \tau_*^{3/2-h}
 \left|\partial_{zz}(\PP u^{\rm st}_\lambda)_\theta
                         (\lambda^{-1},0,t_*)\right|
                        \geq c_4\epsilon^4\lambda^{4h}.
\end{equation}
Thus initial mean geometry, a bounded kinetic energy and a small relative
defect alone do not propagate these mean bounds with a uniform constant.
All thresholds on $\lambda$ may depend on $\nu,\epsilon,h$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Introduce an independent periodic variable
$\alpha\in\R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$ and solve on
$\R^2\times\R\times(\R/(2\pi\mathbb Z))$ with
\[
 D_{\delta,*}=(\partial_1,\partial_2,
                   \delta\partial_Z+\partial_\alpha),\qquad
 \eta=\nu\lambda/A,
\]
\[
 U_s=-\mathbb P_{\delta,*}[(U\cdot D_{\delta,*})U]
                 +\eta\Delta_{\delta,*}U,\qquad
 D_{\delta,*}\cdot U=0,
 \quad U(0)=V+\epsilon W^j.
\]
The projection has symbol
$I-\kappa\otimes\kappa/|\kappa|^2$, where
$\kappa=(k_1,k_2,\delta k_Z+n)$ and $n\in\mathbb Z$ is the
phase frequency. It has norm at most one in every Sobolev space.
The four-dimensional transport field
$(U_1,U_2,\delta U_3,U_3)$ is divergence free. Thus the same
transport cancellation, commutator estimates and nonpositive diffusion
as before give a common smooth interval with a uniform $H^{12}$ bound,
for $0<\delta\leq1$, $0\leq\eta\leq1$, and $0<\epsilon\leq1$.
The first three time derivatives are bounded in $H^{10},H^8,H^6$,
respectively. These estimates use the full phase derivative, not a
truncation of the generated modes.

Restrict this solution to $\alpha=Z/\delta$.
The restricted axial derivative $\delta\partial_Z$ is exactly
$\delta\partial_Z+\partial_\alpha$ before restriction. Hence the
restriction solves the actual three-dimensional projected equation,
with its pressure, from the stated initial datum. The elementary
$H^1$ bound in the periodic variable controls this restriction in
$L^2_{y_h,Z}$; applying it to derivatives gives smoothness and finite
Sobolev norms for each fixed $\delta>0$. The pressure has the same
property, so this is the finite-energy unforced evolution, by uniqueness.
Rescaling $y_h=\lambda x_h$, $Z=\mu z$, and $s=A\lambda t$
gives the physical flow. In particular its phase is $\lambda z$.

For the helical field the extended initial datum obeys the joint
symmetry
\[
 U(R_\beta y_h,Z,\alpha-2\beta)
                  =R_\beta U(y_h,Z,\alpha),
\]
where the rotation fixes the third velocity component. The equation
commutes with this action, so uniqueness preserves it. Angular
averaging therefore makes $v^*=\PP U$ independent of $\alpha$
throughout the interval. This is a nonlinear symmetry assertion, not
only a cancellation of the initial quadratic source.
Consequently the physical mean's axial derivatives involve only its
slow $Z$ derivatives, which are uniformly bounded. Its divergence
constraint is
\[
 \partial_Rv^*_r+R^{-1}v^*_r+\delta\partial_Zv^*_z=0.
\]
The radial primitive and slice estimates in the preceding proof give
the required $O(\delta)$ radial gain. All the mean bounds thus transfer
to the physical scale exactly as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:inwardwindow}.

Angular averaging is orthogonal in the extended isotropic Sobolev
spaces too. The exact homogeneous fluctuation equation therefore gives
$\norm{U-\PP U}_{H^8}+\norm{\partial_s(U-\PP U)}_{H^6}
\leq C\epsilon$. Its stress and the time derivative of that stress
are $O(\epsilon^2)$ in spatial $C^3$ on a fixed annulus.
Direct angular integration gives
\begin{align}
 \PP(W^{\rm st}_rW^{\rm st}_\theta)
     &=-2R^4e^{-2R^2-2Z^2}\cos^2\alpha,\label{eq:standingstress}\\
 \PP(W^{\rm hel}_rW^{\rm hel}_\theta)
     &=-2R^4e^{-2R^2-2Z^2}.\label{eq:helicalstress}
\end{align}
The latter gives the same initial circulation and radial rates as
in the preceding proof, and the initial axial rate of the critical
point is zero. The mean equation again bounds its circulation time
derivatives by $C(\delta+\eta+\epsilon^2)$.
Use the two-variable implicit function theorem for the negative
definite meridional Hessian. The initial inward radial rate and positive
circulation rate persist on a universal interval when
$\delta+\eta$ is sufficiently small compared with $\epsilon^2$.
This proves the assertions about the moving critical point without
assuming that its axial coordinate stays zero. At the fixed point
$(y_h,Z,\alpha)=((1,0),0,0)$ the initial defect is
$\epsilon(-2/e,0,0)$. Its uniform $O(\epsilon)$ time-derivative bound
proves the same critical-history lower bound.

For the standing field put $G=R(\PP U)_\theta$ and
$J=\delta\partial_Z+\partial_\alpha$.
Initially the mean has no meridional component, so its exact equation is
\[
 G_s=\eta(G_{RR}-R^{-1}G_R+J^2G)
        +4\epsilon^2R^4(3-2R^2)e^{-2R^2-2Z^2}\cos^2\alpha
                          \quad\hbox{at }s=0.
\]
At $(R,Z,\alpha)=(1,0,0)$ define $F(s)=J^2G(1,0,0,s)$.
The two exact initial values are
\[
 F(0)=-4\delta^2/e,\qquad
 F'(0)=\eta\delta^2(16+24\delta^2)/e
                     -\epsilon^2(16\delta^2+8)/e^2.
\]
The uniform time and space bounds above give $|F''(s)|\leq C$.
For sufficiently small $\delta$, $F'(0)\leq-4\epsilon^2/e^2$.
Choosing a universal small $c_3>0$, Taylor's theorem at
$s_*=c_3\epsilon^2$ gives $F(s_*)\leq-c\epsilon^4$.
On restriction and physical rescaling,
\[
 \partial_{zz}(\PP u^{\rm st}_\lambda)_\theta
                 (\lambda^{-1},0,t_*)=A\lambda^2F(s_*).
\]
Since $\tau_*\geq\lambda^{-2}/2$ for large $\lambda$, this is
\eqref{eq:fastMeanCurvature}.

Finally angular integration makes the helical defect's squared norm
independent of its phase; it is $\tfrac92 g_2$ before the factor
$\epsilon^2$. The standing defect's squared norm is instead
\[
 \tfrac94 g_2(1+e^{-1/(2\delta^2)}).
\]
Both are orthogonal to $V$. This proves the energy and relative-defect
claims, while the common initial mean is $A V$.
\end{proof}

The standing example disproves a uniform propagation claim based only
on the displayed initial controls. It does not disprove a bound depending
on the full initial datum. The helical example shows why an argument
cannot assume that fast axial oscillations must destroy the mean geometry.
Its exact phase symmetry concerns one family; superposing families with
different phase relations need not preserve it. The uniform Sobolev
control proved here still ends after a fixed normalized time. Neither
example establishes a recurrent cascade or an arbitrary-data history bound.

\subsection{A mean mode generated by incompatible parents}

Two individually helical parents need not share a helical symmetry.
Their interaction can create a fast mean mode even when their initial
mean stress has no fast axial phase. The next example keeps that mode
as a physical Fourier projection of the actual solution.

Fix a real even Schwartz function $g$ whose Fourier transform is smooth,
nonnegative, supported in $[-1,1]$, and has nonzero mass away from zero.
Normalize $g(0)=1$; then $g''(0)<0$.
In the horizontal variables put $q=y_1+i y_2$, $a=|y_h|^2$, and
\[
 V_h=\curl_h(e^{-a}e_z),\qquad
 W_2=\curl_h(q^2e^{-a}e_z),\qquad
 W_4=\curl_h\big(q^4(1-2a/5)e^{-a}e_z\big).
\]
The last two velocities are complex; their real parts will be used.
For $0<h\leq1/6$ define
\[
 \delta=\lambda^{-2h},\quad \epsilon=\lambda^{-h}=\sqrt\delta,
 \quad \mu=\lambda^{1-2h},\quad
 C_\epsilon=\pi\norm g_2^2(1+9\epsilon^2/5),\quad
 A=\lambda^{1+2h}/\sqrt{C_\epsilon}.
\]
In this subsection $C_\epsilon$ denotes this normalization. Take the
real initial datum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixedDatum}
 u_\lambda(x_h,z)=A g(\mu z)
   \left[V_h(\lambda x_h)+\epsilon\operatorname{Re}
        \big((W_2+W_4)(\lambda x_h)e^{2i\lambda z}\big)\right].
\end{equation}
Let $Q_\lambda$ be angular averaging followed by the axial Fourier
projection onto $\lambda\leq|k_z|\leq3\lambda$, with the Fourier
convention $e^{ik_z z}$. This is an orthogonal projection in $L^2$,
preserves solenoidality and commutes with the Laplacian.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mixedParents}
For each fixed $\nu>0$ and $0<h\leq1/6$, and all sufficiently large
$\lambda$, the actual unforced evolution from \eqref{eq:mixedDatum}
exists until $t_\lambda=s_*/(A\lambda)$, for a fixed $s_*>0$.
Its initial squared energy is $\lambda^{-1+6h}\leq1$, its relative
$L^2$ angular defect is $3\epsilon/\sqrt5\to0$, and its initial mean
satisfies \eqref{eq:meanr}--\eqref{eq:meanz} with a uniform constant.
On this interval its critical fluctuation history is uniformly bounded:
\[
 \int_0^{t_\lambda}\norm{u(t)-\PP u(t)}_\infty^2\dd t\leq C.
\]

Set $Y=Q_\lambda u$ and
\[
 E_Y=\tfrac12\norm{\nabla Y}_2^2,\qquad
 D_Y=\nu\norm{\Delta Y}_2^2,\qquad
 P_Y=\langle-\Delta Y,Q_\lambda\mathcal N(u)\rangle,
 \quad \mathcal N(u)=-\mathbb P[(u\cdot\nabla)u].
\]
Here $\mathbb P$ is the physical Leray projection. Exactly
$Y(0)=Y_t(0)=0$, while at the last time
\begin{align}
 c\lambda^{-1}\leq\norm{Y(t_\lambda)}_2^2
                         &\leq C\lambda^{-1},\label{eq:mixedEnergy}\\
 \int_0^{t_\lambda}P_Y(t)\dd t&\geq c\lambda,
 &\int_0^{t_\lambda}D_Y(t)\dd t
                         &\leq C\nu\lambda^{1-2h}.
 \label{eq:mixedWork}
\end{align}
Moreover, with $\tau_\lambda=\lambda^{-2}-t_\lambda>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixedGeometry}
 \tau_\lambda^{3/2-h}
 \left|\partial_{zz}(\PP u)_\theta
       (\lambda^{-1},\pi/(2\lambda),t_\lambda)\right|
                                      \geq c\lambda^h.
\end{equation}
The constants $s_*,c,C$ depend on the fixed envelope and profiles,
not on $\lambda,\nu,h$; the lower threshold on $\lambda$ may depend
on $\nu,h$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the extended construction above, now with
$D=(\partial_1,\partial_2,\delta\partial_Z+2\partial_\alpha)$,
initial field
\[
 U(0)=g(Z)\left[V_h+\epsilon\operatorname{Re}
                       ((W_2+W_4)e^{i\alpha})\right],
\]
and $\eta=\nu\lambda/A$. Restrict to $\alpha=2Z/\delta$ and
then rescale as before. Work initially with a uniform $H^{30}$ bound
on the extended local interval. The projection remains a contraction,
with symbol corresponding to $(k_h,\delta k_Z+2n)$, so the same
existence, trace and uniqueness arguments apply.

We first compute the relevant cubic jet at $\delta=0$. Label a mode
by its horizontal angular index and its phase index $(m,n)$.
The parents have labels $(2,1),(4,1)$ and their negatives. The only
three-parent sum with label $(0,1)$ is $(2,1)+(2,1)-(4,1)$.
No single parent or pair, even including the $(0,0)$ base, reaches it.
Linear derivatives preserve these labels. Thus neither the base nor
any term containing viscosity contributes to this mode in $U_{ss}(0)$.

The two intermediate pressure equations have explicit decaying
solutions. Suppressing the common factor $g(Z)^2$, they are
\[
 p_{22}=-q^4e^{-2a},\qquad
 p_{2\bar4}=\frac{\bar q^2}{5}
                    (4a^3+18a^2-62a+9)e^{-2a}.
\]
The first solves the pressure equation with axial wave number $4$,
so its elliptic operator is $-\Delta_h+16$; the second has zero
axial wave number and operator $-\Delta_h$. Their sources are the
divergences of $(W_2\cdot D)W_2$ and
$(W_2\cdot D)\overline W_4+(\overline W_4\cdot D)W_2$,
respectively. Direct differentiation verifies both equations and
the solenoidality of their full quadratic velocity sources, including
the axial pressure component. Decay removes a harmonic ambiguity.

Insert these two sources into both orders of the outer convection.
The final pressure has zero angular index and hence no azimuthal
component. The resulting circulation coefficient is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixedCubic}
 C_\Gamma(R)=\frac85 R^4
   (8R^8-110R^6+346R^4-275R^2+27)e^{-3R^2},
 \qquad C_\Gamma(1)=-\frac{32}{5e^3}.
\end{equation}
Each real signed parent has amplitude $\epsilon/2$. Including the
conjugate output, the fast mean part of $G_{ss}(0)$, where
$G=R(\PP U)_\theta$, is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixedRealJet}
 \frac{\epsilon^3}{4}g(Z)^3 C_\Gamma(R)\cos\alpha.
\end{equation}
The zero-phase mean can have additional terms; it is not discarded
from the evolution. The lab checker verifies the two pressure
equations, every contributing convection branch, and the real-field
normalization in \eqref{eq:mixedRealJet}.

A bound uniform in the small amplitude is needed to use this jet.
For this purpose hold $\eta$ fixed while differentiating in $\epsilon$;
all estimates will be uniform for $0\leq\eta\leq1$.
If $v_j=\partial_\epsilon^j U$, its equation is the linearized
projected equation about $U$, with additional source
\[
 -\mathbb P_{\delta,*}\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\binom jk
                              (v_k\cdot D)v_{j-k}.
\]
The transport energy estimate gives uniform bounds for $v_j$ in
$H^{30-2j}$ for $j\leq3$. Difference quotients justify these
finite parameter derivatives in the lower Sobolev spaces.
Three time derivatives cost at most six additional spatial derivatives,
leaving a uniform $H^{18}$ bound for the mixed derivatives needed here.
No analyticity of the solution map in a fixed Sobolev norm is assumed.

At $\epsilon=0$, the evolving base has label $(0,0)$, so its linearized
equation preserves $(m,n)$. The first parameter derivative has only the
four parent labels, and the second only their pairwise sums. Neither
has angular index zero with nonzero phase index. Taylor's theorem in
$\epsilon$ consequently bounds the entire fast angular mean, including
its first three time derivatives in the required spatial norms, by
$C\epsilon^3$ throughout a common interval.

The physical projection $Q_\lambda$ also sees high frequencies of the
evolving zero-phase mean. Its slow axial Fourier support is not claimed
to remain compact. Six bounded $Z$ derivatives instead make this
contribution at most $C\delta^6$, also after the needed spatial and
time derivatives. Since $\epsilon=\sqrt\delta$, this is bounded by
$C\epsilon^3$. The phase trace bound transfers the other components
to the actual physical restriction.

At the initial time the support assertions are exact. The initial
zero phase has axial support at most $\mu$, its quadratic sources at
most $2\mu$, and its cubic sources at most $3\mu$. When $\delta<1/3$
these are below the receiving band. The new phases $n=\pm1$ in the
cubic jet lie in $\pm2\lambda+[-3\mu,3\mu]$, wholly inside that band.
The pressure multipliers do not enlarge axial Fourier support.
This proves $Y(0)=Y_t(0)=0$ and identifies its second derivative.

In normalized physical coordinates, let $\widetilde Y$ denote this
recipient and $D_\delta=(\partial_1,\partial_2,\delta\partial_Z)$.
The preceding bounds give, in the norm controlling up to two
$D_\delta$ derivatives,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixedTaylor}
 \widetilde Y(s)=\tfrac12\epsilon^3s^2 C_\delta
                                      +O(\epsilon^3s^3).
\end{equation}
The coefficient has uniformly bounded norms. Its phase coefficients
converge in Sobolev spaces to the nonzero coefficient just computed:
the Leray multipliers converge almost everywhere and are uniformly
bounded, so dominated convergence applies to the fixed smooth inputs.
The two physical axial bands are disjoint, and on them
$|\delta k_Z|\geq1$. It follows that both
$\norm{C_\delta}_2$ and $\norm{D_\delta C_\delta}_2$ have positive
uniform lower bounds for small $\delta$.

Choose a fixed small $s_*>0$ in \eqref{eq:mixedTaylor}. Then the
recipient's squared velocity and gradient norms at $s_*$ are bounded
above and below by constant multiples of $\epsilon^6$, and
\[
 \eta\int_0^{s_*}\norm{\Delta_\delta\widetilde Y(s)}_2^2\dd s
                                             \leq C\eta\epsilon^6.
\]
Physical squared velocity norms acquire the factor
$A^2/(\lambda^2\mu)$, and squared gradient norms acquire $A^2/\mu$.
Since $\epsilon^6=\lambda^{-6h}$, these factors yield
\eqref{eq:mixedEnergy}, a recipient enstrophy of order $\lambda$,
and the dissipation bound in \eqref{eq:mixedWork}.
The exact projected energy identity is
\[
 E_Y'(t)+D_Y(t)=P_Y(t).
\]
Since $E_Y(0)=0$, it proves the signed production lower bound without
replacing the nonlinear work by its absolute value.

For the mean geometry put $J=\delta\partial_Z+2\partial_\alpha$
and evaluate $F(s)=J^2G(1,\pi\delta/2,\pi,s)$.
Initially $F(0)$ is negative of order $\delta^2$, because $g''(0)<0$,
and $F'(0)=O(\eta\delta^2)$. Each initial parent has zero mean
radial--azimuthal stress, and their cross stress has nonzero angular
index; hence there is no initial fast mean torque. Equations
\eqref{eq:mixedCubic}--\eqref{eq:mixedRealJet} give the fast part
\[
 F''(0)=-\frac{32}{5e^3}\epsilon^3+o(\epsilon^3)
\]
as $\delta\to0$; the zero phase contributes only $O(\delta^2)$.
Moreover $|F'''|\leq C(\epsilon^3+\delta^2)$ by the phase-selection
estimate. Here $\delta^2=o(\epsilon^3)$.
After reducing the fixed $s_*$, Taylor's theorem gives
$F(s_*)\leq-c\epsilon^3$. The initial negative term absorbs the
$O(\eta\delta^2s_*)$ term uniformly for $\eta\leq1$.
The physical axial derivative equals $A\lambda^2 F(s_*)$ at the
point in \eqref{eq:mixedGeometry}. Since
$\lambda^{-2}-t_\lambda\geq\lambda^{-2}/2$ for large $\lambda$,
the normalized lower bound is
$c\epsilon^3\lambda^{4h}=c\lambda^h$.

The first parameter-derivative estimate also gives
$\norm{U-\PP U}_\infty\leq C\epsilon$ on the common interval,
since the zero-amplitude solution is angularly symmetric. Therefore
the physical fluctuation history is bounded by
$CA^2\epsilon^2/(A\lambda)
=C/\sqrt{C_\epsilon}$, uniformly in the concentration.

Finally the horizontal squared norms of $V_h$, $\operatorname{Re}W_2$
and $\operatorname{Re}W_4$ are $\pi$, $3\pi/4$ and $21\pi/20$.
Distinct angular indices make all cross energies zero. This proves
the normalization and relative-defect assertions.
\end{proof}

This receiving sector starts empty and is generated by the actual
unforced parents. Its signed production exceeds its own viscous cost
by an unbounded factor across the family, while its kinetic energy
remains small. This does not refute a signed budget for the full
high-frequency tail: the parents are in the same axial band, outside
the angular mean, and their signed work must also be included.
The initial higher norms grow with $\lambda$, so a data-dependent
history allowance and a recurrent estimate from one fixed datum
remain possible. Nor does the example prove that a possible singularity
must preserve the mean geometry assumed in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\subsection{Positive total work after including the parents}

The preceding receiving-sector calculation does not determine the sign
of the total work. A single helical parent gives a separate test of that
sign, with its pressure and the response of the base included.
Use the envelope $g$, base $V_h$, and parent $W_2$ defined above. Fix
$\nu>0$ and $0<h\leq1/6$. With a constant $b>0$ to be chosen, put
\[
 \delta=\lambda^{-2h},\quad \epsilon=b\lambda^{-h},\quad
 \mu=\lambda\delta,\quad
 C_\epsilon=\pi\norm g_2^2(1+3\epsilon^2/4),\quad
 A=\lambda^{1+2h}/\sqrt{C_\epsilon},
\]
and take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:singleHelicalDatum}
 u_\lambda(x_h,z)=A g(\mu z)
       [V_h(\lambda x_h)+\epsilon\operatorname{Re}
                   (W_2(\lambda x_h)e^{2i\lambda z})].
\end{equation}
Here $\epsilon\leq1$ for all sufficiently large $\lambda$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:totalHelicalWork}
One can choose $b$ depending on $\nu$ so that the actual unforced
evolutions from \eqref{eq:singleHelicalDatum} exist until
$t_\lambda=s_*/(A\lambda)$ and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:totalHelicalGain}
 \frac12\norm{\nabla u(t_\lambda)}_2^2
       -\frac12\norm{\nabla u(0)}_2^2\geq c\lambda^{1+4h}.
\end{equation}
Their initial squared energies are $\lambda^{-1+6h}\leq1$, their
relative angular defects tend to zero, and their means obey
\eqref{eq:meanr}--\eqref{eq:meanz} throughout this interval, with
$\tau=\lambda^{-2}-t$ and a constant independent of $\lambda$.
Also $\int_0^{t_\lambda}\norm{u-\PP u}_\infty^2\dd t\leq Cb^2$.

Consequently, for every fixed Fourier cutoff $K<\infty$ and
$\theta\leq1$, the high-frequency projection $u_{\geq K}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:totalHelicalTail}
 \int_0^{t_\lambda}
 \left[\langle-\Delta u_{\geq K},\mathcal N(u)\rangle
              -\theta\nu\norm{\Delta u_{\geq K}}_2^2\right]\dd t
                            \longrightarrow+\infty.
\end{equation}
The projection uses the physical convention $e^{ik\cdot x}$ and
$|k|\geq K$. The constants in this proposition are independent of
$\lambda$; no constant uniform over full initial Sobolev norms is claimed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
First compute the full signed response at $\delta=\eta=0$, with
$\eta=\nu\lambda/A$, per unit fast axial phase and with the slow
envelope temporarily suppressed. More generally let the fast carrier
be $\kappa>0$. Write
\begin{gather*}
 v(R)=2Re^{-R^2},\qquad f(R)=R^2e^{-R^2},\\
 \Omega=v/R,\quad \zeta=v'+v/R,\quad d=v'-v/R,\quad
 L_2=\partial_{RR}+R^{-1}\partial_R-4R^{-2}.
\end{gather*}
The complex parent has cylindrical components $(2if/R,-f',0)$ and
phase $e^{2i\phi+i\alpha}$. Its linear pressure amplitude $p$ solves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:helicalPressureResolvent}
 (-L_2+\kappa^2)p=F,
 \qquad F(R)=32R^2(R^2-1)e^{-2R^2}.
\end{equation}
This source is twice the trace of the base and parent velocity-gradient
matrices; the cylindrical basis derivatives are included.

Let $\Pi=\int\omega\cdot(\omega\cdot\nabla)u$ denote the total
signed enstrophy production, and let $\mathcal F=(\omega\cdot\nabla)u$.
In an unforced inviscid evolution the material identities give
\[
 (\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla)\mathcal F=-(\nabla^2p)\omega,
 \qquad \Pi'=\int(|\mathcal F|^2-\omega\cdot\nabla^2p\,\omega).
\]
Indeed the two products of velocity gradients cancel when
differentiating $\mathcal F$; transport integrates to zero.
The coefficient of $\epsilon^2$ in this identity is
\[
 c_\kappa=\pi\kappa^2\int_0^\infty
 R d[4\zeta f^2/R^2-2\Omega(f')^2]\dd R
       +2\pi\kappa^2\int_0^\infty R p\zeta'f'\dd R.
\]
The first integral is exactly zero. In the second, the vertical
parent vorticity $-L_2f$ cancels the term $\zeta L_2f$ from the
horizontal pressure pairing. Omitting that component gives an incorrect
coefficient. The second-order pressure makes no further contribution:
its pairing with the two base vorticities is an axial second derivative
whose phase average is zero.

The remaining sign can be proved explicitly. The angular heat kernel is
\[
 e^{tL_2}(R^2e^{-\beta R^2})
    =\frac{R^2}{(1+4\beta t)^3}
                 e^{-\beta R^2/(1+4\beta t)}.
\]
Differentiate in $\beta$ to heat $F$. Gaussian integration gives
\[
 J(t):=\int_0^\infty R(e^{tL_2}F)\zeta'f'\dd R
      =\frac{5-24t-256t^3}{2(1+4t)^6}.
\]
Although $J$ changes sign, its primitive is strictly positive:
\[
 B(t)=\int_0^tJ(s)\dd s
      =\frac{t(128t^4+160t^3+112t^2+28t+5)}{2(1+4t)^5}>0
       \quad(t>0),\qquad B(\infty)=\frac1{16}.
\]
The decaying resolvent in \eqref{eq:helicalPressureResolvent} and
integration by parts therefore yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:helicalPositiveCoefficient}
 c_\kappa=2\pi\kappa^2\int_0^\infty e^{-\kappa^2t}J(t)\dd t
         =2\pi\kappa^4\int_0^\infty e^{-\kappa^2t}B(t)\dd t>0.
\end{equation}
All radial boundary terms vanish by smoothness at the axis and decay.
The checker verifies the source, both pressure components, the heat
equation and the displayed primitive. These are exact identities;
positivity follows from the integral formula, not a numerical sign test.

Return to the actual extended flow with $D_3=\delta\partial_Z+
2\partial_\alpha$ and initial field $g(Z)[V_h+
\epsilon\operatorname{Re}(W_2e^{i\alpha})]$.
The preserved joint rotation--phase symmetry from
Proposition~\ref{prop:fastseeds} makes every horizontally integrated
rotation-invariant density independent of $\alpha$. Its physical trace
and its phase average consequently have the same integral. In particular
the computation above applies to the actual physical enstrophy, not to
an ensemble of different solutions.

Let $\mathcal P_{\rm tot}(s)$ be this total normalized production. Initially
$\mathcal P_{\rm tot}(0)=0$ exactly. For a horizontal solenoidal initial field,
the integrated production is
$-\int(\partial_z u_h)\cdot(\nabla_hu_h)(\partial_z u_h)$.
Here the terms with two envelope derivatives vanish by the planar
kinetic identity, the terms with two phase derivatives vanish after
angular averaging, and the remaining terms integrate a multiple of
$g^2g'$ over $Z$, which is zero.

We justify a time interval uniform in $\epsilon,\delta,\eta$.
The extended transport estimate and finite parameter derivatives used
in Proposition~\ref{prop:mixedParents} give uniform Sobolev bounds
for three time derivatives and two amplitude derivatives, with ample
derivative margin. Hold $\eta$ independent of $\epsilon$ in these
estimates. Rotation by $\pi/2$ changes $\epsilon$ to $-\epsilon$,
so the physically integrated production and its time derivatives are
even functions of $\epsilon$.

For the zero-amplitude solution, the vertical velocity and its needed
time derivatives are $O(\delta)$ in Sobolev norms: their equations
have pressure source $-\delta\partial_Zp$ and zero initial vertical
velocity. The double-Riesz representation bounds $p$ uniformly.
Thus the horizontal vorticity and the mixed axial strains are
$O(\delta)$, while $\partial_z U_z=O(\delta^2)$. The same assertions
hold after the required time derivatives. Every contribution to
production at zero amplitude is therefore $O(\delta^2)$.
Evenness and the amplitude estimates imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:helicalTimeRemainder}
 |\mathcal P_{\rm tot}''(s)|\leq C(\delta^2+\epsilon^2)
\end{equation}
on a common normalized interval.

At time zero, the quartic inviscid production derivative has expansion
\[
 \mathcal P_{\rm tot}'(0)=O(\delta^2)+\epsilon^2(c_2\norm g_4^4+o(1))
                    +O(\epsilon^4)+O(\eta\epsilon^2).
\]
The $o(1)$ tends to zero with $\delta$: the Leray multipliers converge
almost everywhere, are uniformly bounded, and act on fixed smooth
inputs. The purely viscous zero-amplitude term is zero because the
heat evolution remains purely azimuthal. All constants are uniform
for the parameters at most one. With $\epsilon^2=b^2\delta$ and
$\eta=\nu\sqrt{C_\epsilon}\delta$, it follows that
$\mathcal P_{\rm tot}'(0)\geq c\epsilon^2$ for sufficiently large $\lambda$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:helicalTimeRemainder} and $\mathcal P_{\rm tot}(0)=0$
then give, for a fixed small $s_*>0$,
\[
 \int_0^{s_*}\mathcal P_{\rm tot}(s)\dd s\geq c\epsilon^2,
 \qquad
 \eta\int_0^{s_*}\norm{\Delta_D U(s)}_2^2\dd s\leq C\eta.
\]

Both enstrophy integrals acquire the physical factor $A^2/\mu$.
Since $C_\epsilon$ is bounded above and below, choosing $b$ sufficiently
large compared with $\sqrt\nu$ makes the difference at least
$c\lambda^{1+4h}$. The exact enstrophy identity proves
\eqref{eq:totalHelicalGain}. The symmetry, incompressibility, and
uniform Sobolev estimates prove the persistent mean geometry as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:fastseeds}. The amplitude derivative gives
$\norm{U-\PP U}_\infty\leq C\epsilon$, so the history is at most
$CA\epsilon^2/\lambda\leq Cb^2$. Orthogonality of angular modes
gives the asserted initial energy.

Finally write $E_{\geq K}=\norm{\nabla u_{\geq K}}_2^2/2$.
The kinetic energy identity bounds the complementary gradient energy
by $K^2/2$. Hence its subtraction changes the enstrophy increment by
at most $K^2/2$. The exact projected identity gives
\[
 \int_0^{t_\lambda}(P_{\geq K}-\theta D_{\geq K})\dd t
 =E_{\geq K}(t_\lambda)-E_{\geq K}(0)
                   +(1-\theta)\int_0^{t_\lambda}D_{\geq K}\dd t
 \geq c\lambda^{1+4h}-K^2/2,
\]
where $D_{\geq K}=\nu\norm{\Delta u_{\geq K}}_2^2$.
This proves \eqref{eq:totalHelicalTail}.
\end{proof}

This example includes the full parent response and preserves the displayed
mean geometry on its short interval. It excludes a uniform signed-tail
allowance based only on the bounded quantities just listed, with a fixed
cutoff. It does not exclude a cutoff or allowance depending on the full
datum: the initial enstrophy itself is of order $\lambda^{1+6h}$,
larger than the increase just proved. No sequence of these events within
one fixed solution, and no failure of the conditional regularity theorem,
is asserted.

\subsection{The generated seed couples to a growing linearized component}

The mean mode in Proposition~\ref{prop:mixedParents} is not automatically
a growing perturbation of the base. The following calculation checks
that coupling, but only for the inviscid linearization about the fixed
column $v(R)e_\theta=2Re^{-R^2}e_\theta$. This distinction matters:
in the nonlinear evolution the parents and the mean keep changing.
The norms below are radial Fourier-mode norms; the fixed column itself
is not a finite-energy field on the whole space.
The columnar-vortex linearization is classical; see, for example,
\cite[Eq.~(1.6)]{GS}. The stability assumptions of that reference are
not satisfied by this column, whose vorticity changes sign.

In the axisymmetric axial mode $e^{i\kappa z}$, write the velocity
components as $(a,b,c)$. At $\kappa=2$ their linearized equations are
\begin{gather}
 a_s-2\Omega b=-p_R,\qquad b_s+\zeta a=0,\qquad
 c_s=-2ip,\qquad a_R+a/R+2ic=0,\label{eq:linearColumn}\\
 \Omega=2e^{-R^2},\qquad \zeta=4(1-R^2)e^{-R^2},\qquad
 \Phi=2\Omega\zeta=16(1-R^2)e^{-2R^2}.
\end{gather}
In $H=L^2((0,\infty),R\dd R)$ let $A$ be the Friedrichs realization of
\[
 I-\tfrac14L_1,\qquad
 L_1=\partial_{RR}+R^{-1}\partial_R-R^{-2}.
\]
Its quadratic form, initially on $C_c^\infty(0,\infty)$, is
\[
 \mathfrak a(f,f)=\int_0^\infty
 R\left[|f|^2+\tfrac14(|f'|^2+|f/R|^2)\right]\dd R.
\]
Thus $A\geq I$, and $B=-A^{-1/2}\Phi A^{-1/2}$ is a bounded
self-adjoint operator on $H$. No discrete-spectrum assumption will be
needed. Elimination of the pressure in \eqref{eq:linearColumn} gives
\[
 Aa_s=2\Omega b,\qquad Aa_{ss}=-\Phi a,
 \qquad x_{ss}=Bx,\quad x=A^{1/2}a.
\]
Conversely these equations recover \eqref{eq:linearColumn} using
$b_s=-\zeta a$, $c=i(a_R+a/R)/2$, and
$p=-(a_{sR}+a_s/R)/4$. The form domain imposes the regular axis boundary
condition. The Gaussian test functions below lie in the form domain by
radial cutoff approximation, and their distributional $Af$ lies in $H$.
Integration by parts and form density therefore put them in the operator
domain as well.

Put $q=R^2$ and $Q_4(q)=8q^4-110q^3+346q^2-275q+27$.
The full cubic source computed in Proposition~\ref{prop:mixedParents},
before its last Leray projection, has components
\begin{align*}
 iP_r&=\frac{8i}{5}R(8q^4+136q^3-262q^2+27q+18)e^{-3q},\\
 P_\theta&=\frac85 R^3Q_4(q)e^{-3q},\qquad
 P_z=-\frac{32}{5}R^6(6q^2-25q+20)e^{-3q}.
\end{align*}
The checker verifies all three components. Its divergence is purely
imaginary, so the last pressure amplitude is purely imaginary too.
Consequently the fully projected source $C$ has a purely imaginary
radial component, the real azimuthal component $P_\theta$, and a real
axial component. Here $C$ is the unscaled complex cubic coefficient;
the real initial jet in \eqref{eq:mixedRealJet} has the additional
factor $\epsilon^3g(Z)^3/4$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:linearSeedCoupling}
Initialize the fixed-column linearized equation \eqref{eq:linearColumn}
with the fully projected coefficient $C$. Its meridional components
satisfy, for all $s\geq0$,
\[
 \big(\norm{a(s)}_H^2+\norm{c(s)}_H^2\big)^{1/2}
       \geq\frac{\sqrt{20}}{40}\sinh(s/\sqrt{20}).
\]
This is a statement about the complex Fourier coefficient in the fixed
inviscid linearization. It is not a growth bound for the actual nonlinear
solution from Proposition~\ref{prop:mixedParents}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The radial second-order equation has real coefficients. Set
$x_{\mathrm R}=\operatorname{Re}x$. Then $x_{\mathrm R}(0)=0$ and
\[
 (x_{\mathrm R})_s(0)=z:=A^{-1/2}h,\qquad
 h=2\Omega P_\theta=\tfrac{32}{5}R^3Q_4(R^2)e^{-4R^2}.
\]
The imaginary meridional part of the generated seed cannot cancel this
real solution in the complex Hilbert norm. It suffices to prove that
the spectral projection of $z$ onto $B>1/20$ has norm greater than $1/40$.

Here is a finite rational certificate. Let $L_j^1$ denote the generalized
Laguerre polynomial, and set
\[
 f(R)=10^{-5}Re^{-R^2}\sum_{j=0}^{31}d_jL_j^1(2R^2),
 \qquad \mu=\frac{21}{200},\qquad
 \mathcal R=-\Phi f-\mu Af.
\]
The coefficients $d_0,\ldots,d_{31}$, in row order, are
\[
 \begin{array}{rrrrrrrr}
 129288&-103710&405&2339&11058&4912&3067&42\\
 -645&-1238&-1012&-844&-499&-285&-77&28\\
 104&123&128&110&91&65&46&25\\
 14&0&-3&-13&-8&-18&-6&-19
 \end{array}.
\]
Exact Gaussian integration gives the strict inequalities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:seedCertificate}
 \mathfrak a(f,f)<\frac{101}{100},\qquad
 \langle h,f\rangle>\frac{27}{100},\qquad
 \norm h_H^2<28,\qquad
 \norm{\mathcal R}_H^2<\frac1{160000}.
\end{equation}
The accompanying checker constructs the polynomial coefficients and
integrates each monomial using
$\int_0^\infty q^ne^{-tq}\dd q=n!/t^{n+1}$.
All comparisons use rational arithmetic, without a numerical eigensolver
or rounded quadrature. It independently checks the form norm against
the differential expression for $Af$.

To see why these four inequalities suffice, put $y=A^{1/2}f$ and let
$E_-$ be the spectral projection onto $B\leq\rho$, where $\rho=1/20$.
Then
\[
 (B-\mu)y=A^{-1/2}\mathcal R,\qquad
 \norm{E_-y}_H\leq\frac{\norm{\mathcal R}_H}{\mu-\rho},\qquad
 \langle z,y\rangle=\langle h,f\rangle.
\]
Use $\norm{A^{-1/2}}\leq1$ and
$\mu-\rho=11/200$. Equation~\eqref{eq:seedCertificate} gives
\[
 |\langle E_-z,E_-y\rangle|
 <\frac{(16/3)(1/400)}{11/200}=\frac8{33},\qquad
 \norm y_H<\frac{101}{100}.
\]
Since $27/100-8/33>(101/100)/40$, Cauchy--Schwarz yields
$\norm{(I-E_-)z}_H>1/40$.

The bounded self-adjoint equation $(x_{\mathrm R})_{ss}=Bx_{\mathrm R}$
with $x_{\mathrm R}(0)=0$ has, on
the positive spectral subspace, multiplier
$\sinh(s\sqrt\sigma)/\sqrt\sigma$ applied to $z$.
For $\sigma>\rho$ this multiplier is at least
$\sinh(s\sqrt\rho)/\sqrt\rho$, as follows directly from its power
series. Thus
\[
 \norm{x_{\mathrm R}(s)}_H
       \geq\frac{\sqrt{20}}{40}\sinh(s/\sqrt{20}).
\]
Finally incompressibility and the radial integration by parts give
$\norm a_H^2+\norm c_H^2=\mathfrak a(a,a)=\norm x_H^2$.
This also follows by density from the form domain and completes the proof.
\end{proof}

The certificate establishes coupling to growth, not persistence of the
actual forcing that created the seed. At cubic order the evolving parents
drive an inhomogeneous equation. Their subsequent signed contributions
could reinforce or offset the initial one; a nonzero initial jet does not
determine that history. Viscosity, the slow envelope, and a controlled
longer nonlinear time interval also remain to be included. The classical
fixed-column instability and this explicit seed coupling supply none of
the arbitrary-data physical-time budget required for continuation.

\subsection{A signed numerical history with evolving parents}

The cubic amplitude variation provides a finite test of the preceding
distinction. Label modes by their angular and axial indices, with physical
axial wave number twice the latter. Write
$a=(2,1)$, $b=(-4,-1)$, $d=(4,2)$, $e=(-2,0)$, and $c=(0,1)$.
If $\mathcal L_j$ is the full inviscid linearization about $V_h$ on
mode $j$, and $\mathcal B(u,v)=-\mathbb P[(u\cdot\nabla)v]$, the exact
ancestry of the cubic recipient is
\begin{align*}
 a'&=\mathcal L_a a,& b'&=\mathcal L_b b,\\
 d'&=\mathcal L_d d+\mathcal B(a,a),&
 e'&=\mathcal L_e e+\mathcal B(a,b)+\mathcal B(b,a),\\
 c'&=\mathcal L_c c+\mathcal B(a,e)+\mathcal B(e,a)
                         +\mathcal B(b,d)+\mathcal B(d,b).
\end{align*}
Initially $a=W_2$, $b=\overline{W_4}$, and $d=e=c=0$.
Every intermediate Leray projection is retained.
In particular $c'(0)=0$ and $c''(0)$ is the full cubic coefficient
checked in Proposition~\ref{prop:mixedParents}.
These coefficients are unscaled: the real datum's contribution at this
order is $\epsilon^3\operatorname{Re}(c(s)e^{2iz})/4$.

Let $f,\mu,R=-\Phi f-\mu Af$ be the rational test and its residual
from Proposition~\ref{prop:linearSeedCoupling}, and put
$\gamma=\sqrt\mu$. If $a_c,b_c$ are the radial and azimuthal components
of the recipient, define the complex scalar
\[
 q_c=\gamma\langle Af,a_c\rangle_H+\langle2\Omega f,b_c\rangle_H.
\]
Writing $F_c$ for its evolving nonlinear source, the exact axisymmetric
linear equations give
\[
 q_c'-\gamma q_c
  =\langle R,a_c\rangle_H+
       \gamma\langle Af,F_{c,r}\rangle_H+
                         \langle2\Omega f,F_{c,\theta}\rangle_H.
\]
Thus the discounted Duhamel integral includes both the forcing and the
explicit residual. Treating $f$ as an exact eigenfunction would lose
the first term.

\begin{array}{c|c|c|r|r|r}

 256&14&0.01 &42.91325951&-1.21208574&3.95253213\\

\end{array}

Here $\norm c^2=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\R^2}|c|^2$, approximated by the

\subsection{Stretching need not drain the maximum mean circulation}

For the simplest helical parent, the total enstrophy initially curves
upward while the mean circulation at its original maximum curves
downward. This compensation is not forced by the equation. A change
of the radial profile, even one preserving its first two jets at that
radius, reverses the second sign.

Put
\[
 f_0(r)=r^2e^{-r^2},\qquad
 f_1(r)=r^2e^{-r^2}\big[1+(r^2-1)^3\big],\qquad
 W_j=\curl_h\big(f_j(r)e^{2i\phi}e_z\big).
\]
Consider local Euler evolutions on $\R^2\times(\R/\pi\mathbb Z)$
from $V_h+\epsilon\operatorname{Re}(W_j e^{2iz})$.
Use per-unit-axial-length integrals for the total production $\Pi_j$,
and write $G_j=r(\PP u_j)_\theta$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:helicalMeanFeedback}
At the initial mean-circulation maximum $r=1$,
\[
 G_{j,s}(0)=0,\qquad G_{j,ss}(0)=\epsilon^2 d_j,\qquad
 d_0<0,\qquad d_1>\frac{105}{128e^3}>0.
\]
Both profiles have positive total enstrophy curvature:
\[
 \Pi_j(0)=0,\qquad
 \Pi_j'(0)=\epsilon^2 c_j+O(\epsilon^4),\qquad
 c_0>0,\qquad c_1>\frac{1053\pi}{128}.
\]
The initial velocities and their first spatial derivatives agree on
the cylinder $r=1$ for the two profiles.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The last assertion follows from $f_1-f_0$ vanishing to order three
at $r=1$. In particular $f_j(1)=e^{-1}$, $f_j'(1)=0$, and
$f_j''(1)=-4e^{-1}$. Set $\Omega=2e^{-r^2}$ and
$\zeta=4(1-r^2)e^{-r^2}$.
For a real radial profile $f$, the complex parent components are
$(2if/r,-f',0)$. If $p$ is its linear pressure, the angular mean
of the time derivative of its radial/azimuthal stress is
\[
 T=\frac12\left[2\Omega(f')^2-\frac{4\zeta f^2}{r^2}
                              +f'p'-\frac{4fp}{r^2}\right].
\]
The initial stress itself vanishes. At the peak the derivative of
the mean-advection term vanishes because the initial mean meridional
velocity and the mean circulation gradient vanish. The averaged axial
stress has no axial dependence. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:helicalPeakAcceleration}
 d_j=-\left.\frac1r\partial_r(r^2T)\right|_{r=1}
                         =\frac4e p_j'(1)-\frac{16}{e^3}.
\end{equation}
There are no higher powers of $\epsilon$ in this second derivative:
the Euler second time derivative is cubic in the datum, and angular
averaging is even in $\epsilon$.

Let $L_2=\partial_{rr}+r^{-1}\partial_r-4r^{-2}$,
$\mathcal R=(-L_2+4)^{-1}$ with the smooth decaying radial solution,
and $q=r^2e^{-2r^2}$. The full cylindrical pressure source is
$4e^{-r^2}(L_2f-2rf'+8f)$. Direct differentiation gives
\begin{align*}
 p_0&=-2q+24\mathcal Rq,\\
 p_1&=q\left(-2r^6+\frac{15}2r^4-\frac{75}8r^2-\frac{57}{32}\right)
                                      +\frac{651}8\mathcal Rq.
\end{align*}
Thus, with $H=(\mathcal Rq)'(1)$,
\[
 p_0'(1)=4e^{-2}+24H,\qquad
 p_1'(1)=\frac{169}{16}e^{-2}+\frac{651}8H.
\]
The angular heat kernel identifies
\[
 H=\int_0^\infty e^{-4t}h(t)\dd t,\qquad
 h(t)=\frac{2(8t-1)}{(1+8t)^4}e^{-2/(1+8t)}.
\]
Writing $v=(1+8t)^{-1}$, its primitive is
\[
 C(t)=\int_0^t h
  =\frac14\left[\frac74e^{-2}
                    -(v^2+v/2+1/4)e^{-2v}\right].
\]
It decreases until $t=1/8$ and then increases to
$(7e^{-2}-1)/16<0$. Hence $C(t)<0$ for every $t>0$, and integration
by parts gives $H=4\int_0^\infty e^{-4t}C(t)\dd t<0$.
This proves $d_0=96H/e<0$.

For the opposite sign, discard the positive part of $h$ and use
$e^{-4t}\leq1$ on its negative part:
\[
 H\geq C(1/8)=\frac{7-3e}{16}e^{-2}
                             >-\frac5{64}e^{-2}.
\]
Here the elementary exponential series gives
$8/3<e<49/18<11/4$. Substitution into
\eqref{eq:helicalPeakAcceleration} yields
$d_1>105/(128e^3)$.

It remains to keep the full signed work, including the parent response.
The calculation of Proposition~\ref{prop:totalHelicalWork} applies
to every real radial profile. For $f_1$ the non-linearized-pressure
integral there is $I_0=1053/512$, and the heat-pressure pairing is
\[
 J_1(t)=\frac{9M(t)}{256(1+4t)^{12}},
 \qquad
 \begin{aligned}
 M(t)={}&67108864t^9+201326592t^8+127401984t^7\\
       &+60293120t^6+7004160t^5+3280896t^4\\
       &+77568t^3+51456t^2+1680t+5.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Every coefficient is positive. Therefore
\[
 c_1=4\pi I_0+8\pi\int_0^\infty e^{-4t}J_1(t)\dd t
                                          >\frac{1053\pi}{128}.
\]
The sign of $c_0$ was proved in
Proposition~\ref{prop:totalHelicalWork}. The checker
\texttt{labs/helical\_mean\_feedback.py} verifies the complete gradient
and pressure identities, matching jets, resolvent decompositions, heat
primitive, and Gaussian integrals with exact symbolic arithmetic.
\end{proof}

The simultaneous growth also survives on a common short interval of
actual viscous whole-space solutions.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:helicalSimultaneous}
Fix $\nu>0$, $0<h\leq1/6$, and the envelope $g$ used in
\eqref{eq:mixedDatum}. For a sufficiently large fixed $b$, put
\[
 \delta=\lambda^{-2h},\quad \epsilon=b\sqrt\delta,\quad
 \mu=\lambda\delta,\quad
 C_\epsilon=\pi\norm g_2^2(1+27\epsilon^2),\quad
 A=\lambda^{1+2h}/\sqrt{C_\epsilon},
\]
and take initial data
$u_\lambda=A g(\mu z)[V_h(\lambda x_h)+
 \epsilon\operatorname{Re}(W_1(\lambda x_h)e^{2i\lambda z})]$.
For all sufficiently large $\lambda$, their unforced viscous
evolutions exist through $t_\lambda=s_*/(A\lambda)$ and satisfy
\begin{align*}
 \tfrac12\norm{\nabla u(t_\lambda)}_2^2
       -\tfrac12\norm{\nabla u(0)}_2^2
                              &\geq c\lambda^{1+4h},\\
 \Gamma(\lambda^{-1},0,t_\lambda)-\sup_{r,z}\Gamma(r,z,0)
                              &\geq c b^2,
       \qquad \Gamma=r(\PP u)_\theta .
\end{align*}
Their initial squared energies are $\lambda^{-1+6h}\leq1$,
their relative angular defects tend to zero, their means retain the
bounds \eqref{eq:meanr}--\eqref{eq:meanz}, and their critical
fluctuation histories are at most $Cb^2$ on this interval.
The positive constants are independent of $\lambda$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The exact horizontal defect energy divided by $\pi$ is $27$.
The extended local evolution and amplitude estimates used in
Propositions~\ref{prop:fastseeds} and \ref{prop:totalHelicalWork}
apply unchanged to this fixed smooth helical profile.
Its positive total coefficient gives the enstrophy assertion after
choosing $b$ large enough to pay the viscous loss.

For clarity, the mean-circulation assertion requires a separate
parameter estimate. Write $\eta=\nu\lambda/A$ and evaluate the
normalized mean at $(R,Z)=(1,0)$. At zero amplitude its vertical
velocity is $O(\delta)$ on the common interval. Incompressibility
and smoothness at the axis then make its radial velocity
$O(\delta^2)$ on fixed radial compact sets, using
\[
 U_r(R,Z)=-\frac{\delta}{R}
                   \int_0^R \rho\,\partial_Z U_z(\rho,Z)\dd\rho.
\]
Its circulation equation
therefore gives a change $O(\delta^2+\eta)$ at this point.
No global radial-velocity norm of order $\delta^2$ is required.

The mean is even in $\epsilon$, by the preserved joint rotation--phase
symmetry. Four amplitude derivatives on the same interval give
\[
 G(s_*,\epsilon,\delta,\eta)-G(s_*,0,\delta,\eta)
       =\epsilon^2 D(s_*,\delta,\eta)+O(\epsilon^4).
\]
The coefficient converges as $(\delta,\eta)\to(0,0)$: the extended
Leray multipliers converge almost everywhere and are uniformly bounded,
and the differentiated local equations have uniform smooth bounds.
At the limit, $D(0)=D'(0)=0$ and $D''(0)=d_1>0$ at $Z=0$, since
$g(0)=1$. Choose a fixed small $s_*>0$ for which $D(s_*,0,0)>0$.
It follows that
\[
 G(s_*)-G(0)\geq c\epsilon^2-C(\delta^2+\eta)-C\epsilon^4.
\]
Since $\epsilon^2=b^2\delta$ and
$\eta=\nu\sqrt{C_\epsilon}\delta$, a fixed large $b$ makes this at
least $c\epsilon^2$ for large $\lambda$. Multiplication by $A/\lambda$
gives the asserted $cb^2$ physical increase. Initially the evaluated
point is a global maximum because $|g|\leq1$ and
$\max_R 2R^2e^{-R^2}=2/e$.
The mean geometry and history estimates follow from the same helical
symmetry and amplitude bounds as in Proposition~\ref{prop:totalHelicalWork}.
\end{proof}

This excludes compulsory depletion of the maximum mean circulation as
an explanation of the positive stretching event. It does not show
unbounded circulation for a single datum: the initial peak itself
grows like $\lambda^{2h}$, and the finite increase is paid at the same
order as the critical defect history. A quantitative recurrent estimate
with the full initial datum remains a separate question.

\subsection{The energy inequality alone permits the stress cost}

The time-integrated energy inequality cannot by itself exclude the cost
in \eqref{eq:stresscost}. An explicit path shows this while keeping all
the mean geometry bounds. This path is not a Navier--Stokes solution.
Use $W_+$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}, choose $0<h<1/4$, and set
\[
 \alpha=\tfrac12-2h,\qquad
 c^2=\frac{160\nu}{3\alpha},\qquad d^2=\frac{8\nu}{\alpha},\qquad
 A_0=\big(\tfrac92g_2c^2d\big)^{-1/2}.
\]
For $0<\tau\leq1$ define the whole-space smooth solenoidal path
\[
 u(x,-\tau)=A_0\tau^{-1/2-h/2}
  W_+\left(\frac{x_1}{c\sqrt\tau},
           \frac{x_2}{c\sqrt\tau},
           \frac{z}{d\tau^{1/2-h}}\right).
\]
It has zero angular mean. Its squared velocity norm $E$ and squared
gradient norm $D$ are exactly
\[
 E(-\tau)=\tau^\alpha,\qquad
 D(-\tau)=E(-\tau)\left(\frac{20}{3c^2\tau}
                        +\frac{1}{d^2\tau^{1-2h}}\right).
\]
Since $t=-\tau$, these imply
\[
 \tfrac12E'(t)+\nu D(t)
   =\frac{\alpha E(t)}{8\tau}(\tau^{2h}-3)<0,\qquad
 \nu\int_{-1}^0D(t)\dd t=\frac{1-2h}{4}<\infty.
\]
Thus the path starts with unit squared velocity norm and satisfies the
energy inequality on every time interval, with finite total dissipation.
On sufficiently late slices,
\[
 K(-\tau)=A_0\norm{W_+}_{L^\infty(\R^3)}\tau^{-1/2-h/2},
\]
so the critical history is infinite. Its radial--azimuthal stress has supremum
$2A_0^2e^{-2}\tau^{-1-h}$ and its axial--azimuthal stress is zero, so
\[
 \mathcal B_\tau(T)=\frac{2A_0^2e^{-2}}h(1-T^{-h})>0
\]
for fixed $T>1$ and sufficiently small $\tau$. The stress maximum then
lies in $B(1)$ throughout the interval.

The strict energy loss already shows why this is not an unforced smooth
solution, which would satisfy energy equality. Its zero mean also has
nonzero Reynolds torque, so an original force would be needed to keep
that mean zero. The example only separates the energy inequality and
mean geometry from the actual evolution required to control the history.

\section{Scope of the calculation}

The theorem allows an unbounded angular fluctuation, but requires its
history to satisfy \eqref{eq:history} and separately assumes the mean
geometry. Neither hypothesis is established here for general data.
Theorem~\ref{thm:amplitude} separately supplies a quantitative gradient
history under a stronger weighted amplitude condition. Its localization
constants may depend on the solution on a regular annulus; they are not
uniform data-only bounds.
In Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, an original force uniformly bounded in
spatial $C^2$ on $Q$ can also be included: its rescaled swirl and
vorticity sources vanish, and its
enstrophy contribution is bounded by $C(Y+1)$.
Thus a distinction between forced and unforced flows must enter a
proof of the hypotheses, not be inferred from this criterion.

The accompanying symbolic calculation checks the mean equation,
cylindrical stress divergence, lifted force identity, and the
stretching integration by parts on explicit polynomial fields with
all six stress components nonzero. It also checks the general cylindrical
stretching and local radial integration identities, and the Gaussian
moments and complete
convection work in Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}. The polynomial fields
are local algebra tests, whereas the Gaussian fields are finite-energy
initial data. It also checks the signed dual-work integrations and the
scaling of the final kinematic path. That path is explicitly distinct
from the actual unforced initial jets. The program does not integrate solutions or certify the
analytic proof. No statement in this
note has been added as an axiom or a theorem to the Lean library.
