\section{The problem and the role of hypothesis (H1)}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{The SG--Cole--Hopf route}

We work on the flat three-torus $\mathbb{T}^3$ with smooth divergence-free
initial data $u_0$. Ben Goertzel's manuscript \emph{Global Regularity of 3D
Navier--Stokes via SG--Cole--Hopf Program}, version~v7 (hereafter ``v7''), lifts
the incompressible Navier--Stokes equation to a diffusion on the
volume-preserving diffeomorphism group
\[
  \SG := \SDiff(\mathbb{T}^3),
\]
equipped with a right-invariant Fourier frame $\{D_i\}_{i\ge 1}$ induced by a
divergence-free frame $\{E_i\}_{i\ge 1}$ on $\mathbb{T}^3$. The generator and
carré du champ are
\[
  L := \sum_{i\ge 1} D_i^2,
  \qquad
  \Gamma(F) := \sum_{i\ge 1} (D_i F)^2 .
\]
One solves the SG heat equation $\partial_t \Phi = \nu L\Phi$ with a strictly
positive datum $\varphi = \exp(-W_0/(2\nu))$, applies the Cole--Hopf transform
$W = -2\nu \log \Phi$ to obtain an SG Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman potential, and
pushes the $\Gamma$-gradient down at the identity to a projected
Navier--Stokes solution $u(t)$ on $\mathbb{T}^3$.

The analytic content is concentrated entirely in closing the
Beale--Kato--Majda (BKM) vorticity criterion. With $u(t)=\mathrm{Ev}(W(t))$ and
$\omega(t)=\operatorname{curl}u(t)$, the push-down identity and Cauchy--Schwarz
give a frame constant $C_{\mathrm{curl}E}<\infty$ with
\begin{equation}
  \|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb{T}^3)}
    \le \sqrt{C_{\mathrm{curl}E}}\,\sqrt{\Gamma(W(t))(\mathrm{id})},
  \label{eq:vort}
\end{equation}
so that a uniform-in-time bound on $\Gamma(W(t))(\mathrm{id})$ makes the BKM
integral $\int_0^T \|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}\,dt$ finite on every finite interval.
By the log chain rule $\Gamma(\log\Phi)=\Gamma(\Phi)/\Phi^2$ and the maximum
principle $\Phi(t,\mathrm{id})\ge m_\varphi$ (v7, Prop.~7.1),
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma(W(t))(\mathrm{id})
    = 4\nu^2\,\Gamma(\log\Phi(t))(\mathrm{id})
    \le \frac{4\nu^2}{m_\varphi^2}\,\Gamma(P_t\varphi)(\mathrm{id}),
  \label{eq:logbound}
\end{equation}
which reduces the whole program to a single uniform bound on
$\Gamma(P_t\varphi)(\mathrm{id})$.

\subsection{Reduction to a local energy bound, and hypothesis (H1)}

Rather than invoking a global Bakry--\'Emery $\Gamma_2$ curvature lower bound,
v7 obtains that single bound from a \emph{local} package. Let
$\kappa : U \to V \subset \Hms(\mathbb{T}^3)$ be the chart of a small
$\SDiff^m$ neighborhood of the identity ($m\ge 5$ integer, with the
$\Gamma$-Hilbert structure at $\mathrm{id}$ equivalent to the $\Hms$ norm), let
$\chi$ be the cutoff defining $W_0$ and $\varphi$, and set the cutoff region
\[
  K := \kappa^{-1}(\operatorname{supp}\chi)\subset U .
\]
Outside $K$ one has $W_0\equiv 0$, hence $\varphi\equiv 1$ and
$\Gamma(\varphi)\equiv 0$. On $K$, v7 assumes two local hypotheses (v7,
\S5.3 and \S6, displayed at Lemma~D.18):
\begin{align}
  \text{(H1) Adjoint bounded on support:} \quad
    & \exists\,\CAd<\infty : \;
      \|\Ad_g\|_{\Hm\to\Hm}\le \CAd \quad \text{for all } g\in K,
      \label{eq:H1}\\[2pt]
  \text{(H2) Finite cutoff energy:} \quad
    & \exists\,\Cphi<\infty : \;
      \Gamma(\varphi)(g)\le \Cphi^2 \quad \text{for all } g\in K.
      \label{eq:H2}
\end{align}
From \eqref{eq:H1}--\eqref{eq:H2} v7 derives the key estimate via a conjugation
differentiation identity (v7, Prop.~6.5, proved as Lemma~F.7): for all $t\ge 0$,
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma(P_t\varphi)(\mathrm{id}) \;\le\; \CAd^2\,\Cphi^2 .
  \label{eq:prop65}
\end{equation}
The mechanism is transparent. In the flow representation
$P_t F(g)=\mathbb{E}[F(G_t\circ g)]$, differentiating along a conjugation curve
$C_{t,i}(\varepsilon)=g\circ\exp(\varepsilon E_i)\circ g^{-1}$ produces the
direction $\Ad_g E_i$, and Cauchy--Schwarz in the $\Gamma$-Hilbert structure
gives the pointwise bound
\begin{equation}
  \bigl|D_{\Ad_g E_i}\varphi(g)\bigr|^2
    \le \|\Ad_g\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\,\Gamma(\varphi)(g)
    \le \CAd^2\,\Cphi^2\,\mathbf{1}_{\{g\in K\}} ,
  \label{eq:cauchyschwarz}
\end{equation}
after which a Jensen/energy reduction yields \eqref{eq:prop65}. Chaining
\eqref{eq:prop65} through \eqref{eq:logbound} and \eqref{eq:vort} closes BKM.
Hypothesis~(H1) is therefore the single load-bearing analytic input of the
route: \emph{every} subsequent estimate inherits the constant $\CAd$ from
\eqref{eq:H1}, and the program establishes global regularity if and only if
\eqref{eq:H1} holds.

\subsection{How v7 attempts to verify (H1)}

v7 verifies~(H1) at Lemma~6.1 (and again at Lemma~D.18). The adjoint /
push-forward action is written, for $g\in\SDiff^m$ and $v\in\Hms$, as
\begin{equation}
  \Ad_g v := (Dg)\,\bigl(v\circ g^{-1}\bigr),
  \label{eq:adg}
\end{equation}
and the argument is that, for $m>5/2$, composition and inversion are continuous
(indeed smooth) operations on $\SDiff^m$, so the map $(g,v)\mapsto \Ad_g v$ is
continuous from $\SDiff^m\times\Hms$ into $\Hms$; hence
$g\mapsto\|\Ad_g\|_{\mathrm{op};\,\Hm\to\Hm}$ is locally bounded near
$\mathrm{id}$, and one shrinks $K$ until \eqref{eq:H1} holds.

The gap is in \eqref{eq:adg} itself. The factor $Dg$ is the Jacobian of $g$, and
a map $g\in\Hm$ has Jacobian $Dg\in H^{m-1}$ only. The adjoint thus carries a
\emph{derivative loss}: $\Ad_g$ maps $\Hm$ into $H^{m-1}$, and there is no
uniform $\Hm\to\Hm$ operator bound near the identity in the $\Hm$ topology.
This is the classical Ebin--Marsden phenomenon: right translation on
$\SDiff^m$ is smooth, but the adjoint of the right-invariant frame is not a
bounded $\Hm$ operator uniformly over an $\Hm$-neighborhood. The
local-boundedness argument conflates topological-group continuity of
composition with the operator-norm estimate \eqref{eq:H1} that the route
actually needs.

\section{The Fourier-mode shear model and the checked $\neg$(H1) theorems}
\label{sec:lean}

We make the derivative-loss obstruction precise and machine-checkable in a clean
analytic model, on the normalized two-torus, of an area-preserving shear family
\[
  g_k(x,y) = \bigl(x,\; y + a_k \sin(kx)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \delta_k := g_k - \mathrm{id} = \bigl(0,\; a_k\sin(kx)\bigr).
\]
The amplitude $a_k$ is chosen so that the displacement has \emph{fixed} $\Hm$
size $\varepsilon$ for every wavenumber $k$; thus the whole family sits inside
any prescribed chart radius. Applying the push-forward \eqref{eq:adg} to the
constant horizontal field $v=(1,0)$ gives
\[
  \Ad_{g_k} v = \bigl(1,\; a_k\, k\, \cos(kx)\bigr),
\]
and, with the same $\Hm$ normalization, the operator ratio is
\[
  \frac{\|\Ad_{g_k}v\|_{\Hm}}{\|v\|_{\Hm}}
    = \sqrt{1+(\varepsilon k)^2}.
\]
The displacement stays at size $\varepsilon$ while the ratio grows without bound:
no finite $\CAd$ can dominate it on any positive chart radius.

This is formalized, with no \texttt{sorry} and by elementary means, in the Lean
module \texttt{Mettapedia.FluidDynamics.NavierStokes.BenH1Break}. The model
quantities are defined as follows.

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
/-- In the normalized two-torus shear family, every selected displacement has
fixed `H^m` size `epsilon`. -/
def benH1ModeDisplacementHNorm (epsilon : Real) (_k : Nat) : Real :=
  epsilon

/-- Lower-bound ratio obtained by applying the shear push-forward to the
constant horizontal vector field. -/
def benH1ModeAdjointRatio (epsilon : Real) (k : Nat) : Real :=
  Real.sqrt (1 + (epsilon * (k : Real)) ^ 2)

/-- The linearized commutator part alone grows like `epsilon * k`. -/
def benH1ModeCommutatorRatio (epsilon : Real) (k : Nat) : Real :=
  epsilon * (k : Real)
\end{lstlisting}

Hypothesis~(H1), restricted to this normalized family inside a chart radius
$\rho$ (taking $\varepsilon=\rho/2$ so the family is strictly inside the ball),
is the predicate:

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
/-- H1 restricted to the normalized Fourier-mode shear family inside chart
radius `rho`. -/
def benH1FourierModeUniformBoundWithinRadius (rho C : Real) : Prop :=
  forall k : Nat,
    benH1ModeDisplacementHNorm (rho / 2) k <= rho ->
      benH1ModeAdjointRatio (rho / 2) k <= C
\end{lstlisting}

The core fact is that the adjoint ratio dominates the linear commutator part
$\varepsilon k$, which is unbounded over $k$. The proof is fully elementary: it
picks $k>C/\varepsilon$, lower-bounds $\sqrt{1+(\varepsilon k)^2}$ by
$\varepsilon k$, and derives a contradiction.

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
theorem benH1ModeAdjointRatio_unbounded {epsilon C : Real}
    (hepsilon : 0 < epsilon) :
    Not (forall k : Nat, benH1ModeAdjointRatio epsilon k <= C) := by
  intro hbound
  obtain ⟨k, hk⟩ := exists_nat_gt (C / epsilon)
  have hC_lt : C < epsilon * (k : Real) := by
    have hmul := mul_lt_mul_of_pos_left hk hepsilon
    field_simp [ne_of_gt hepsilon] at hmul
    exact hmul
  have hnonneg : 0 <= epsilon * (k : Real) := by positivity
  have hlinear_le_ratio :
      epsilon * (k : Real) <= benH1ModeAdjointRatio epsilon k := by
    unfold benH1ModeAdjointRatio
    have hsquare :
        (epsilon * (k : Real)) ^ 2 <= 1 + (epsilon * (k : Real)) ^ 2 := by
      linarith
    calc
      epsilon * (k : Real)
          = Real.sqrt ((epsilon * (k : Real)) ^ 2) := by
              rw [Real.sqrt_sq_eq_abs, abs_of_nonneg hnonneg]
      _ <= Real.sqrt (1 + (epsilon * (k : Real)) ^ 2) :=
              Real.sqrt_le_sqrt hsquare
  exact not_lt_of_ge (le_trans hlinear_le_ratio (hbound k)) hC_lt
\end{lstlisting}

From this the refutation of~(H1) on the model follows directly. Any positive
chart radius $\rho$ admits the normalized family at $\varepsilon=\rho/2$ (whose
displacements all have $\Hm$ size $\rho/2\le\rho$), and its adjoint ratios are
unbounded, contradicting any proposed finite $C$.

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
/-- Any positive chart radius contains the normalized shear family with
`epsilon = rho / 2`, and its adjoint ratios are unbounded. -/
theorem not_benH1FourierModeUniformBoundWithinRadius {rho C : Real}
    (hrho : 0 < rho) :
    Not (benH1FourierModeUniformBoundWithinRadius rho C) := by
  intro hbound
  have hepsilon : 0 < rho / 2 := by positivity
  have hmode_bound : forall k : Nat, benH1ModeAdjointRatio (rho / 2) k <= C := by
    intro k
    apply hbound k
    unfold benH1ModeDisplacementHNorm
    linarith
  exact benH1ModeAdjointRatio_unbounded hepsilon hmode_bound

/-- Compact public verdict: Ben's H1, as a uniform `H^m -> H^m` adjoint bound,
is false on the normalized Fourier-mode shear model for every positive chart
radius and every proposed finite constant. -/
theorem benGoertzelH1_false_in_fourierModeShearModel :
    forall rho C : Real,
      0 < rho -> Not (benH1FourierModeUniformBoundWithinRadius rho C) := by
  intro rho C hrho
  exact not_benH1FourierModeUniformBoundWithinRadius (rho := rho) (C := C) hrho
\end{lstlisting}

The three checked statements are
\texttt{benH1ModeAdjointRatio\_unbounded},
\texttt{not\_benH1FourierModeUniformBoundWithinRadius}, and the compact verdict
\texttt{benGoertzelH1\_false\_in\_fourierModeShearModel}: for every positive
chart radius and every proposed finite constant, the modeled (H1) uniform
$\Hm\to\Hm$ adjoint bound fails. (A companion lemma
\texttt{benH1ModeCommutatorRatio\_unbounded} records that the linearized
commutator part $\varepsilon k$ is already unbounded on its own.)

\section{The numeric lab: the norms are derived, not assumed}
\label{sec:lab}

The Lean development encodes the two normalized quantities
$\|\delta_k\|_{\Hm}=\varepsilon$ and
$\|\Ad_{g_k}v\|_{\Hm}/\|v\|_{\Hm}=\sqrt{1+(\varepsilon k)^2}$ as definitions. To
confirm that these are not circular --- that the factor $k$ in the adjoint ratio
is a genuine consequence of the Sobolev-weight structure and the derivative in
\eqref{eq:adg}, rather than an assumption --- a companion numeric lab
(\texttt{ns\_ben\_h1\_fourier\_mode\_lab.py}, in the MeTTapedia validation lab)
\emph{computes} the norms from first quantities. With $\Hm$ Fourier weight
$w_k = (1+k^2)^{m/2}$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the amplitude is set to $a_k = \sqrt{2}\,\varepsilon / w_k$, so the
        displacement norm is
        $\|\delta_k\|_{\Hm} = |a_k|\,w_k/\sqrt{2} = \varepsilon$
        (the $\sqrt2$ is the $L^2$ normalization of $\sin(kx)$);
  \item differentiating, $\partial_x\bigl(a_k\sin(kx)\bigr)=a_k k\cos(kx)$, whose
        $\Hm$ norm is $|a_k|\,k\,w_k/\sqrt{2} = \varepsilon k$. The weight $w_k$
        \emph{cancels} the amplitude, leaving $\varepsilon$; the surviving factor
        $k$ is the one derivative gained by $D g_k$;
  \item the constant field has $\|v\|_{\Hm}=1$, so the adjoint norm is
        $\sqrt{1^2+(\varepsilon k)^2}$ and the ratio is
        $\sqrt{1+(\varepsilon k)^2}$.
\end{itemize}
This is exactly the Ebin--Marsden derivative loss of \eqref{eq:adg} made
quantitative: $g_k\in\Hm$ has $Dg_k\in H^{m-1}$, and the action of $Dg_k$
multiplies the mode's high-frequency content by its wavenumber. Table~\ref{tab:lab}
lists the lab output for $\varepsilon=0.01$, $m=3$. The displacement norm stays
fixed at $\varepsilon$ across four decades of $k$ while the adjoint ratio grows.

\subsection{Why a pointwise transport route can evade the H1 countermodel}
\label{sec:evasion}

The first question for any repair is whether the Section~\ref{sec:lean}
countermodel already defeats it.  It does not, and this is itself a theorem.
On \emph{the same} $\Hm$-normalized shear family that refutes (H1), the
module \texttt{ShearJacobianBounds} proves
(\texttt{shearFamily\_pointwise\_bounded\_while\_sobolev\_ratio\_unbounded}):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item every pointwise Jacobian deviation is bounded by $\varepsilon$
        uniformly in the frequency $k$
        (\texttt{shearFamily\_jacobian\_uniform\_bound}:
        $\|Dg_k(x)v - v\| \le \varepsilon\|v\|$ for all $x,v$); while
  \item the $\Hm\to\Hm$ operator ratio $\sqrt{1+(\varepsilon k)^2}$ of the
        same family admits no finite uniform bound (Section~\ref{sec:lean}).
\end{enumerate}

The high-frequency-shear derivative-loss mechanism therefore lives entirely
in the Sobolev operator topology: the quantity feeding a pointwise gate
$\int_0^T \mathbb{E}\|DX_t\|_\infty\,dt$ is not stressed at all by this
family.  The checked statement is deliberately labeled: it does not prove any
pointwise gate --- it proves this family cannot refute one.  That is what
licenses moving the route from adjoint bounds to Lagrangian transport.

\subsection{The one-form layer: non-exact momenta and the frame Laplacian}
\label{sec:oneform}

The repaired state is a momentum one-form with a gauge, $\mu=u^\flat+dW$,
not a scalar potential.  Two checked facts pin down why this shape is forced
and why the frame generator acts correctly on it.

\emph{Non-exact momenta are necessary.}  Exact one-forms are closed
(\texttt{oneFormExtDerivAt\_eq\_zero\_of\_exact}), and there is a smooth,
everywhere divergence-free field that is not exact --- the transverse sine
mode:

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
theorem exists_divergenceFree_smooth_not_exact_dim3 :
    exists u0 : EuclideanSpace Real (Fin 3) -> EuclideanSpace Real (Fin 3),
      ContDiff Real 2 u0 /\
      (forall x, coordDivergence u0 x = 0) /\
      Not (IsExactOneForm u0)
\end{lstlisting}

A scalar-potential (pure Cole--Hopf) ansatz therefore cannot carry a general
divergence-free momentum: vorticity lives exactly in the non-exact part.
This is the constructive face of the Section~\ref{sec:problem} diagnosis, and
it is what the additive gauge $dW$ is for.

\emph{The frame reproduces the Laplacian.}  The route's generator is a sum of
squared frame derivatives (v7's $L=\sum_i D_i^2$).  On the truncated
trigonometric coordinate frame (three axes, transverse polarizations, both
phases) the squared Lie derivatives of a one-form sum \emph{exactly} to the
coordinate Laplacian: with amplitude $1/\sqrt2$ in dimension~3,
\[
  \sum_{j}\sum_{p\ne j}
    \Bigl(L^2_{\cos\text{-mode}(j,p)} + L^2_{\sin\text{-mode}(j,p)}\Bigr)\mu
  = \Delta\mu
\]
(\texttt{frameLaplacian\_oneForm\_normalized}, from the general
$(\mathrm{card}\,\iota-1)a^2$ identity \texttt{frameLaplacian\_oneForm} and
the phase-pair cancellation \texttt{lieOneForm\_sq\_cos\_add\_sin}).  The
supporting calculus (\texttt{GradientOneForm}) includes the coordinate Cartan
formula for exact forms (\texttt{lieOneForm\_gradField}: the Lie derivative
of $dV$ along $u$ is exact with potential $\langle u,\nabla V\rangle$, i.e.\
$L_u\,dV = d\langle u,\nabla V\rangle$ pointwise) and the self-transport
identity (\texttt{lieOneForm\_self}: $L_u u^\flat = (u\cdot\nabla)u +
\tfrac12\nabla|u|^2$).  These two identities are what turn transport
equations into Navier--Stokes with a pressure, next.

\subsection{The pushdown theorem: Navier--Stokes with an explicit pressure}
\label{sec:pushdown}

The structure \texttt{TransportedMomentumData} packages a velocity $u$, a
gauge $W$, their certified time derivatives, the gradient/Laplacian exchange
facts, and one transport hypothesis --- the It\^o-averaged frame-transport
equation for the momentum one-form:
\[
  \partial_t u + \nabla \dot W
    + L_u\bigl(u^\flat + dW\bigr)
  = \nu \,\Sigma_{\mathrm{frame}}\bigl(u^\flat + dW\bigr),
\]
where $\Sigma_{\mathrm{frame}}$ is the normalized frame second-order sum of
Section~\ref{sec:oneform} (the hypothesis is stated in averaged form; no
stochastic calculus is formalized, and the field is labeled accordingly in
the source).  From this single hypothesis the pushdown theorem derives the
Navier--Stokes equations with a fully explicit pressure:

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
/-- Pushdown theorem: the averaged frame-transport equation for the
momentum one-form u^flat + dW forces u to satisfy the Navier-Stokes
equations with the explicit pressure
p = dW/dt + (1/2)<u,u> + <u, grad W> - nu * Lap W. -/
theorem navierStokes_of_transportedMomentum (t : Real) (x : R3) :
    deriv (fun s => D.u s x) t + fderiv Real (D.u t) x (D.u t x) +
      gradField (D.pressure t) x =
    D.nu smul coordLaplacian (D.u t) x
\end{lstlisting}

with
\[
  p \;=\; \partial_t W \;+\; \tfrac12\langle u,u\rangle
      \;+\; \langle u,\nabla W\rangle \;-\; \nu\,\Delta W .
\]
The pressure is not posited: it is assembled from the exact terms of the
transport (gauge drift, kinetic energy via the self-transport identity, gauge
advection via the Cartan formula, viscous gauge term), and the theorem is
unconditional given the data.  This is the repaired route's replacement for
v7's identity-energy pushdown: the same endpoint (a genuine Navier--Stokes
solution with pressure), reached without the false adjoint bound.

\subsection{The vorticity-stretching gate and the conditional route}
\label{sec:gate}

Continuation is fed by the averaged Cauchy formula
$\omega(t)=\mathbb{E}[(DX_t)(\omega_0\circ A_t)]$, which bounds
$\|\omega(t)\|_\infty$ by moments of the flow Jacobian.  The gate is the
named open input of the coarse route, exactly:

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
/-- The vorticity-stretching gate (named analytic input; OPEN). -/
def StochasticStretchingEstimate
    (D : StochasticCauchyVorticityData Omega) (T : Real) : Prop :=
  IntervalIntegrable D.expectedJacobianBound volume 0 T
\end{lstlisting}

Its consumers are mechanical and checked: a time-uniform Jacobian bound gives
a time-uniform vorticity bound
(\texttt{vorticity\_uniform\_bound\_of\_jacobian\_bound}), and under the gate
the vorticity norm $t\mapsto\|\omega(t,x)\|$ at each fixed point is interval-integrable with
$\int_0^T\|\omega\| \le \|\omega_0\|_\infty \int_0^T
\mathbb{E}\|DX_t\|_\infty$
(\texttt{bkm\_integrand\_intervalIntegrable}, \texttt{bkm\_integral\_bound}).
The assembled conditional route
(\texttt{stochasticLagrangian\_conditional\_route}) states: given transported
momentum data, averaged-Cauchy vorticity data linked by
$\omega=\operatorname{curl}u$, and the gate on $[0,T]$, the velocity
satisfies Navier--Stokes with the explicit pressure \emph{and} the BKM
integrand is controlled.  Every step is machine-checked; the only analytic
input is the gate.

\emph{The route is inhabited.}  Non-vacuity is itself checked, twice.  The
rest state instantiates the route end-to-end
(\texttt{conditional\_route\_rest}, with the gate discharged by a constant
envelope).  Beyond the rest state, the module \texttt{DecayingShearInstance}
provides a nonzero exact solution of the full transported-momentum system:
the decaying shear $u(t,y)=e^{-t}\sin(y_1)\,e_0$ with gauge amplitude
$b(t)=(e^{-2t}-e^{-4t})/8$ on the frequency-doubled mode, whose balance
$b'=-4b+\tfrac14 e^{-2t}$ (\texttt{gaugeAmp\_balance}) absorbs the kinetic
self-transport term; \texttt{decayingShear\_navierStokes} confirms the pair
satisfies Navier--Stokes with the representation pressure at $\nu=1$.  So the
conditional machinery is not vacuously true: its hypotheses have nonzero
exact witnesses.

\subsection{The frozen-strain countermodel and the aperture geometry}
\label{sec:frozen}

Any proof of the gate must use more than local strain geometry plus
stochastic averaging.  The frozen-strain model makes this a theorem.  For the
prescribed strain $A=\mathrm{diag}(2\gamma,-\gamma,-\gamma)$ and the pancake
packet (vorticity along the expanding axis, wave vector along a compressive
axis), the module \texttt{FrozenStrainModel} proves that
\[
  \omega(t,x) = a(t)\,\sin\bigl(k(t)\,x_1\bigr)\,e_0,
  \qquad
  k(t)=k_0e^{\gamma t},
  \qquad
  a(t)=a_0\exp\Bigl(2\gamma t - \nu k_0^2\tfrac{e^{2\gamma t}-1}{2\gamma}\Bigr)
\]
is an \emph{exact} solution of the frozen vorticity equation
$\partial_t\omega+(Ax\cdot\nabla)\omega=A\omega+\nu\Delta\omega$
(\texttt{frozenStrainVorticity\_solves}; the drift, stretching, phase and
viscous terms are all checked, not modeled).  Writing
$g=2\gamma/(\nu k_0^2)$ for the normalized strain, the amplification peaks at
$t^*=\log g/(2\gamma)$ with the exact value
\[
  \frac{a(t^*)}{a_0} = g\,e^{1/g-1}
  \qquad(\texttt{frozenStrainAmplification\_peak}),
\]
which is unbounded over the strain family: no uniform depletion bound of the
form ``pancake alignment always acquires diffusive decay at rate
$\nu\lambda^2$'' survives
(\texttt{frozenStrain\_no\_uniform\_amplification\_bound}).  Any correct gate
proof must therefore use the \emph{self-consistency} of the Navier--Stokes
strain --- a statement quantified over arbitrary imposed strains is false.

The angular version is the aperture-coverage geometry.  Anisotropic
Bernstein estimates control cone apertures up to $\lambda^{-1/2}$;
stretch--diffusion covariance controls apertures above
$\sqrt{\gamma/\nu}/\lambda$.  The band claimed by neither,
\[
  \bigl(\lambda^{-1/2},\; \sqrt{\gamma/\nu}/\lambda\bigr),
  \qquad\text{is nonempty}\iff \nu\lambda<\gamma
  \qquad(\texttt{anisotropic\_coverage\_gap}).
\]

\subsection{July additions: the aperture dichotomy is the strain budget}
\label{sec:aperture}

The July campaign sharpened the coverage gap into an equivalence and closed
the remaining escape routes.  In \texttt{ApertureCoverageDichotomy.lean}:

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
theorem pancakeApertureCovered_iff_strain_le
    (hnu : 0 < nu) (hlam : 0 < lam) :
    (forall theta : Real,
        theta <= (Real.sqrt lam)^{-1} \/ Real.sqrt (gamma / nu) / lam <= theta)
      <-> gamma <= nu * lam
\end{lstlisting}

That is: the ``narrow $\Rightarrow$ enstrophy-controlled, else
$\Rightarrow$ angularly coercive'' dichotomy tiles the whole aperture range
\emph{exactly when} the scale-local strain budget $\gamma\le\nu\lambda_q$
holds.  On the gap band both mechanisms fail quantitatively:
\texttt{gapAperture\_diffusionRate\_lt\_strain} proves
$\nu(\theta\lambda)^2<\gamma$ below the stretch--diffusion threshold (the
diffusive rate is strictly beaten by the strain), and
\texttt{gapAperture\_bernstein\_volume\_exceeds\_planar\_budget} proves
$\lambda^2<\theta^2\lambda^3$ above the Bernstein threshold (the cone's
Fourier-support volume exceeds the planar budget behind the improved
$L^2\to L^\infty$ constant).

The time-averaged escape route is closed by a Gr\"onwall-saturation
computation: over the coherent window the packet consumes an integrated
strain budget of exactly $\int_0^{t^*}2\gamma\,dt=\log g$
(\texttt{frozenStrain\_integratedStrain\_peakWindow}) while amplifying to
$g\,e^{1/g-1}\ge e^{\log g-1}$, i.e.\ amplification at least
$e^{\,\mathrm{budget}-1}$
(\texttt{frozenStrainAmplification\_peak\_ge\_exp\_integratedStrain\_sub\_one}).
An averaged strain hypothesis therefore bounds amplification only
exponentially in itself and cannot substitute for the pointwise-in-time
aperture condition in a dyadic relaxation estimate.

\subsection{The dyadic pancake closure: verified pieces and the isolated pin}
\label{sec:pancake}

The July repair proposal claims a complete closure of the gate: decompose the
weighted stretching sum by angular sector, prove the pancake-sector estimate
\[
  \int_0^T \sum_{q\ge Q}\lambda_q^{-2}
    \bigl\|A_q\,\omega_q^{\mathrm{pan}}\bigr\|_\infty\,dt
  \;\le\; C(T,\nu,u_0,Q) + \varepsilon\int_0^T D_Q(t)\,dt,
  \qquad
  D_Q(t):=\sum_{q\ge Q}\|\Delta_q\omega(t)\|_\infty,
\]
with $A_q=S(S_{q-L}u)$ the low-frequency strain and
$\omega_q^{\mathrm{pan}}$ the projection onto the pancake cone of aperture
$\delta_q=\lambda_q^{-1/2}$, then absorb $\varepsilon\int D_Q$ and conclude
$\int_0^T D_Q<\infty$, hence BKM.  The formalization adjudicates this claim
piece by piece.

\emph{The genuinely new ingredient is verified.}  The proposal's Step~1 --- a
pancake vorticity mode does not generate the strain that stretches itself ---
is checked exactly, not at symbol level.  In \texttt{PlaneWaveSelfStrain}:
for $\omega(x)=\alpha\sin(\kappa x_1)e_0$ with its Biot--Savart velocity (the
shear $u(x)=-(\alpha/\kappa)\cos(\kappa x_1)e_2$),
\texttt{curlField\_planeWaveVelocityMode} proves
$\operatorname{curl}u=\omega$ exactly, and
\texttt{strainRateApply\_planeWaveVelocityMode\_vorticity} proves
$S(u)\,\omega = \tfrac12\bigl(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\mathsf T}\bigr)\omega=0$
pointwise, with a non-vacuity witness
(\texttt{planeWaveVorticityMode\_ne\_zero}).  The scope is honest: the
identity is for one exact mode; a pancake \emph{sector} aggregates a cone of
modes, and charging the cross-mode strain is precisely the open budget below.

\emph{The absorption skeleton is checked with explicit constants.}  The
structure \texttt{DyadicVorticityStretchingData} carries the four time
functionals ($D_Q$; pancake, angularly coercive, and residual stretching
sums), their nonnegativity and integrability, and the transported dyadic
relaxation inequality.  The three sector estimates are explicit predicates,
e.g.
\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
def PancakeSectorEstimate (C eps : Real) : Prop :=
  (Integral of D.pancakeStretchSum over 0..D.T) <=
    C + eps * (Integral of D.highBlockSum over 0..D.T)
\end{lstlisting}
(coercive and residual analogous; both are \emph{interfaces} here ---
established in the source analysis by good-unknown/stretch--diffusion and
Coifman--Meyer arguments respectively, consumed as named hypotheses, not
re-proved).  The absorption theorem
\texttt{highBlockIntegral\_le\_of\_sectorEstimates} proves: sector estimates
with total gain $\varepsilon_p+\varepsilon_c+\varepsilon_r<1$ imply
\[
  \int_0^T D_Q(t)\,dt \;\le\;
  \frac{C_{\mathrm{relax}} + C_p + C_c + C_r}
       {1-(\varepsilon_p+\varepsilon_c+\varepsilon_r)} ,
\]
with the a-priori finiteness carried by the integrability fields (supplied in
the analytic setting by local-in-time regularity and continuation, which is
standard and not re-derived).  The theorem
\texttt{pancakeSectorEstimate\_of\_scaleLocalStrainBudget} derives the
pancake-sector estimate from the pointwise scale-local budget by time
integration.

\emph{Where the claimed proof fails.}  The proposal's high-low split charges
the whole non-pancake complement to the angularly coercive estimate.  By
Section~\ref{sec:aperture} that charge is \emph{equivalent} to the
scale-local budget $\gamma\le\nu\lambda_q$ --- which is exactly the bound the
proposal's own conditional section had identified as ``the remaining
burden'' before the claimed proof.  The claimed proof therefore consumes, at
its high-low split, the statement it set out to prove; the closure is
circular at precisely that point, and nothing else in it repairs the circle.

The remaining pin is a precise predicate, structurally verbatim:
\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]

Here \texttt{pancakeStretchSum} represents

\emph{self-consistent} Navier--Stokes strain (generated from the vorticity by
the Biot--Savart law), not for an externally imposed frozen strain --- via a
genuinely Navier--Stokes-specific mechanism (pressure/Hessian feedback,
multi-scale Biot--Savart self-depletion, or frequency-cascade accounting, in
the proposal's own taxonomy).  The verified plane-wave null structure is
evidence in the right direction; the aperture equivalence states exactly what
quantitative multi-scale estimate is still missing.

\emph{The dichotomy.}  Wavevector-cone confinement alone does not
deplete pairwise stretching.  In the exact lattice interaction model
(the amplitude-level Fourier symbol of $S(u)\omega$ with Biot--Savart

saturates the generic maximum at every aperture.  The distilled exact

the self-depletion mechanism is therefore the vorticity-direction
misalignment inside the pancake --- the Constantin--Fefferman coherence
quantity --- not the wavevector cone.

reference direction.  The bound aggregates over any finite family of
coherent sources (\texttt{sum\_sigmaSq\_coherent\_le}), matching the
measured coherent-aggregate gain of the second lab.

and its Cauchy--Schwarz energy form, where $p_k=P_ke_0$.  The exact theorem

\section{Opening the dynamical stage: direction rate and the tilting gap}
\label{sec:direction-dynamics}

The first dynamical audit separates two notions that should not be
conflated: rotation of the vorticity direction and rotation of the strain
eigenframe.  Both are relevant to a pancake configuration, but they obey
different equations.

For a nonzero vorticity vector written $\omega=r\xi$, with $|\xi|=1$, and a
material forcing $F$, define the transverse projection
\[
  P_{\xi}^{\perp}F=F-(\xi\cdot F)\xi,
  \qquad \dot\xi=r^{-1}P_{\xi}^{\perp}F.
\]
The module \texttt{DirectionEvolutionTilting} verifies this formula from a
differentiable factorization $\omega=r\xi$, including tangency
$\xi\cdot\dot\xi=0$.  If the pointwise vorticity equation is written as
$F=rS\xi+d$, where $S$ is the strain and $d$ is the diffusive remainder,
the exact split is
\[
  \dot\xi=P_{\xi}^{\perp}(S\xi)
       +r^{-1}P_{\xi}^{\perp}d.
\]
For two differentiable direction paths, the same module proves
\[
  \frac{d}{dt}|\xi_1-\xi_2|^2
   =2(\xi_1-\xi_2)\cdot(\dot\xi_1-\dot\xi_2),
\]
together with its Cauchy--Schwarz bound by
$2|\xi_1-\xi_2|(|\dot\xi_1|+|\dot\xi_2|)$.  Thus total direction turning is
an exact dynamical input to misalignment production; obtaining the
scale-critical time integral required by \texttt{MisalignmentStrainBudget}
remains analytic work.

The module \texttt{PancakeDyadicDirectionEvolution} makes this kinematics
apply to the same hard block used by the Fourier misalignment estimate.  For
the square-dyadic pancake selector $P_{N,F}$ at a fixed spatial point, it proves
coefficientwise that
\[
 \partial_t\,\Re(P_{N,F}\omega)
   =\Re(P_{N,F}\dot\omega).
\]
If this real block is $r\xi$, the exact direction equation is
\[
 \dot\xi=P_\xi^\perp(S\xi)+r^{-1}P_\xi^\perp R_{N,F},
 \qquad
R_{N,F}=\Re(P_{N,F}\dot\omega)-rS\xi.
\]
Thus the previously separate physical-direction and Fourier-block statements
now meet at a single explicit residual.  This is not yet a bound on that
residual: its Navier--Stokes decomposition and scale-critical time estimate
are the next dynamical obligations.
For any proposed coefficient field $G$ representing the stretching part, the
same module further proves the exact split
\[
 R_{N,F}=\Re P_{N,F}(\dot\omega-G)
   +\bigl(\Re P_{N,F}G-rS\xi\bigr).
\]
The first term is the projected equation remainder (transport and viscosity
for the vorticity equation); the second is the projection/variable-strain
defect once $G$ is identified with the coefficients of $S\omega$.  This
separates the two estimates needed for closure instead of charging both to an
undifferentiated direction-coherence premise.
The companion module \texttt{PancakeFourierStretchingEvolution} constructs
the canonical phase-suppressed coefficient field: every retained
source/receiver pair is collected in the
fiber $k+\ell=q$, and finite reconstruction of the resulting output
coefficients is proved exactly equal to the previously defined pair-indexed
complex stretching polynomial.  The module
\texttt{PancakePeriodicVorticityEquation} then fixes the unit-torus constants.
Writing $D=2\pi i$, it defines the physical Biot--Savart coefficient as
$-D^{-1}|k|^{-2}k\times\widehat\omega_k$ and proves exactly that physical
strain stretching is the negative of the suppressed symbol.  It also derives
the right-hand-side transport sign, includes the multiplier
$\nu D^2|q|^2$, and packages the finite coefficient equation.  Under that
equation the first block residual is proved to be precisely projected
transport plus viscosity; the second is the physical stretching
projection/variable-strain defect.  What remains is a scale-critical estimate
for those two concrete channels and a controlled passage from finite
convolutions to the full periodic solution, not a normalization ambiguity.
The companion module \texttt{PancakeBlockReality} proves that both the radial
shell and oriented squared-aperture predicate are invariant under frequency
negation.  Reindexing the finite Fourier sum by $k\mapsto-k$, using character
and coefficient conjugate symmetry, proves
$\overline{P_{N,F}\omega}=P_{N,F}\omega$ coordinatewise.  It follows that the
complex block has zero imaginary part and complexifying the real block used
above returns the full reconstruction exactly.  The same module then checks
symbol-by-symbol conjugate covariance of the physical unit-torus equation and
reindexes both convolution supports simultaneously.  For negation-closed
supports and real coefficient fields it proves
${\rm RHS}(-q)=\overline{{\rm RHS}(q)}$, so the finite dynamics is internally
consistent with the real block on which the direction law is stated.
The next exact cancellation is material transport.  If $B$ is the hard
pancake mode set, then
\[
 P_B[-(u\cdot\nabla)\omega]+(u\cdot\nabla)(P_B\omega)
   =[u\cdot\nabla,P_B]\omega
\]
is checked coefficientwise for finite supports.  For an output $q\in B$ the
surviving sum contains only receiver frequencies initially outside $B$
(incoming transfer); for $q\notin B$ it is minus the sum from receivers inside
$B$ (outgoing transfer).  Thus following the fluid removes bulk advection and
leaves an explicit frequency-boundary flux.
The physical stretching defect has the same algebra: the checked coefficient
of $P_B(S\omega)-S(P_B\omega)$ is incoming stretching when $q\in B$ and minus
outgoing stretching otherwise.  Combining the two shows that the entire
material nonlinear commutator consists only of transport-plus-stretching
interactions crossing the boundary of $B$; interactions staying within the
same hard block cancel from this residual exactly.
The module — now completed as
\texttt{PancakeMovingBlockEvolution} — verifies that this commutator is the
one actually produced by a moving observation point, rather than merely a
formal coefficient subtraction.  It differentiates both factors in
\[
  \sum_{k\in B}e^{2\pi i k\cdot X(t)}\widehat\omega_k(t).
\]
For the finite velocity-generated character rate, the character derivative
is proved exactly equal to positive advection of $P_B\omega$, including the
$2\pi i$ normalization.  Combining this product rule with the finite
vorticity equation gives the material hard-block direction law
\[
 \dot\xi=P_\xi^\perp(S\xi)
   +r^{-1}P_\xi^\perp\!\left(
       R_{\partial B,\nu}+R_{\rm proj}
     \right).
\]
Here $R_{\partial B,\nu}$ is the material nonlinear boundary-transfer field
plus projected viscosity, and $R_{\rm proj}$ is the discrepancy between
physical stretching of the projected block and $rS\xi$.  At coefficient
level, Lean proves that $R_{\partial B,\nu}$ contains only incoming/outgoing
frequency-boundary interactions and the viscous multiplier.  This is still
an exact finite-dimensional identity, not the scale-critical estimate needed
for global regularity.  In this theorem the observation point moves, but
the frame $F$ and the hard mode set are fixed in time.

The module \texttt{PancakeHardBoundaryObstruction} tests that residual at
the actual angular boundary.  For every integer $N\geq2$, set
\[
 k=(-1,0,0),\qquad \ell=(N+1,0,N^2),\qquad q=k+\ell=(N,0,N^2),
 \qquad w_k=w_\ell=(0,1,0).
\]
The receiver $\ell$ lies outside the identity-frame hard pancake sector,
while $q$ lies inside its complete square-dyadic shell-and-cone selector.
Both input amplitudes are transverse and have norm one.  The physical
transport symbol is exactly $-N^2(0,1,0)$, and the physical stretching
symbol is zero.  Lean checks that the corresponding incoming material
boundary coefficient has norm exactly $N^2$ and hence admits no
scale-independent instantaneous bilinear bound by the product of the two
input amplitude norms.  Adding the conjugate partner modes $-k,-\ell$
leaves the coefficient at $q$ unchanged, so the calculation also applies
to real trigonometric inputs.  This is a bilinear obstruction, not a
self-consistent solution counterexample or a refutation of a
time-integrated budget with viscosity.

The module \texttt{PancakeEvolvingFilter} supplies the derivative term needed
when the filter itself changes.  For differentiable weights $\gamma(t,k)$
on a fixed finite ambient support, it differentiates the filter, vorticity
coefficient, and moving Fourier character.  At a fixed time the exact
material residual coefficient is
\[
 R_q=\dot\gamma(q)\widehat\eta_q
   +\sum_{k+\ell=q}\bigl(\gamma(q)-\gamma(\ell)\bigr)
       \bigl(T_{k,\ell}+S_{k,\ell}\bigr)
   +\gamma(q)V_q,
\]
where $T,S,V$ are the physical transport, strain-stretching, and viscous
symbols with the same finite supports as the vorticity equation.  The
nonlinear formula specializes exactly to the previously constructed hard
commutator when $\gamma$ is the indicator of its block.  A changing hard
membership predicate need not be differentiable at a crossing; the product
rule does not supply that missing hypothesis.  An adaptive argument must
control the filter-motion term as well as the nonlinear boundary transfer.
Standard smooth Littlewood--Paley transport estimates provide useful
comparison machinery, but their velocity-gradient assumptions must also be
discharged: for example, the regularity-preserving estimates in
R.~Danchin, \emph{Estimates in Besov spaces for transport and
transport-diffusion equations with almost Lipschitz coefficients},
\emph{Rev.\ Mat.\ Iberoamericana} \textbf{21} (2005), 863--888,
Section~2, require time-integrated velocity-gradient control.

The module \texttt{PancakeSignedTransportEnergy} now retains signed
quadratic transfer before taking absolute values.  For a negation-closed
finite source set $K$ and conjugate-symmetric source coefficients, let
\[
 b_{q\ell}=\mathbf1_{q-\ell\in K}\,
   \ell\cdot\bigl(|q-\ell|^{-2}(q-\ell)\times
     \widehat\omega_{q-\ell}\bigr).
\]
This is proved to be the matrix of the existing physical negative-advection
convolution, with its defined zero-mode value, and
$b_{\ell q}=-\overline{b_{q\ell}}$.  Consequently
$F_{q\ell}=\operatorname{Re}\langle\widehat\eta_q,
b_{q\ell}\widehat\eta_\ell\rangle$ is antisymmetric.  On any finite
receiver/output mode set $M$, a real filter $\gamma$ satisfies the exact
transport-energy identity
\[
 2\sum_{q\in M}\gamma_q^2
   \operatorname{Re}\langle\widehat\eta_q,(T\widehat\eta)_q\rangle
 =\sum_{q,\ell\in M}(\gamma_q^2-\gamma_\ell^2)F_{q\ell}.
\]
Thus an exact quadratic frequency partition $\sum_i\gamma_i(q)^2=1$
cancels the total signed transport contribution.  Differentiating a
differentiable such partition also proves
$\sum_i2\gamma_i\dot\gamma_i=0$, rather than assuming the filter-rate
cancellation.  These are identities for exact frequency partitions, not a
claim that the previously constructed approximate spatial partitions already
satisfy them.  The full filtered coefficient-energy derivative is checked
with filter motion, transport, stretching, and viscous dissipation all retained.

Directional energy has an additional term.  For self-adjoint
complex-linear mode weights $A_q$, the same argument leaves
\[
 2\sum_{q\in M}\operatorname{Re}
   \langle A_q\widehat\eta_q,(T\widehat\eta)_q\rangle
 =\sum_{q,\ell\in M}\operatorname{Re}
   \langle\widehat\eta_q,b_{q\ell}(A_q-A_\ell)
       \widehat\eta_\ell\rangle.
\]
A common frozen weight cancels; different directional weights need not.
The module proves self-adjointness and idempotence of the route's actual
\texttt{misalignmentComponent} projector $Q_q$, including its degenerate
reference case, and specializes this identity to that projector.  Under
the physical finite vorticity coefficient equation it then proves
\begin{align*}
 \frac{d}{dt}\sum_{q\in M}|Q_q\widehat\eta_q|^2
 ={}&\sum_{q,\ell\in M}\operatorname{Re}
       \langle\widehat\eta_q,b_{q\ell}(Q_q-Q_\ell)
          \widehat\eta_\ell\rangle\\
 &+2\sum_{q\in M}\operatorname{Re}
       \langle Q_q\widehat\eta_q,(S\widehat\eta)_q\rangle
   -2\nu(2\pi)^2\sum_{q\in M}|q|^2|Q_q\widehat\eta_q|^2.
\end{align*}
Here the coherent reference is fixed, $S\widehat\eta$ is the already
constructed physical stretching convolution, and the energy normalization
is the one used by the finite Parseval theorem.  This is a differential
identity for the existing projector and coefficient equation, not a
constructed solution or a dynamical budget.  The remaining estimate must
control the projector-mismatch and stretching terms together with
dissipation; moving strain references, spatial localization, and the
scale-critical time sum still require additional work.  In particular,
scalar energy conservation does not by itself close the misalignment pin.

The next module, \texttt{PancakeFrequencyProjectorCommutator}, estimates
this specific projector-mismatch channel.  In a complex Hilbert space,
normalization and rank-one projection give the checked bound
\[
 \|Q_q-Q_\ell\|\le
   \frac{16|q-\ell|}{|q|\,|p(q)|},
 \qquad p(q)=P_qe_x,
\]
provided both frequencies and both coherent references are nonzero.
The Hilbert-space construction is identified exactly with the periodic
\texttt{misalignmentComponent}; it is not a surrogate projector.
The actual identity-frame pancake cone at $N\ge2$ gives
$|p(q)|\ge1/2$ and $N^2\le|q|<2N^2$.  A separate checked physical-symbol
estimate gives
\[
 |b_{q\ell}|\le
   \frac{6|\ell|}{|q-\ell|}\,|\widehat\omega_{q-\ell}|
\]
for a retained nonzero source frequency.  Thus the source-frequency factors
cancel.  For $q,\ell$ in the same pancake shell,
\[
 |b_{q\ell}(Q_q-Q_\ell)v|
   \le384\,|\widehat\omega_{q-\ell}|\,|v|.
\]
The more general theorem needs only $|\ell|\le2|q|$ and the two
coherent-reference norms at least $1/2$, so it also applies to buffered
frequencies satisfying those conditions.  The coincident-frequency and
absent-source cases are included explicitly.

Injectivity of the frequency shifts bounds both row and column sums of the
source convolution matrix.  A checked finite Schur estimate then yields
\[
 \left|\sum_{q,\ell\in M}\operatorname{Re}
   \langle\widehat\eta_q,b_{q\ell}(Q_q-Q_\ell)
     \widehat\eta_\ell\rangle\right|
 \le384\left(\sum_{k\in K}|\widehat\omega_k|\right)
          \left(\sum_{q\in M}|\widehat\eta_q|^2\right),
\]
with no additional mode-count or frequency factor.  It plugs directly into
the signed misalignment-energy derivative above.  This removes a derivative
loss from the same-shell projector-mismatch channel, but it does not prove
the time budget: the source Fourier $\ell^1$ vorticity factor still needs
dynamical control, and the right-hand energy is full receiver energy, not
misalignment energy alone.  Nor does this estimate control a hard-selector
crossing, transfers from other sectors, or moving strain-frame terms.
Uniform physical-frame localization and the scale-critical time sum remain
required before this estimate can discharge any part of
\texttt{MisalignmentStrainBudget} for actual solution data.

\paragraph{Projected-amplitude absorption and the remaining source cutoff.}
The modules \texttt{PancakeViscousMisalignmentAbsorption} and
\texttt{PancakeSameSectorDissipation} strengthen this estimate by retaining
the misaligned output amplitude.  Write
\[
 E_Q=\sum_{q\in M}|Q_q\widehat\eta_q|^2,\qquad
 E=\sum_{q\in M}|\widehat\eta_q|^2,\qquad
 D_Q=\sum_{q\in M}|q|^2|Q_q\widehat\eta_q|^2,\qquad
 A_K=\sum_{k\in K}|\widehat\omega_k|.
\]
For the same finite source set $K$, closed under negation, and
conjugate-symmetric source coefficients, transport of $Q\eta$ has zero
total energy rate.  Subtracting that identity gives the exact expression
\[
 T_Q=2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{q,\ell\in M}
   \langle Q_q\widehat\eta_q,
       b_{q\ell}(Q_q-Q_\ell)\widehat\eta_\ell\rangle.
\]
A weighted finite Schur estimate, with an arbitrary parameter $r>0$, yields
\[
 |T_Q|\le384A_K(rE_Q+E/r).
\]
In particular, for every $\delta>0$,
\[
 |T_Q|\le\delta E_Q+\frac{(384A_K)^2}{\delta}E.
\]
The zero-source case is proved separately, so this optimization does not
divide by an amplitude assumed positive.

Stretching can be treated with the same projected amplitude.  The exact
complex Lagrange identity for a real lattice frequency is
\[
 |k\mathbin\times w|^2=|k|^2|w|^2-|k\mathbin\cdot w|^2.
\]
It gives $|k|\,|\widetilde u_k|\le|w|$ for the phase-suppressed
Biot--Savart symbol $\widetilde u_k$.  Restoring the physical Fourier
phases and estimating both terms of the symmetric strain gives the
checked, non-sharp bound
\[
 |S_k(w)v|\le3|w|\,|v|,
 \qquad |S_Q|\le3A_K(rE_Q+E/r).
\]
This includes the zero source frequency.  The second inequality follows
by reindexing the actual physical stretching convolution by its receiver
frequency and applying the same row/column estimate.

Transport and stretching are therefore absorbed \emph{together}, spending
the physical diffusion only once.  Since $N^4E_Q\le D_Q$, their combined
constant $387=384+3$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-same-sector-absorption}
 E_Q'+\nu(2\pi)^2D_Q\le C_{K,N},
 \qquad C_{K,N}:=\frac{(387A_K)^2}{\nu(2\pi)^2N^4}E.
\end{equation}
Lean identifies the derivative here under the previously defined physical
finite coefficient equation.  With the displayed rates explicitly
interval-integrable on $[0,T]$, it also proves
\[
 E_Q(T)+\nu(2\pi)^2\int_0^T D_Q(t)\,dt
 \le E_Q(0)+\int_0^T C_{K,N}(t)\,dt.
\]
Neither statement constructs a solution of that coefficient equation or
asserts that a sector of a full Navier--Stokes solution is an autonomous
subsystem.  Incoming receiver-sector interactions and moving-projector
terms must be restored for that application.

The remaining amplitude cost has a precise physical energy interpretation.
Let
\[
 U_K=\sum_{k\in K}|u_k|^2,\qquad
 V_M=\sum_{q\in M}|v_q|^2,\qquad
 H_K=\sum_{k\in K}|k|^2,
\]
where $u_k$ and $v_q$ are the physical unit-torus Biot--Savart velocities
of $\widehat\omega_k$ and $\widehat\eta_q$.  These sums use the squared
Parseval norm, without the conventional kinetic-energy factor $1/2$.
For nonzero transverse source modes, the checked exact identity
$(2\pi)^2|k|^2|u_k|^2=|\widehat\omega_k|^2$ and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 A_K^2\le(2\pi)^2H_KU_K.
\]
For transverse receiver modes in the actual pancake shell, its upper
frequency bound gives $E\le4(2\pi)^2N^4V_M$.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-same-sector-kinetic-cost}
 C_{K,N}\le\frac{4\cdot387^2(2\pi)^2}{\nu}\,H_KU_KV_M.
\end{equation}
For a finite family of disjoint receiver sectors of a single coefficient
field, with the same source set $K$, Lean sums this estimate with $V_M$
replaced by the velocity energy on the union.  There is no extra
receiver-sector count or receiver-frequency factor.

The explicit factor $H_K$ is essential to the present bound.  A fixed
finite source set permits low-source energy accounting, but allowing its
cutoff to grow is not free.  Source-cutoff-uniform control, incoming
cross-sector interactions, evolving strain frames, physical localization,
and the conversion to the scale-critical misalignment strain budget remain
unproved.  Equations~\eqref{eq:ns-same-sector-absorption}
and~\eqref{eq:ns-same-sector-kinetic-cost} do not establish the
arbitrary-data continuation theorem.

\paragraph{Incoming low sources: full receivers and a fixed-cutoff time bound.}
\texttt{PancakeIncomingLowSourceEnergy} removes the same-receiver-sector
restriction for the low-source channel.  Let $S$ be the finite ambient
source support, $R$ the full finite receiver support, and $M$ the selected
output modes.  They need not coincide.  For a fixed cutoff $L$, set
\[
 K_L=\{k\in S:|k|\le L\},\quad K_L^+=S\setminus K_L,
 \quad B_L=\sum_{k\in K_L}|u_k|,\quad
 E_R=\sum_{\ell\in R}|\widehat\eta_\ell|^2.
\]
Assume $|q|\ge L$ for $q\in M$.  For an actual pancake output shell this
follows from $L\le N^2$, with no restriction on receiver modes.

The physical Biot--Savart velocity satisfies
\[
 \ell\cdot u_{q-\ell}=q\cdot u_{q-\ell}.
\]
Thus the transport derivative can be estimated at the output frequency,
where diffusion is available.  Lean checks
\[
 |T_{q\ell}|\le3(2\pi)|q|\,|u_{q-\ell}|\,|\widehat\eta_\ell|,
 \qquad
 |S_k\widehat\eta_\ell|
   \le3(2\pi)|k|\,|u_k|\,|\widehat\eta_\ell|.
\]
For low sources, $|k|\le L\le|q|$, so both terms have the same
output-frequency bound.  Pairing with $Q_q\widehat\eta_q$ and using a
rectangular Schur estimate over $M\times R$ gives
\[
 |I_L|\le6(2\pi)B_L(rD_Q+E_R/r),\qquad r>0,
\]
where $I_L$ is the actual low-source contribution to the derivative of
$E_Q$.  Optimizing the parameter proves
\[
 |I_L|\le\nu(2\pi)^2D_Q+C_L,
 \qquad C_L=\frac{36B_L^2}{\nu}E_R.
\]
Every incoming receiver mode remains in $R$, including modes outside the
hard pancake boundary.  No scalar cutoff smoothing or cancellation between
an inside-only set of receivers is assumed.

The source split is also an exact identity for the physical nonlinear
coefficient and its energy rate.  Under the full finite coefficient
equation, the checked differential inequality is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-incoming-low-source}
 E_Q'+\nu(2\pi)^2D_Q\le I_L^+ + C_L,
\end{equation}
where $I_L^+$ is the explicitly defined physical contribution from
$K_L^+\times R$, not an abstract error variable.  Lean integrates this
inequality with all displayed rates interval-integrable.  The high-source
term is retained, not declared controlled.

There is now a fixed-cutoff, horizon-uniform bound for the low-source
\emph{cost}, conditional on physical energy estimates.  Writing
$U_L(t)=\sum_{k\in K_L}|u_k(t)|^2$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$B_L^2\le\#K_L\,U_L$.  For a natural cutoff $L$, an explicitly constructed
integer cube gives $\#K_L\le(2L+1)^3$, independently of $S$.  If
\[
 U_L(t)\le U\quad(0\le t\le T),\qquad
 2\nu\int_0^T E_R(t)\,dt\le U,\qquad U\ge0,
\]
then, with the stated integrability assumptions, Lean proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-incoming-fixed-cutoff-budget}
 \int_0^T C_L(t)\,dt\le
   \frac{18(2L+1)^3U^2}{\nu^2}.
\end{equation}
This bound has no time-horizon, output-frequency, output-mode-count, or
ambient-support factor.  It does depend on the \emph{fixed} source cutoff
$L$; choosing $L$ to grow with output frequency does not preserve a uniform
constant.  The kinetic and enstrophy bounds above remain explicit inputs;
this module does not derive them from an arbitrary-data solution
construction.  It also leaves $I_L^+$, moving-frame terms, physical
localization, the strain-budget conversion, and continuation open.  The
previous instantaneous hard-boundary obstruction is consistent with this
estimate: its output-frequency growth is paid for by diffusion rather than
denied.

\paragraph{Exchange-symmetric refinement: the complete low-touch budget.}
The modules \texttt{PancakeExchangeSymmetricCurl} and
\texttt{PancakeLowTouchEnergy} now impose physical self-consistency:
the receiver is the same transverse vorticity field as the source, and
the finite support $S$ excludes the zero mode.  The existing physical
Biot--Savart formula then satisfies
$\widehat\omega_k=(2\pi i)k\times u_k$.  With $q=k+\ell$, Lean checks
\[
 T(k,\ell)+S(k,\ell)
  =(2\pi i)q\times(u_k\times\widehat\omega_\ell)
    -\tfrac12\widehat\omega_k\times\widehat\omega_\ell.
\]
The last term sums to zero on every finite ordered pair family closed
under exchanging $k$ and $\ell$.  The curl-recovery condition is essential:
transversality alone does not imply it at zero frequency, as an explicit
checked counterexample records.

Write $H_L=S\setminus K_L$.  The disjoint physical partition is
\[
 F_{S,S}=F_L^{\mathrm{touch}}+F_L^{HH},\qquad
 F_L^{\mathrm{touch}}=F_{K_L,S}+F_{H_L,K_L},\qquad
 F_L^{HH}=F_{H_L,H_L}.
\]
Both pair families are exchange-symmetric.  Thus
\[
 F_L^{\mathrm{touch}}
   =\nabla\times(u_{K_L}\times\omega+u_{H_L}\times\omega_{K_L}),
 \qquad
 F_L^{HH}=\nabla\times(u_{H_L}\times\omega_{H_L}).
\]
This repartitions the \emph{full} nonlinearity.  The old high-source
term alone is $I_L^+=I_{H_L,K_L}+I_{H_L,H_L}$; it is not the sum of
the complete low-touch and high-high contributions.

Let $m_L=\#K_L$, $U(t)=\sum_{k\in S}|u_k(t)|^2$, and
$\Omega(t)=\sum_{k\in S}|\widehat\omega_k(t)|^2$.
The complex Lagrange identity gives the sharp Euclidean estimate
$|a\times b|\le |a|\,|b|$.  A rectangular Schur estimate for the
output-curl convolution, applied once in each input orientation, proves
for every $\theta>0$ that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-low-touch-absorption}
 |I_L^{\mathrm{touch}}|
 \le \theta\nu(2\pi)^2D_Q+C_L^{\mathrm{touch}},\qquad
 C_L^{\mathrm{touch}}=\frac{4m_L}{\theta\nu}U\Omega.
\end{equation}
No lower bound on output frequency, same-sector receiver confinement,
or moving-frame cancellation is used.  The output projector remains the
actual fixed misalignment projector.

If $U(t)\le U_0$, $U_0\ge0$, and
$2\nu\int_0^T\Omega(t)\,dt\le U_0$, with the displayed rates
interval-integrable, Lean proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-low-touch-time-cost}
 \int_0^T C_L^{\mathrm{touch}}(t)\,dt
 \le\frac{2m_LU_0^2}{\theta\nu^2}
 \le\frac{2(2L+1)^3U_0^2}{\theta\nu^2}
 \quad(L\in\mathbb N).
\end{equation}
It also integrates the absolute flux in
\eqref{eq:ns-low-touch-absorption}, retaining its viscous payment.
Under the full self-consistent finite vorticity equation, the resulting
checked evolution estimate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-high-high-remainder}
 E_Q(T)+(2-\theta)\nu(2\pi)^2\int_0^T D_Q(t)\,dt
 \le E_Q(0)+\int_0^T I_L^{HH}(t)\,dt
      +\frac{2m_LU_0^2}{\theta\nu^2}.
\end{equation}
Choosing $0<\theta<2$ retains positive output dissipation.  The cost
is uniform in time horizon, output frequency and output count, and in the
ambient retained support, at a \emph{fixed} low cutoff and uniform $U_0$.
The kinetic/enstrophy estimates, integrability, and solution realization
remain explicit hypotheses.  The high-high dynamics, moving-frame terms,
field-level strain transfer, and arbitrary-data continuation remain open.

\paragraph{Physical velocity realization of the energy and vorticity inputs.}
\texttt{PancakeGalerkinKineticEnergy} and
\texttt{PancakeGalerkinVorticityBridge} now discharge the independent
kinetic/enstrophy, vorticity-evolution, and integrability inputs above for
coefficient paths satisfying the physical finite velocity equation.
Let $S$ be a finite nonzero mode set closed under negation and let
$u_k(t)$ be conjugate-symmetric and transverse, $k\cdot u_k(t)=0$,
on $[0,T]$.  Write $c=2\pi i$ and define
\[
 \widehat\omega_k=c\,k\times u_k,\qquad
 V_q(u)=-c P_q\!\sum_{\substack{k,\ell\in S\\ k+\ell=q}}
              (\ell\cdot u_k)u_\ell
             -\nu(2\pi)^2|q|^2u_q.
\]
The source velocity recovered from $\widehat\omega$ is exactly $u$ on
$S$.  This identifies the implemented RHS with $V$, including the
unit-torus normalization.  For every path with $\dot u_q=V_q(u)$ on
the retained modes, pressure does no kinetic work and signed transport
cancels.  Lean proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-galerkin-kinetic-identity}
 U(T)+2\nu\int_0^T\Omega(t)\,dt=U(0).
\end{equation}
The coefficient equation itself implies continuity of the finite enstrophy
sum and hence its time integrability; that integrability is not a premise.

For the curl bridge, exchanging the two inputs cancels the gradient defect
in the identity between $u\times\operatorname{curl}u$ and negative velocity
convection.  Pressure has zero curl and viscosity commutes with curl.
Consequently the exact implemented coefficient identity is
\[
 c\,q\times V_q(u)
   =F_{S,S}(\widehat\omega,\widehat\omega)_q
      -\nu(2\pi)^2|q|^2\widehat\omega_q.
\]
The pathwise vorticity ODE follows by differentiating the finite linear curl
map.  Continuity of the fixed misalignment projector and finite bilinear
convolution supplies all remaining rate-integrability facts.

Thus \texttt{integrated\_highHigh\_remainder\_of\_velocityRHS} proves
\eqref{eq:ns-high-high-remainder} for every output subset $M\subseteq S$
using $U_0=U(0)$, without independent kinetic, enstrophy, vorticity-ODE,
or rate-integrability assumptions.  Its natural-cutoff version replaces
$m_L$ by $(2L+1)^3$.  The output dynamics still use the full retained
source and receiver sets; the selected output sector is not declared
autonomous.

These results are a priori theorems about paths satisfying the finite ODE.
They do not construct such paths, prove preservation of the real transverse
subspace from initial data, or pass to an infinite-dimensional solution.
They also do not replace the fixed misalignment projector by the moving
strain-eigenframe projector.  High-high control, that moving-frame passage,
the scale-critical strain budget, and BKM continuation remain open.

\paragraph{Moving localization: positive energy and material projector tangency.}
\texttt{PancakeLocalizedQuadraticEnergy} implements the noncommuting
localized energy
\[
 A=C^*PC,\qquad \mathcal E_A=\langle\omega,A\omega\rangle
       =\|PC\omega\|_2^2
\]
for an orthogonal projector $P$.  No commutation of $C$ and $P$ is
required.  Its coherent/complementary partner is $C^*(I-P)C$, and the
two operators sum exactly to $C^*C$.  The unsandwiched expression
$C^2P$ is not used as though it were self-adjoint.

For $\dot\omega=-B\omega+s+\nu\Delta\omega$, the checked identity is
\begin{align*}
 \dot{\mathcal E}_A
  ={}&\langle\omega,\dot A\omega\rangle
      +\langle\omega,[B,A]\omega\rangle
      +2\langle A\omega,s\rangle\\
    &+\nu\langle\omega,(A\Delta+\Delta A)\omega\rangle.
\end{align*}
The abstract statement requires skew symmetry of $B$ and symmetry of
$\Delta$ on an explicit common core containing $\omega$ and $A\omega$;
neither unbounded operator is declared bounded on ambient $L^2$.
Nor is advection treated as an endomorphism of divergence-free fields.
Finite complete operator partitions cancel the summed motion and signed
transport-transfer terms, not the corresponding individual sector terms.

Writing $z=C\omega$, the sum of the motion and transport terms is exactly
\[
 2\langle Pz,(\dot C+[B,C])\omega\rangle
   +\langle z,(\dot P+[B,P])z\rangle.
\]
The common-core hypotheses now include $z$ and $Pz$.
The diffusion anticommutator remains explicit; positivity of $A$ alone
does not turn it into a commuting negative dissipation term.

\texttt{PancakeMaterialProjectorTangent} sharpens the second term.
For a differentiable path of orthogonal projectors and bounded
skew-adjoint $B$, differentiation of $P^2=P$ proves that
\[
 F:=\dot P+[B,P],\qquad F^*=F,\qquad FP+PF=F,
 \qquad PFP=0.
\]
Consequently Lean checks the exact off-diagonal identity and bounds
\begin{align*}
 \langle z,Fz\rangle&=2\langle Pz,F(I-P)z\rangle,\\
 |\langle z,Fz\rangle|&\le2\|F\|\,\|Pz\|_2\,\|(I-P)z\|_2\\
 &\le\rho\|(I-P)z\|_2^2
        +\frac{\|F\|^2}{\rho}\|Pz\|_2^2\qquad(\rho>0).
\end{align*}
For the complementary energy the projector-motion term has the opposite
sign, so the same absolute estimate applies.  This retains a misalignment
factor; it does not assert that the squared material-rate cost is integrable.
Vanishing of the diagonal block is instantaneous and does not imply
persistence of a pure coherent or misaligned sector.

\texttt{PancakeFiniteLocalizedEvolution} constructs the ambient retained
Hilbert space $\mathbb C^{S\times\{0,1,2\}}$, its restriction/extension
maps, and the actual advection and Laplacian matrices.  Their real inner
product agrees with the previous Fourier Hermitian sum, and their squared
norm with the previous coefficient energy.  Advection is exactly the
negative of the physical RHS transport; its skew-adjointness follows from
source reality.  The Laplacian is the physical $(2\pi i)^2|q|^2$ multiplier.
The moving-energy identity follows directly from a physical velocity path
by the preceding curl bridge, with no separate abstract vorticity ODE or
transport identity assumed.  The off-diagonal material-rate estimate is
also specialized to this actual finite advection operator.

The localizer and projector paths remain explicit inputs.  Constructing
them from the actual evolving strain, bounding their material rates and
diffusion errors uniformly in truncation and dyadic scale, controlling the
high-high channel, and reaching the pointwise strain/BKM budget remain
open.  The bounded-operator tangent theorem does not by itself extend to
unbounded advection on the infinite-dimensional ambient space.

The pressure Hessian enters through the strain-eigenframe equation instead.
Differentiating $Se_i=\lambda_i e_i$ and pairing with $e_j$, $i\ne j$, gives
\[
  \langle e_j,\dot e_i\rangle
   =\frac{\langle e_j,\dot S e_i\rangle}{\lambda_i-\lambda_j}.
\]
The anisotropic pressure Hessian contributes to the numerator.  The checked
theorem \texttt{no\_uniformGapFreePressureTiltingBound} records the necessary
denominator: no nonnegative constant bounds the angular rate from a bounded
off-diagonal torque independently of the strain eigenvalue gap.  Unit torque
with gap $1/(C+1)$ already defeats a proposed constant $C$.

This rules out a gap-free angular-rate envelope based only on a raw
pressure-Hessian norm.  A viable
dynamical continuation must instead control a gap-weighted pressure torque,
or prove cancellation/residence-time control over near-degenerate intervals,
and then transfer that control to the Littlewood--Paley misalignment share.

\paragraph{An intrinsic defect identity through collisions.}
The new \texttt{PancakeTopEigenvalueDerivative},
\texttt{PancakeSpectralDefectEvolution}, and
\texttt{PancakeSpectralDefectBudget} modules work directly with
\[
 \lambda=\lambda_{\max}(S),\qquad
 D=\lambda\|w\|^2-\langle w,Sw\rangle\ge0,\qquad
 z=(\lambda I-S)w.
\]
These are still strain-alignment quantities: in the constructed ordered
eigenbasis,
\begin{align*}
 D&=(\lambda_0-\lambda_1)|w_1|^2
       +(\lambda_0-\lambda_2)|w_2|^2,\\
 \|z\|^2&=(\lambda_0-\lambda_1)^2|w_1|^2
       +(\lambda_0-\lambda_2)^2|w_2|^2.
\end{align*}
For real Hilbert-space paths obeying
\[
 \dot w=Sw+f,\qquad \dot S=-S^2+R,
\]
and a unit top eigenvector $e$ at the time in question, the exact checked
cancellation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-spectral-defect-rate}
 \dot D=-\|z\|^2+\mathcal A_R(e,w)+2\langle z,f\rangle,
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_R(e,w)=\langle e,Re\rangle\|w\|^2-\langle w,Rw\rangle.
\end{equation}
In particular no derivative of a chosen eigenvector appears. On a simple
top-eigenvalue branch, the derivative
$\dot\lambda=\langle e,\dot S e\rangle$ is proved using the continuous
spectral projector applied to one fixed reference eigenvector, not an
assumed differentiable eigenframe.

The time-integrated statement requires no simple-gap hypothesis at all.
The top eigenvalue is globally 1-Lipschitz as a function of the symmetric
operator. Along a $C^1$ strain path on a compact interval it is absolutely
continuous. At every time where its derivative exists, the Rayleigh
variational minimum proves the same derivative formula for every unit top
eigenvector, including at repeated eigenvalues. The resulting almost-everywhere
identity and absolute continuity of $D$ yield
\[
 D(b)+\int_a^b\|z\|^2
 =D(a)+\int_a^b\mathcal A_R(e,w)+2\int_a^b\langle z,f\rangle.
\]
For $a\le b$ and $\theta>0$, Young's inequality gives
\[
 D(b)+(1-\theta)\int_a^b\|z\|^2
 \le D(a)+\int_a^b\mathcal A_R(e,w)
       +\theta^{-1}\int_a^b\|f\|^2.
\]
The formal hypotheses are $C^1$ strain and vector paths, continuous $f$,
and the two displayed differential equations on the compact interval.
Integrability of the scalar anisotropy is derived from the almost-everywhere
identity, not assumed for a potentially discontinuous eigenvector selection.
No bound on that integral from the initial data is obtained.

Isotropic forcing cancels exactly:
$\mathcal A_{R+\alpha I}=\mathcal A_R$. For skew-adjoint spin $W$,
\[
 \mathcal A_{-W^2}(e,w)
 =\|We\|^2\|w\|^2-\|Ww\|^2.
\]
Thus when $Ww=0$, its contribution is nonnegative and cannot be discarded
as damping. For a high-frequency receiver and low-frequency spin,
$W_{<L}w$ need not vanish; the full two-term identity must be retained.
In the full strain equation, $R$ includes $-W^2$, the negative
pressure Hessian, and viscous strain forcing. A filtered equation also has
the actual subgrid forcing; see the gradient equation (2.4b) in
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.04300v1}{Tom--Carbone--Bragg}.
Their coefficient-level and real-material-path identifications are now
checked below; a uniform estimate of their signed combination remains
required. Moreover each
corresponding squared-gap weight vanishes at a collision: this budget is not an unweighted
line-misalignment estimate or a completed Littlewood--Paley/BKM bridge.

\paragraph{Physical pressure and the filtered strain equation.}
The \texttt{PancakeFourierPressureStrain} module constructs pressure from
the same finite convection convolution as the physical velocity RHS.
Writing $c=2\pi i$, $A_{q,ij}=c q_j u_{q,i}$ and
$C_q=\sum_{k+\ell=q}(\ell\cdot u_k)u_\ell$, it sets
\[
 p_q=-\frac{q\cdot C_q}{|q|^2},\qquad p_0=0,\qquad
 H_{q,ij}=c^2q_iq_jp_q.
\]
The Poisson equation, pressure-gradient identity, and exact material
gradient coefficient equation are proved from these definitions and the
physical velocity RHS. Strain and spin are the transpose-symmetric and
transpose-antisymmetric parts of $A_q$; individual complex Fourier
coefficients are not incorrectly declared Hermitian.

For a static multiplier $\chi$, \texttt{PancakeFilteredStrainDynamics}
retains the explicit stress-divergence coefficient
\[
 T^\chi_q=\sum_{k+\ell=q}
  \bigl[\chi(q)-\chi(k)\chi(\ell)\bigr](\ell\cdot u_k)u_\ell.
\]
Thus $\chi C(u,u)=C(\chi u,\chi u)+T^\chi$ exactly. Differentiating
convection and symmetrizing after exchanging the ordered Fourier pair
gives the coefficient form of
\[
 \partial_t S_\chi+(u_\chi\cdot\nabla)S_\chi
 =-S_\chi^2-W_\chi^2-\chi H
   +\nu\Delta S_\chi+\operatorname{sym}\nabla(-cT^\chi).
\]
Products and advection here mean the explicitly defined finite Fourier
convolutions. Their coefficient time derivatives are derived from the
original retained-mode velocity ODE. When $\chi$ vanishes outside the
retained modes, the derivative theorem holds at every output frequency
without assuming an equation for discarded modes. Reconstruction along
real material paths is supplied by the next bridge below. A moving multiplier would
also require its time-derivative term, which is not suppressed by this
static-filter theorem.

\paragraph{Low-output pressure is paid by kinetic energy.}
The \texttt{PancakeLowPressureBudget} module uses incompressibility to
move both numerator derivatives to the output mode:
\[
 q\cdot C_q=\sum_{k+\ell=q}(q\cdot u_k)(q\cdot u_\ell),\qquad
 |p_q|\le U,\qquad U=\sum_k|u_k|^2.
\]
The constant in the last bound is exactly one, independent of the
source support and all input frequencies. Each convolution fiber uses
each source and receiver at most once, so Cauchy--Schwarz incurs no
cardinality factor. For a fixed finite pressure output set $K$, define
\[
 C_K=(2\pi)^2\sum_{q\in K}|q|^2,\qquad
 H_K(x)=\sum_{q\in K}e^{2\pi i q\cdot x}H_q.
\]
The constructed Hessian operators satisfy
$\|H_K(x)\|\le\sum_{q\in K}\|H_q\|\le C_K U$, uniformly in space.
Let $\Omega=\sum_k|\omega_k|^2$. The physical energy identity yields
\[
 \int_0^T 2\!\sum_{q\in K}\|H_q(t)\|\,\Omega(t)\,dt
 \le \frac{C_K U(0)^2}{\nu}.
\]
The \texttt{PancakePressureAnisotropyBudget} module connects this envelope
to the actual spatial quadratic term. For unit directions $e(t,x)$ and
the reconstructed vorticity, finite Parseval gives
\[
 \int_0^T\!\int_{\mathbb T^3}
  |\mathcal A_{H_K(t,x)}(e(t,x),\omega(t,x))|\,dx\,dt
 \le \frac{C_K U(0)^2}{\nu}.
\]
The bound is proved on complex amplitudes regarded as a real Hilbert
space, so real fields are included. Spatial integrability follows for
measurable unit directions. The time theorem explicitly assumes
measurability of the spatial integral and derives its time integrability
and bound; it does not assume a pressure or enstrophy budget.
All these dynamic statements assume a retained-mode velocity solution
with preserved reality and incompressibility; existence and preservation
are not proved by the estimates themselves.

This pays all pressure inputs generating a fixed low output, including
high--high inputs. It does not pay high pressure outputs: $C_K$ grows
with the output cutoff. The signed high-pressure/spin/subgrid combination,
the remaining high--high transfer, and the scale-uniform BKM closure
are still open.

\paragraph{The defect identity for actual material fields.}
The \texttt{PancakeFourierMaterialPaths} and
\texttt{PancakeSpatialStrainEvolution} modules now construct that bridge.
For a differentiable real coordinate path $X(t)$, the Fourier character
rate is proved from the exponential formula:
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}e^{2\pi i q\cdot X(t)}
 =2\pi i\,(q\cdot\dot X(t))e^{2\pi i q\cdot X(t)}.
\]
The real trajectory equation $\dot X=u_\chi(t,X)$ therefore supplies the
fluid-generated phase rate; no independent phase-derivative hypothesis
is needed. The reconstructed Fourier gradient is also proved to give
the directional derivative of the real velocity field along every such
path. Its real operator splits into self-adjoint strain and skew-adjoint
spin. Conjugate symmetry justifies multiplication of reconstructed real
matrices; multiplicativity of real part is not presumed.

The output set is constructed as $Q=S\cup(S+S)$ for retained modes $S$.
Thus reconstruction includes every linear and quadratic output.
Finite convolution becomes the full matrix product, and positive
advection cancels the trajectory's character-motion contribution exactly.
The resulting real material equation is
\[
 \dot S_\chi=-S_\chi^2+R_\chi,\qquad
 R_\chi=-W_\chi^2+F_\chi,
\]
where $F_\chi$ is the reconstructed pressure/viscous/subgrid expression
above, not an assumed forcing operator. The checked spin contribution
retains both terms:
\[
 \mathcal A_{R_\chi}(e,w)
 =\|W_\chi e\|^2\|w\|^2-\|W_\chi w\|^2
   +\mathcal A_{F_\chi}(e,w).
\]

The \texttt{PancakePhysicalSpectralDefect} module constructs
$w=\omega(t,X(t))$ from the retained curl coefficients and its exact
material rate from the physical vorticity RHS and the derived character
rate. Subtracting $S_\chi w$ defines the explicit mismatch $f_\chi$;
there is no time derivative hidden in that definition, and no smallness
claim is attached to it. Both evolution hypotheses of
\eqref{eq:ns-spectral-defect-rate} are thereby discharged from the
original velocity and real trajectory equations.

Finally, \texttt{PancakePhysicalDefectODEBudget} derives $C^1$ regularity
of the retained velocity coefficients and real path from those ODEs.
Finite reconstruction transfers it to strain and vorticity. The
largest eigenvalue is absolutely continuous across collisions, and
the actual vector forcing is continuous because it equals

the within-interval derivative and handles the singleton interval separately.

 D(b)+\int_a^b\|z\|^2
 =D(a)+\int_a^b\mathcal A_{R_\chi}(e,w)
       +2\int_a^b\langle z,f_\chi\rangle.
\]
Integrability of the signed anisotropy is a conclusion. No separate
strain ODE, phase ODE, $C^1$ field assumption, positive eigenvalue gap,

\paragraph{Diffusion is not free damping of the intrinsic defect.}
The signed viscous term cannot be discarded on the strength of
incompressibility alone. The modules \texttt{PancakePlanarSpectralDefect},
\texttt{PancakePlanarDiffusion}, and
\texttt{PancakePeriodicDiffusionWitness} give an explicit pointwise test.
For a planar incompressible velocity, write
\[
 S=\begin{pmatrix}a&b&0\\b&-a&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \omega=(0,0,w).
\]
The constructed top eigenvalue is identified with
$\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ on the branch $a>0$, and the actual defect is
$D=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\,w^2$. At a normalized point $a=w=1$, $b=0$,
the checked second-derivative formula in one spatial direction is
\[
 D''=a''+(b')^2+4a'w'+2(w')^2+2w''.
\]
The mixed term has no fixed sign. More generally, at $b=0$, $a>0$,
the corresponding directional heat-chain-rule production has the
completed-square algebra
\begin{align*}
 Q&=-\frac{w^2(b')^2}{a}-4wa'w'-2a(w')^2\\
  &=\frac{2w^2(a')^2}{a}-\frac{w^2(b')^2}{a}
       -2a\left(w'+\frac{wa'}a\right)^2
   \le \frac{2w^2(a')^2}{a}-\frac{w^2(b')^2}{a}.
\end{align*}
The algebraic equality and bound are checked; they expose a gradient cost
rather than supplying a scale-uniform estimate of it.

On the $2\pi$-periodic torus, take the smooth velocity

                +8\cos x-9\sin x\sin y,\qquad u_3=0.
\end{align*}

\end{gather*}
For the actual constructed spectral defect, the checked values are

\paragraph{Two-shear obstruction to the integrated sign.}
A simpler constructed heat evolution shows that spatial integration does
not restore free monotonicity. Its initial velocity is

 P(F,G)=\tfrac12\{D(F,G)+D(-F,G)\}
 =\begin{cases}
 4G(G^2-F^2),&F\le G,\\
 4F(F^2-G^2),&G\le F.
 \end{cases}
\]
Its fast logarithmic amplitude rate away from $F=G$ is
\[
 A(F,G)=\begin{cases}
 -8GF^2,&F<G,\\
 12F^3-4FG^2,&G<F.
 \end{cases}
\]
Assigning zero at the cusp preserves the checked bound
$A\le-8GF^2+16F^3$. The actual two heat rates
$F'=-\nu N^2F$, $G'=-\nu G$ give
$P'=-\nu[3P+(N^2-1)A]$ away from the crossing; this derivative identity
is checked. The negative leading amplitude term explains why removing a
small fast shear can increase this alignment defect.

For $\varepsilon=1/16$, $N=64$, $\nu=1$, numerical periodic quadrature
at resolutions $256$, $512$, $1024$, and $2048$ gives a normalized mean

\emph{diagnostic, not certified quadrature or a formal integral theorem}.
The script \texttt{ns\_two\_shear\_diffusion\_sanity.py} reproduces the

 M(p,q)=\mathop{\mathrm{mean}}_{x,y}

 M(\alpha\varepsilon,1)-M(\varepsilon,1)

This uses cubic homogeneity, $M(1/16,1)\le4$, and elementary exponential

\paragraph{A regularized paid gradient bound.}
The module \texttt{PancakeRegularizedDiffusion} constructs

 Q_j&=-2\langle v_j,K_\delta v_j\rangle
                -4\langle v_j,G_j\omega\rangle,\\
 Q_j+\delta|v_j|^2
    &\le \frac{4}{\delta}\|G_j\|^2|\omega|^2
     \le \frac{16}{\delta}\|\partial_jS\|^2|\omega|^2,
       \qquad \delta>0.
\end{align*}
The derivative of $K_\delta$ is constructed along actual differentiable
strain paths whenever the top eigenvalue is differentiable; its norm is
at most twice that of the strain derivative. This uses the unit
Rayleigh derivative, not an inverse eigenvalue gap. The finite sum over
spatial directions and multiplication by $\nu\ge0$ are also checked.
This theorem proves the gradient algebra only. The spatial coordinate
diffusion bridge is proved below by finite differences, without a smooth
eigenframe. The full material balance, a scale-uniform bound for the
time-integrated strain-gradient cost, and control of the regularization
in the complete alignment budget remain obligations. In particular,
$\delta$ is not silently sent to zero and the estimate is not scale-uniform.

\paragraph{Physical payment for a fixed low strain cutoff.}

\paragraph{Collision-safe Haar diffusion limit.}

The actual entrywise transport sum is proved to equal
$\mathcal W_\delta=\sum_j a_j\langle w,G_jw\rangle$ almost everywhere.
Consequently,
\[
 |\mathcal W_\delta|
 \le2|a|\Bigl(\sum_j\|\partial_jS_K\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}|w|^2.
\]
The operator norm is used for each coordinate derivative. This estimate
is independent of $\delta$ and contains no inverse eigenvalue gap.

For an arbitrary transporting multiplier $\beta$, put
\[
 H_{M,\beta}=\sum_{q\in M}|\beta(q)|^2,
 \qquad D_{K,M,\chi,\beta}=2\sqrt{H_{M,\beta}}\sqrt{C_{K,\chi}}.
\]
\texttt{PancakeWeightVariationCutoff} proves the physical velocity bound
$|v_\beta|\le\sqrt{H_{M,\beta}}\sqrt U$ and then, by finite Parseval,
$\int|\mathcal W_\delta|\le D_{K,M,\chi,\beta}U\Omega$.
For the mismatch $\beta=\chi-1$, write $D=D_{K,M,\chi,\chi-1}$.
Under the supplied retained-solution hypotheses and $\nu>0$, the checked

 \begin{aligned}
 \int_0^T\left|\int_{\mathbb T^3}\mathcal W_\delta\right|
   &\le \frac{D\,U(0)^2}{2\nu},\\
 \overline E_\delta(T)+\frac12\int_0^T\!\int|z|^2
   &\le\overline E_\delta(0)
      +\left(\frac{8C_{K,\chi}}\delta+\frac{D}{2\nu}\right)U(0)^2
      +\int_0^T\!\int\widetilde{\mathcal B}_\delta.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This is a finite-cutoff payment, not a uniform one. The same module proves
the exact identity
\[
 H_{M,\chi-1}=|M\setminus K|+\sum_{q\in K}|\chi(q)-1|^2,
 \qquad K\subseteq M,\quad\operatorname{supp}\chi\subseteq K.
\]
Thus the estimate itself exposes a retained-high-mode count. This is a
limitation of this bound, not a lower bound on the actual transport term
and not a refutation of a finer cancellation.

\paragraph{Alignment depletion, including the full top eigenspace.}
\texttt{PancakeAnisotropyDepletion} retains the transverse component

 \left|\langle e,Ae\rangle|w|^2-\langle w,Aw\rangle\right|
       \le4\|A\|\,|w|\,|\eta|.

The exact expansion into the transverse quadratic term and two mixed

 |\mathcal W_\delta|
   \le4\Bigl(\sum_j|a_j|\|\partial_jS_K\|\Bigr)|w|\,|\eta|
 \quad\hbox{almost everywhere}.

No time-integrated estimate of this depleted expression is asserted here.

At a repeated largest eigenvalue, the selected line is not the full

vanishing statement: if $K_0(S_K)w=0$, then $\mathcal W_\delta=0$ almost

eigenspace, without choosing a differentiable eigenvector or dividing by
a spectral gap. Quantitative control near that space, uniform summation,

\paragraph{The actual projection defect and its signed oscillation cost.}
Let $M\subseteq O$ and assume $M+M\subseteq O$, with the preceding
nonzero retained frequencies, incompressibility, conjugate reality and
negation symmetry. Write $N_q$ for the actual nonviscous convolution
coefficient and define the discarded output field
\[
 D(x)=\operatorname{Re}\sum_{q\in O\setminus M}e^{2\pi i q\cdot x}N_q.
\]
\texttt{PancakePhysicalNonlinearReconstruction} identifies the full-output
reconstruction with $S_Mw-(v_M\cdot\nabla)w$. It uses physical curl
inversion, the exact matrix--vector convolution and real reconstruction
of the velocity and strain. Consequently
\texttt{PancakeRetainedProjectionDefect} proves
\[
 f_{M,0}=-D,\qquad g=(S_M-S_K)w-D.
\]
These are instantaneous identities for the same coefficients $u$;
setting the viscosity parameter to zero in this expression does not
replace the viscous solution by an Euler solution.

\texttt{PancakeFiniteFourierOrthogonality} proves orthogonality of
disjoint finite Fourier supports, including the real vector pairing when
the first reconstructed field is real. No reality assumption on the
second output set is needed. The retained curl has conjugate symmetry,
and \texttt{PancakeProjectionWeightOscillation} extends the cancellation
to every constant real linear operator $A$:
\[
 \int_{\mathbb T^3}\langle Aw,D\rangle=0.
\]
For any continuous operator weight $L(x)$ it follows that
\[
 \int\langle L(x)w,D\rangle
   =\int\langle(L(x)-A)w,D\rangle.
\]
This cancellation is global. Multiplying by a spatial cell cutoff can
mix frequencies, so the same cancellation is not asserted on each cell.

\texttt{PancakeProjectionEnergyCancellation} first removes the scalar
regularization from the integrated projection pairing. Taking
$A=K_0(S_K(x_0))$ then gives the checked exact identity
\[
 \int 2\langle K_\delta(S_K)w,f_{M,0}\rangle
  =-2\int\bigl\langle
       [K_0(S_K(x))-K_0(S_K(x_0))]w,D\bigr\rangle.
\]
If $\|S_K(x)-S_K(x_0)\|\le\varepsilon$ for all $x$, the already
proved gap-operator Lipschitz bound yields
\[
 \left|\int 2\langle K_\delta(S_K)w,f_{M,0}\rangle\right|
       \le4\varepsilon\int |w|\,|D|.
\]
There is no inverse eigenvalue gap, inverse $\delta$ or mode count in
this inequality. The size and time integral of $D$ are still unbounded
here; the displayed estimate alone is not a cutoff-uniform budget.

\texttt{PancakeProjectionMaterialBudget} preserves this cancellation
inside the material estimate. Set
\[
 h=(S_M-S_K)w,\qquad
 \mathcal B^h_\delta=2\|R_0\||w|^2+2|h|^2
                           +2\delta\langle w,S_Kw+h\rangle.
\]
For integrable $F$ dominated almost everywhere by the actual material
rate, $\delta>0$ and $\nu\ge0$, the checked spatial estimate is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int F+\frac12\int|z|^2
 &\le\frac{16\nu}{\delta}C_{K,\chi}U\Omega
       +\int\mathcal B^h_\delta-\int\mathcal W_\delta\\
 &\quad-2\int\bigl\langle
       [K_0(S_K(x))-K_0(S_K(x_0))]w,D\bigr\rangle.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Only $h$, not $h-D$, is squared by Young's inequality. Thus the
projection defect has a signed linear cost instead of an artificial
squared cost. The spatially varying projection term, high-strain term,
strain remainder, transport variation, growing diffusion constant and
arbitrary-data limiting construction remain to be controlled. No new
time-integrated projection budget is claimed here.

\paragraph{Localizing the cancellation: the exact escaping field.}
For finite scalar cutoff frequencies $P$ and coefficients $c_p$, let
$\rho=\sum_{p\in P}c_pe^{2\pi i p\cdot x}$ be real-valued.
Let $b_q=\sum_{p+k=q,\ p\in P,\ k\in M}c_p\omega_k$ be the
already constructed cutoff convolution, and define
\[
 R_\rho=\operatorname{Re}\sum_{q\in M}e^{2\pi iq\cdot x}b_q,
 \qquad
 E_\rho=\operatorname{Re}\sum_{q\in(P+M)\setminus M}
                    e^{2\pi iq\cdot x}b_q.
\]
Here $E_\rho$ is escaped \emph{localized vorticity}, not the discarded
\emph{nonlinear output} $D$ above.
\texttt{PancakeLocalizedProjectionBoundary} proves
\[
 \rho w=R_\rho+E_\rho,\qquad
 \int\langle A(\rho w),D\rangle=\int\langle AE_\rho,D\rangle
\]
for every constant real operator $A$ and negation-closed $M$.
\texttt{PancakeRealFourierProjection} removes the need for conjugate
symmetry of the localized coefficients in this step: on a
negation-closed set, replacing $b_q$ by
$(b_q+\overline{b_{-q}})/2$ leaves the real reconstruction unchanged and
makes it real as a complex reconstruction. Thus reflection of frequencies
under real-part extraction is explicitly accounted for.

The source of $E_\rho$ is narrowed further by
\texttt{PancakeCutoffBoundarySupport}. Define the actual finite boundary
receiver set
\[
 \partial_P M=\{k\in M:\ \exists p\in P,\ p+k\notin M\}.
\]
At every exterior output $q\notin M$, replacing receiver set $M$ by
$\partial_PM$ leaves $b_q$ unchanged. Accordingly $E_\rho$ reconstructs
only outputs in $(P+\partial_PM)\setminus M$. If every shift of every
active receiver lies inside $M$, then $E_\rho=0$ exactly. This is a
buffered-support theorem, not a claim that the full unbuffered field
has no boundary receivers.

\paragraph{Quadratic weights, coverage error, and a count-free energy bound.}
Take the previously constructed weights
$\rho_i=|p_i|^2\ge0$ with their actual collected Fourier coefficients.
\texttt{PancakeQuadraticProjectionLocalization} identifies their scalar
reconstructions with $\rho_i$ and sets $E_i=E_{\rho_i}$.
The support of each quadratic coefficient family lies in the proved
difference set $\operatorname{supp}p_i-\operatorname{supp}p_i$; a buffer
containing all these candidate shifts makes the corresponding $E_i$ zero.
For a continuous operator field $L(x)$ and constant operators $A_i$, the
exact integrated decomposition is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int\langle Lw,D\rangle
 &=\int\sum_i\rho_i\langle(L-A_i)w,D\rangle
      +\sum_i\int\langle A_iE_i,D\rangle\\
 &\quad+\int\left(1-\sum_i\rho_i\right)\langle Lw,D\rangle.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The three terms are local freezing error, boundary-frequency coupling,
and coverage defect. The approximate Fourier partition is not silently
normalized or treated as exact.

\texttt{PancakeBoundaryEnergy} proves the orthogonal energy splitting
\[
 \int|\rho w|^2=\int|R_\rho|^2+\int|E_\rho|^2.
\]
In particular, if $\sum_i\rho_i(x)\le C$ everywhere, positivity and
finite Parseval give
\[
 \sum_i\int|E_i|^2
 \le\int\sum_i\rho_i^2|w|^2
 \le C^2\int|w|^2
 \le C^2\Omega.
\]
There is no cell-count or retained-mode-count factor. This controls the
\emph{sum of individual squared norms}, not the squared norm of an
unweighted sum of exterior fields. The latter does not follow without
additional cross-term control.

For a fixed quadratic family and a supplied retained velocity solution,
\texttt{PancakeBoundaryTimeBudget} constructs continuity of the actual
parameterized cutoff convolution and its mean energy. Time integrability
is therefore proved, not supplied as a budget assumption. With $\nu>0$,
$T\ge0$, the preceding retained reality/incompressibility hypotheses
and the retained ODE supplied on $\mathbb R$, kinetic dissipation gives
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \int_0^T\sum_i\int_{\mathbb T^3}|E_i(t,x)|^2
       \le\frac{C^2}{2\nu}U(0).
 \quad}
\]
For the constructed coverage bound $C=3/2$, the price is
$9U(0)/(8\nu)$, independent of cell count, cutoff bandwidth and retained
mode count. The weights are fixed in time in this theorem. Moving their
supports or coefficients requires further time-regularity work.
Most importantly, this pays only the escaped-vorticity side of
$\sum_i\int\langle A_iE_i,D\rangle$. It does not bound the nonlinear
field $D$, the weighted sum's cross terms by itself, moving-patch rates, or the
remaining high-strain and strain-remainder terms. The scale-critical
coupled budget and arbitrary-data continuation remain open.

\paragraph{The full boundary sum retains spectral depletion.}
The cross terms can now be handled explicitly. For arbitrary constant real
operators $A_i,A_j$, \texttt{PancakeWeightedBoundaryAssembly} proves
\[
 \int\langle A_iR_i,A_jE_j\rangle=0.
\]
Indeed the adjoint moves $A_j$ to the retained factor, after which the
negation-closed real Fourier orthogonality applies. Neither operator needs
to be symmetric. Set
\[
 G=\sum_i\rho_i A_iw,\qquad
 \mathcal R=\sum_iA_iR_i,\qquad
 \mathcal B=\sum_iA_iE_i.
\]
Then $G=\mathcal R+\mathcal B$ and
\[
 \int|G|^2=\int|\mathcal R|^2+\int|\mathcal B|^2.
\]
Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz, checked in
\texttt{PancakeWeightedBoundaryDepletion}, therefore gives
\[
 \boxed{\quad \int|\mathcal B|^2
       \le C\int\sum_i\rho_i|A_iw|^2.\quad}
\]
This is the squared norm of the \emph{complete operator-weighted sum}.
No cell count enters the bound, and the actual operator action has not
been replaced by $\|A_i\|\,|w|$.

For symmetric $S$, write $K_0(S)=\lambda_{\max}(S)I-S$,
$d_S(w)=\lambda_{\max}(S)|w|^2-\langle w,Sw\rangle$ and
$\sigma(S)=\lambda_{\max}(S)-\lambda_{\min}(S)$.
The two proved spectral-coordinate identities yield
\[
 |K_0(S)w|^2\le\sigma(S)d_S(w)
                \le 2\|S\|d_S(w).
\]
\texttt{PancakeSpectralResidualDepletion} also proves
$d_S(w)=0\iff K_0(S)w=0$. These statements hold at eigenvalue collisions
and refer to the entire top eigenspace; there is no inverse gap. With
$A_i=K_0(S_i)$, the assembled boundary estimate thus becomes
\[
 \int|\mathcal B|^2
   \le C\int\sum_i\rho_i\sigma(S_i)d_{S_i}(w).
\]

Unfreezing must retain the same benefit. The collision-safe inequality
$\|K_\delta(T)-K_\delta(S)\|\le2\|T-S\|$ implies
\[
 |K_\delta(T)w|^2
 \le2|K_\delta(S)w|^2+8\|T-S\|^2|w|^2.
\]
\texttt{PancakeBoundaryFreezingError} consequently proves, for a continuous
strain field $S(x)$ and constant sampled strains $S_i$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q(x)&=\sum_i\rho_i(x)\|S_i-S(x)\|^2,\\
 \int|\mathcal B|^2
 &\le2C^2\int|K_\delta(S(x))w(x)|^2
       +8C\int Q(x)|w(x)|^2,
 \end{aligned}
\]
where here $A_i=K_\delta(S_i)$. The unregularized case $\delta=0$ is
specialized in \texttt{PancakePhysicalBoundaryDepletion} to the actual
filtered Fourier strain, its sampled values at the patch centers, and
$w=\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Rec}_M\operatorname{curl}u$.
The exact three-term nonlinear pairing above then uses the single field
$\mathcal B$ in its middle term, including all cross-cell contributions.

The variance is quantified in \texttt{PancakeBoundaryFreezingVariance}.
If an exactly subordinate partition $\psi_i$ has active-cell oscillation
at most $\varepsilon$, $|\rho_i-\psi_i|\le\eta_i$, and the global
oscillation from center $i$ is at most $L_i$, then
\[
 Q(x)\le\varepsilon^2+\sum_i\eta_iL_i^2.
\]
For the constructed quadratic family with approximation tolerance $a>0$,
one may take $\eta_i=a(a+2)$ and $L_i=L$, obtaining
$V=\varepsilon^2+na(a+2)L^2$ with $n$ cells. Provided
$na(a+2)\le1/2$, the same
existentially constructed family has coverage between $1/2$ and $3/2$,
off-cell leakage below $a^2$, and
\[
 \int|\mathcal B|^2
   \le\frac92\int|K_0(S)w|^2+12V\int|w|^2.
\]
The partition and its active-cell/global oscillation bounds are explicit
inputs to this last construction. Its Fourier approximation precision
must pay the displayed aggregate tail; no uniform bandwidth or moving
support cost is inferred. In particular, this aligns the boundary energy
with the actual negative residual square in the material identity, but
does not yet control $\int\langle\mathcal B,D\rangle$. Squaring $D$ by
Young's inequality would leave an unpaid nonlinear-output energy, not a
closed arbitrary-data estimate.

\paragraph{Removing the projection artifact under a common smoothness envelope.}
The discarded field $D$ is a finite-projection error. One can remove this
error by a consistency limit under an explicit smoothness hypothesis,
without claiming an initial-energy bound for $|D|^2$.
For velocity coefficients $u_k$, put
\[
 m_s(u,k)=(1+|k|)^s|u_k|.
\]
Suppose $g_k\ge0$, $G=\sum_k g_k<\infty$, and $m_2(u,k)\le g_k$.
\texttt{PancakeCurlOutputTail} checks the two physical curl factors,
including the unit-torus derivative phase $2\pi i$. In particular, the
ordered coefficient of $\operatorname{curl}(u\times\operatorname{curl}u)$
at an input pair $(k,l)$ has norm at most
\[
 H_g(k,l)=(2\pi)^2g_kg_l.
\]
This majorant is summable over all lattice pairs. Define
\[
 \tau_M(g)=\sum_{k,l:\ k+l\notin M}H_g(k,l).
\]
\texttt{PancakeSummableOutputTail} proves $\tau_M(g)\to0$ as the finite
retained sets exhaust the lattice: each fixed output eventually lies in
$M$, and the entire pair series has a common summable bound.
\texttt{PancakeExteriorReconstructionTail} proves the corresponding
uniform reconstruction estimate, allowing arbitrary finite input-pair
and output sets.

For mean-zero velocity, $u_0=0$, with $k\cdot u_k=0$ on $M$,
\texttt{PancakeZeroModeCurlBridge} identifies the actual nonviscous
vorticity coefficients with this output curl even when $0\in M$.
\texttt{PancakeDiscardedNonlinearLimit} consequently gives
\[
 \|D_{M,O}(u)\|_{L^\infty}\le\tau_M(g).
\]
The same finite core works for every coefficient field under the same
$g$ and every finite output set $O$; this is not restricted to a single
fixed polynomial.

If $|\chi_k|\le B$ with $B\ge0$, the actual physical fields satisfy
\[
 \|w_M\|_{L^\infty}\le2\pi G,\qquad
 \|S_{\chi,M}\|_{L^\infty}\le9(2\pi)BG,\qquad
 \|K_0(S_{\chi,M})\|_{L^\infty}\le18(2\pi)BG.
\]
The factor $9$ is the explicit matrix-to-operator estimate used in
\texttt{PancakeSmoothSpectralEnvelope}, not a frequency count.
For normalized torus measure, \texttt{PancakeSmoothProjectionPairing}
therefore proves
\[
 \left|\int\langle K_0(S_{\chi,M})w_M,D_{M,O}\rangle\right|
 \le C(B,g)\tau_M(g),\qquad C(B,g)=18(2\pi)^2BG^2.
\]

For a time-dependent coefficient path continuous in each retained
coordinate, \texttt{PancakeSmoothProjectionTimeLimit} constructs joint
continuity of the actual discarded nonlinearity and continuity of this
spatial mean. If the same envelope and mean-zero/transversality hypotheses
hold throughout $[0,T]$, with $T\ge0$ and a fixed multiplier $\chi$, then
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \int_0^T\left|\int\langle K_0(S_{\chi,M})w_M,D_{M,O}\rangle\right|\,dt
 \le T C(B,g)\tau_M(g)\longrightarrow0.
 \quad}
\]
The coefficient continuity used here is on $\mathbb R$; domination is
required only on $[0,T]$. The convergence is uniform over paths with the
same envelope and multiplier bound. Time integrability is proved, not
inserted as an assumption.

This is a conditional consistency theorem, not a global regularity
estimate. It neither constructs the common envelope from a solution,
derives it from kinetic energy, constructs an arbitrary-data Galerkin
family, nor passes the complete material identity to a continuum limit.
The envelope may be used only where it has been established; its cost
must not be relabelled an a priori global bound. Intrinsic high-strain,
pressure, moving-weight and scale-summed misalignment estimates remain
separate open obligations.

\paragraph{Joint input/output consistency against a constructed full series.}
An output-only limit does not justify restricting a continuum solution
to a Galerkin system: high input frequencies can also contribute to
retained outputs. The full comparison is now constructed explicitly.
With $e_q(x)=\exp(2\pi i q\cdot x)$, set
\[
 C_{k,l}(u)=2\pi i(k+l)\times(u_k\times\omega_l),\qquad
 \mathcal N_\infty(u,x)=\sum_{k,l}\operatorname{Re}

Here $\omega_l=2\pi i\,l\times u_l$ is the physical curl coefficient.
\texttt{PancakeInfinitePairReconstruction} constructs this type of real
pair series, proves absolute summability under a summable coefficient

collection is identified exactly with the existing reconstruction.

For a mean-zero velocity the pair $C_{k,l}(u)$ is zero if $k=0$, $l=0$
or $k+l=0$. Thus \texttt{PancakeZeroSafeInteractionTail} replaces $H_g$ by

 H_g^0(k,l)=
 \begin{cases}
 (2\pi)^2g_kg_l,& k\ne0,\ l\ne0,\ k+l\ne0,\\

 \end{cases}
\]
Let $\mathcal T_M=\{(k,l):k,l,k+l\in M\}$ and

 \sigma_M(g)=\sum_{(k,l)\notin\mathcal T_M}H_g^0(k,l).

\texttt{PancakeInteractionTruncationTail} proves that this combined

$\sigma_{M\setminus\{0\}}(g)=\sigma_M(g)$ exactly: excluding the zero

This makes the exhaustion compatible with nonzero retained-mode sets.

Write $\mathcal N_M(u)$ for the real reconstruction on $M$ of the
actual finite nonviscous vorticity RHS, using $M$ for both inputs.
\texttt{PancakeFullNonlinearConsistency} proves, for $u_0=0$,
transversality on $M$, and the preceding common second-moment envelope,

 \|\mathcal N_\infty(u)-\mathcal N_M(u)\|_{L^\infty}
 \le\sigma_M(g)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Its finite-core theorem is uniform over all coefficient fields under $g$,
including exhaustion through $M\setminus\{0\}$.

\texttt{PancakeParametricInfiniteCurl} proves joint continuity of the
infinite series for continuously parameterized coefficients under a common
envelope. The parameter space may be the compact interval $[0,T]$ itself.
Accordingly \texttt{PancakeFullNonlinearTimeLimit} needs only coordinate
continuity, the common envelope and the algebraic hypotheses on that
interval, not on all of $\mathbb R$. For fixed $|\chi_k|\le B$, with

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \int_0^T\int_{\mathbb T^3}
       |\langle K_0(S_{\chi,M})w_M,R_M\rangle|\,dx\,dt
 \le T C(B,g)\sigma_M(g)\longrightarrow0.
 \quad}
\]
Absolute value is now inside both integrals. Spatial and temporal

This closes the combined Fourier-series consistency estimate under the
stated envelope. The spatial nonlinear identification is supplied next.
Constructing a continuum solution and its envelope, passing the complete
material inequality to the limit, and obtaining the intrinsic

\paragraph{Identification with the actual real curl nonlinearity.}
The infinite interaction is now identified with a derivative-defined
physical field. This step does not define curl by its Fourier symbol.
For a frequency map $\kappa:I\to\mathbb Z^3$ and complex vector coefficients
$a_n$, construct the series in the Banach space of continuous fields:
\[
 F_{\kappa,a}(x)=\sum_{n\in I}e_{\kappa(n)}(x)a_n,\qquad
 d_j a_n=2\pi i\,\kappa(n)_j a_n.
\]
\texttt{PancakeInfiniteFourierDerivative} differentiates its translation
curve in that Banach space under absolute summability of $a$ and $d_ja$.
Evaluation gives the ordinary coordinate derivative on the torus.
\texttt{PancakeIndexedFourierMoments} pays these hypotheses with
\[
 M_1^\kappa(a_n)=(1+|\kappa(n)|)|a_n|,\qquad
 |d_ja_n|_\infty,\ |2\pi i\,\kappa(n)\times a_n|_\infty
       \le 2\pi M_1^\kappa(a_n).
\]
Here the unsubscripted coefficient norm is Euclidean; its relation to the
coordinate sup norm is proved explicitly.
\texttt{PancakeInfiniteSpatialCurl} consequently proves
\[
 \nabla\times F_{\kappa,a}
   = F_{\kappa,\,2\pi i\kappa\times a},
\]
where the left side uses actual derivatives of coordinate translations.

For $\kappa(k,l)=k+l$, the product estimate is
\[
 (1+|k+l|)|u_k\times v_l|
       \le (1+|k|)|u_k|\,(1+|l|)|v_l|.
\]
Thus two summable first moments pay the first moment of the pair series.
Continuous bilinearity and absolute double summation in
\texttt{PancakeInfiniteFourierProduct} identify it exactly with
$F_{\mathrm{id},u}\times F_{\mathrm{id},v}$.
\texttt{PancakeInfiniteRealCurl} proves reality by conjugation and
reindexing $k\mapsto-k$, and commutes real part with the actual derivatives.

Write $U=\operatorname{Re}F_{\mathrm{id},u}$. Assume the reality symmetry
$u_{-k}=\overline{u_k}$ and a summable second moment
$\sum_k(1+|k|)^2|u_k|<\infty$.
The theorem in \texttt{PancakeFullAnalyticNonlinearity} gives
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal N_\infty(u)
   =\nabla\times\bigl(U\times(\nabla\times U)\bigr).
 \quad}
\]
Both curls here are ordinary derivatives of real fields. The second
moment pays the first moments of both $u$ and its curl. No incompressibility
or zero-mean hypothesis is needed for this identification itself.
Under the additional mean-zero, transversality and common-envelope
hypotheses used above, the retained nonlinear RHS therefore satisfies
\[
 \left\|\nabla\times\bigl(U\times(\nabla\times U)\bigr)
            -\mathcal N_M(u)\right\|_{L^\infty}
       \le \sigma_M(g)\longrightarrow0.
\]
This removes the spatial series-identification gap. It does not construct
a time-evolving Navier--Stokes solution from arbitrary data, establish a
common envelope on its lifespan, or bound that envelope globally. The
intrinsic high-strain, pressure, moving-weight and scale-critical
misalignment estimates remain open.

\paragraph{Constructing the common envelope from physical regularity.}
The envelope can now be obtained from defined spatial derivatives of a
physical input field. For a scalar field $f:\mathbb T^3\to\mathbb C$, set
\[
 D_jf(x)=\left.\frac{d}{dh}f(x+h e_j)\right|_{h=0},
 \qquad J_{j,n}f=D_j^n f.
\]
\texttt{PancakeContinuousCoordinateJet} uses these iterates themselves.
Its order-six regularity predicate requires continuity of $J_{j,n}f$
for $0\le n\le6$ and coordinate differentiability of the iterates for
$n<6$. No independently chosen derivative fields are supplied.

Haar translation invariance gives
\[
 \widehat{f(\,\cdot+c\,)}(k)=e_k(c)\widehat f(k).
\]
Differentiation under this integral, dominated by the norm of the
continuous derivative, proves

 (2\pi|k_j|)^n|\widehat f(k)|

 (1+|k|)^6|\widehat f(k)|\le12288B .

 w_k=(1+|k|)^{-4},\qquad \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^3}w_k<\infty.

 \sup_{p\in P,\ x\in\mathbb T^3}

\paragraph{Constructed local retained evolution from physical data.}
The finite coefficient evolution is now constructed, rather than supplied
as a path satisfying an equation. Write
\[
 E_M=\mathbb C^{M\times\{1,2,3\}},\qquad
 H_M=\{v\in E_M:\ k\cdot v_k=0,\ v_{-k}=\overline{v_k}\},
\]
where coefficients outside $M$ are extended by zero. The subspace $H_M$
is a real Hilbert space. Assume $M=-M$ and $0\notin M$.
\texttt{PancakeFiniteVelocitySmooth} proves that the previously defined
physical velocity RHS, restricted to $E_M$, is smooth: its expression is
finite and quadratic, and every inverse wave-number factor is constant
with respect to the state.

\texttt{PancakeFiniteVelocityInvariant} proves directly that this vector
field maps $H_M$ into $H_M$. The Fourier Leray projection preserves
conjugate symmetry, and its output is transverse. The transport
convolution and viscous coefficient respect the same reality symmetry.
An orthogonal projection gives a smooth map with codomain $H_M$; the
checked inclusion identity proves that this projection changes no
coefficient of the actual vector field on $H_M$.

Picard--Lindel\"of on this complete finite-dimensional space yields, in
\texttt{PancakeLocalVelocityExistence}, a time $T_M>0$ and a coefficient
curve $u_M$ such that
\[
 \begin{split}
 &(u_M)_k(0)=\widehat u_0(k)\quad(k\in M),\qquad
   (u_M)_k(t)=0\quad(k\notin M),\\
 &\partial_t(u_M)_k=V_M(u_M)_k\quad(0\le t\le T_M,\ k\in M),\\
 &k\cdot(u_M)_k=0,\qquad (u_M)_{-k}=\overline{(u_M)_k}.
 \end{split}
\]
A closed positive-time interval is chosen strictly inside the open
existence interval, so the derivative statements hold at both endpoints.
There is no input evolution or assumed invariant-subspace preservation.

The already checked energy and curl identities now apply to this
constructed curve. \texttt{PancakeLocalVelocityEnergy} proves the actual
finite vorticity equation and
\[
 U_M(t)+2\nu\int_0^t\Omega_M(s)\,ds=U_M(0),\qquad 0\le t\le T_M.
\]
For $\nu\ge0$, \texttt{PancakeLocalVelocityUnique} proves uniqueness on
this interval: the energy ball is compact, the smooth finite vector
field is Lipschitz on it, and the ODE uniqueness theorem applies.

Finally, \texttt{PancakeLocalL2Velocity} takes actual componentwise
$L^2(\mathbb T^3)$ data, not a free Fourier coefficient sequence.
Almost-everywhere reality gives conjugate symmetry of the Fourier
integrals; incompressibility is stated as their Fourier divergence
condition. Parseval yields, for every retained set and on its constructed
lifespan,
\[
 U_M(t)\le\|u_0\|_{L^2}^2,\qquad
 2\nu\int_0^t\Omega_M(s)\,ds\le\|u_0\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
Thus the energy payment has no cutoff or mode-count loss. At this stage
the quantifier order is $\forall M\,\exists T_M>0$; the continuation
construction below subsequently provides a common interval. An
infinite-dimensional evolution is not yet constructed. The nonzero
truncations exclude the initial mean; their initial
matching is asserted only on retained modes. Uniform higher-regularity
estimates, passage to the continuum, and the intrinsic global alignment
budget remain necessary.


\paragraph{Cutoff-independent local second-order energy control.}
Set
\[
 w(k)=(1+|k|)^2,\qquad
 E_M(u)=\sum_{k\in M}w(k)^2|u_k|^2,\qquad
 D_M(u)=\sum_{k\in M}w(k)^2|k|^2|u_k|^2,
\]
and let $L=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^3}(1+|k|)^{-4}<\infty$ be the
already constructed lattice sum. All coefficient norms here are Euclidean.
\texttt{PancakeFiniteConvolutionL2} proves a finite convolution estimate
without a mode-count factor. If $a_M$ is the zero extension of a sequence
on $M$, translation injectivity and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \sum_{k,l\in M}a_M(k+l)b_kc_l
 \le \|a\|_{\ell^2(M)}\|b\|_{\ell^2(M)}
           \sum_{l\in M}c_l
 \quad(c_l\ge0),
\]
and the analogous bound with the two inputs exchanged.
\texttt{PancakeSobolevWeights} proves
\[
 w(k+l)\le2\bigl(w(k)+w(l)\bigr),\qquad
 \sum_{k\in M}a_k\le\sqrt L\,
       \left(\sum_{k\in M}w(k)^2a_k^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Applying these two input placements to $a_k=|u_k|$ gives the
weighted convection majorant $4\sqrt L\,E_M\sqrt{D_M}$.

\texttt{PancakeSobolevVelocityRate} identifies this majorant with the
existing physical velocity equation, not a replacement convolution.
The Leray projection does no work against the transverse output
coefficient; the nonzero transverse source coefficients justify the
existing curl-to-velocity recovery identity. Retaining the physical
$2\pi$ factor and the exact viscosity term yields
\[
 E'_M+2\nu(2\pi)^2D_M
       \le8(2\pi)\sqrt L\,E_M\sqrt{D_M}.
\]
The derivative is obtained from the actual coefficient ODE.
\texttt{PancakeSobolevAbsorption} then proves, for $\nu>0$,
\[
 \boxed{\qquad E'_M+\nu(2\pi)^2D_M
                 \le C_\nu E_M^2,\qquad C_\nu=\frac{16L}{\nu}.\qquad}
\]
There is no cutoff radius or cardinality in $C_\nu$.

For any initial bound $E_M(0)\le B_0$, $B_0\ge0$, define
\[
 \tau_\nu(B_0)=\frac{1}{2(C_\nu+1)(B_0+1)}>0.
\]
\texttt{PancakeQuadraticGrowthWindow} uses monotonicity of
$(E+1)^{-1}+C_\nu t$ to handle zero initial energy without division by it.
\texttt{PancakeUniformLocalSobolev} applies this scalar comparison to the
constructed local coefficient solution:
\[
 E_M(t)\le2(B_0+1),\qquad
 0\le t\le T_M,\quad t\le\tau_\nu(B_0).
\]
A nonnegative summable initial envelope $m_2(u_0,k)\le g_k$ pays
$E_M(0)\le(\sum_k g_k)^2$ for every $M$.
\texttt{PancakePhysicalInitialWindow} constructs such an envelope from
the defined order-six continuous coordinate derivatives of the original
physical initial field. It combines that construction with actual local
Galerkin existence, assuming reality and the Fourier divergence condition.
No regularity or Fourier envelope of a future trajectory is an input.

This cutoff-independent local \emph{a priori} estimate bounds each
solution on the intersection of its existing lifespan with the common
window. The following continuation construction extends that conclusion
to actual solutions on the entire common window. The quadratic growth
inequality is not a global regularity bound.

\paragraph{Constructed continuation and a common Galerkin interval.}
\texttt{PancakeDissipativeFiniteODE} proves a finite-dimensional
continuation theorem directly. Let $F$ be a smooth vector field on a
finite-dimensional real Hilbert space, with
$\langle x,F(x)\rangle\le0$ for every $x$.
Choose a smooth scalar bump $\chi$ that is one on
$|x|\le|x_0|+1$, nonnegative everywhere, and zero outside a larger ball.
The field $G=\chi F$ is smooth with compact support, hence globally
bounded and Lipschitz. Picard--Lindel\"of on $[-1,T+1]$, with a spatial
ball enlarged to pay its global speed bound, constructs a trajectory
for every prescribed finite $T\ge0$. There is no small-time restriction
on the Lipschitz constant in this construction.

Along the constructed curve,
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}|a(t)|^2
   =2\chi(a(t))\langle a(t),F(a(t))\rangle\le0
       \qquad(0\le t\le T).
\]
Thus $|a(t)|\le|x_0|$, so $\chi(a(t))=1$ throughout the required
interval. The output therefore solves $a'=F(a)$, not merely the
auxiliary cutoff equation. The larger initial time interval supplies
two-sided derivatives at both endpoints of $[0,T]$.

\texttt{PancakeFiniteVelocityContinuation} applies this construction
to the already defined physical invariant subspace. Its actual vector
field satisfies
\[
 2\langle v,F_M(v)\rangle
    =-2\nu\sum_{k\in M}|\widehat{\operatorname{curl}u}(k)|^2\le0
       \qquad(\nu\ge0).
\]
The equality uses the exact retained kinetic cancellation. The
codomain projection is proved equal to the original velocity RHS,
so neither that projection nor the auxiliary bump changes the
dynamics. A physical retained solution now exists on every prescribed
finite horizon. Uniqueness proves agreement with any previously
constructed solution on their common interval; no separate extension
hypothesis is supplied.

Combining this with the preceding second-order estimate,
\texttt{PancakeCommonGalerkinWindow} closes the lifespan quantifier gap:
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \exists\,\tau>0,\ B\ge0\quad
 \forall\,M\quad
 \exists\,u_M\text{ on }[0,\tau]\quad
 \forall\,t\in[0,\tau],\ E_M(u_M(t))\le B .
 \quad}
\]
Here $M$ is any symmetric finite set excluding zero. The initial
coefficients are the Fourier integrals of the physical field with the
defined order-six continuous coordinate derivatives, reality and the
Fourier divergence condition. The interval and bound precede the
choice of $M$. Separately, actual $L^2$ initial data give solutions on
any finite horizon with the same Parseval-paid kinetic and integrated
enstrophy bounds, without a uniform high-regularity claim for those data.

\texttt{PancakeSobolevTimeBudget} also proves continuity, and hence
integrability, of the finite viscous quantity directly from the
coefficient equation. The absorbed differential estimate yields
\[
 E_M(t)+\nu(2\pi)^2\int_0^t D_M(s)\,ds
       \le E_M(0)+C_\nu B^2t .
\]
Consequently the common physical family has the cutoff-independent
time budget
\[
 \nu(2\pi)^2\int_0^t D_M(s)\,ds
       \le H,\qquad H=B+C_\nu B^2\tau,\qquad 0\le t\le\tau .
\]
This constructs the common finite family and its local bounds.
Convergence to a continuum evolution, propagation of the regularity
needed by the full nonlinear consistency theorem, and the intrinsic
global alignment budget remain to be proved. In particular, finite
horizon existence at every cutoff is not continuum global regularity;
the bound $E'\le C_\nu E^2$ remains a local comparison.

\paragraph{A constructed, time-uniform coefficient limit.}
The fixed-output estimate can use kinetic energy alone.
Incompressibility gives
\[
 k\cdot u_k=0,\quad k+l=q
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 l\cdot u_k=q\cdot u_k .
\]
The already checked convolution-fiber bound, together with this
identity, yields in \texttt{PancakeFixedOutputVelocityBound}
\[
 \left|\sum_{\substack{k,l\in M\\k+l=q}}(u_k\cdot l)u_l\right|
       \le |q|\,U_M(u),\qquad U_M(u)=\sum_{k\in M}|u_k|^2 .
\]
The Euclidean norm of the actual Leray symbol is proved contractive,
including its defined zero-mode branch. Retaining viscosity and the
unit-torus derivative factor gives
\[
 |V_M(u)_q|
   \le (2\pi)|q|\,U_M(u)
         +|\nu|(2\pi)^2|q|^2|u_q|.
\]
There is no input-frequency or mode-count factor.

For a constructed solution with $U_M(t)\le U$,
\texttt{PancakeCoefficientEquicontinuity} proves
\[
 |(u_M)_q(t)|\le\sqrt U,\qquad
 |\partial_t(u_M)_{q,i}(t)|
   \le A_q:=(2\pi)|q|U+|\nu|(2\pi)^2|q|^2\sqrt U .
\]
Every fixed component is therefore $A_q$-Lipschitz on the common
interval. Excluded coefficients are identically zero. Dependence
on the fixed output $q$ is explicit; a uniform Lipschitz constant
over all output frequencies is not asserted.

\texttt{PancakeCountablePathCompactness} proves a reusable extraction
theorem. For each coordinate, the bounded equicontinuous paths have
compact closure in the uniform norm by Arzel\`a--Ascoli. The countable
product of these compact closures is compact and first countable.
Sequential compactness therefore supplies one strictly increasing
subsequence for every coordinate simultaneously, rather than a
different subsequence for each coordinate.

\texttt{PancakeGalerkinCoefficientLimit} takes the explicit nested
sets
\[
 M_n=\{k\in\mathbb Z^3:\ |k_i|\le n\text{ for all }i\}\setminus\{0\}
\]
and proves their symmetry, exclusion of zero, monotonicity, and
eventual inclusion of every nonzero mode. The common physical
existence theorem supplies $u_{M_n}$, and $U_{M_n}\le E_{M_n}\le B$
supplies the kinetic bound. A single extraction $\phi:\mathbb N\to
\mathbb N$ and continuous coefficient paths $a_{q,i}$ are constructed,
with
\[
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}
       |(u_{M_{\phi(n)}})_{q,i}(t)-a_{q,i}(t)|
            \longrightarrow0
       \qquad\text{for every fixed }q,i .
\]
The original physical initial field is an input; these finite
trajectories and the subsequential coefficient field are outputs.

\texttt{PancakeCoefficientLimitProperties} proves that this same
coefficient limit retains conjugate symmetry, Fourier transversality,
and zero mean. Every nonzero initial coefficient is preserved.
Full initial matching is proved under the explicit mean-zero
initial condition. For every finite set $F$, the finite-support
energy comparison and passage to the coefficient limit give
\[
 \sum_{q\in F}(1+|q|)^4|a_q(t)|^2\le B .
\]
Consequently the infinite series is proved summable, with
\[
 \boxed{\qquad
   \sum_{q\in\mathbb Z^3}(1+|q|)^4|a_q(t)|^2\le B
       \qquad(0\le t\le T).
 \qquad}
\]
This is a constructed infinite coefficient field with physical
constraints and a genuine Sobolev energy bound. It is not yet a
proved Navier--Stokes evolution: strong convergence sufficient for
the nonlinear products, passage to the evolution equation, and
the subsequent global intrinsic alignment estimate remain open.
The local $H^2$ bound also does not by itself provide the common
summable pointwise second-moment envelope used by the earlier
higher-regularity consistency theorem.


The eigenvalue-gap denominator also appears in spatial frame freezing. In
\texttt{PancakeEigenframePerturbation}, if $Ae=\lambda e$, $Bf=\mu f$, $B$
is self-adjoint, and $e,f$ are unit vectors, then
\[
  (\lambda-\mu)\langle f,e\rangle
    =\langle f,(A-B)e\rangle,
  \qquad
  |\lambda-\mu|\,|\langle f,e\rangle|\leq\|A-B\|.
\]
Consequently, at every positive threshold, either the eigenvalues are in the
near-degenerate band or the frame overlap is bounded by the strain oscillation
divided by that threshold.  This is the exact gap/near-degeneracy alternative
needed by the variable-frame remainder; it is not yet the scale-summed bound
needed for closure.

Two further modules turn this into an intrinsic local dynamics statement.
\texttt{PancakeStrainSpectralFrame} constructs, for every symmetric
three-dimensional strain operator, an ordered orthonormal eigenframe and
proves the three-coordinate Parseval and Rayleigh identities.  Thus the
spectral coordinates used below are constructed objects, not assumed data.
For a unit vorticity direction $\xi$ and a moving unit top eigenvector $e$,
\texttt{PancakeAlignmentDynamics} defines
\[
  E(\xi,e)=1-\langle\xi,e\rangle^2
\]
and proves the exact simple-gap identity
\[
 \dot E
 =-2\langle\xi,e\rangle^2
     \bigl(\lambda_0-\langle\xi,S\xi\rangle\bigr)
   -2\langle\xi,e\rangle
     \bigl(\langle d,e\rangle+\langle\xi,\theta\rangle\bigr),
\]
where $d$ is the non-strain direction forcing and $\theta=\dot e$.  The
Rayleigh defect is bounded below by the top spectral gap times $E$, and the
module integrates this damping to obtain the residence estimate
\[
 \int_0^T (\lambda_0-\lambda_1)E\,dt
 \le E(0)+2\int_0^T
   |\langle d,e\rangle+\langle\xi,\theta\rangle|\,dt
\]
under its stated regularity and simple-gap hypotheses.  It also proves the
complementary upper bound by the full spectral width.

These identities expose rather than hide the hard cases.  Substituting the
usual eigenvector perturbation estimate
$|\theta|\lesssim|\dot S|/(\lambda_0-\lambda_1)$ into the residence inequality
and applying a naive Young inequality produces still worse inverse-gap costs;
it is not a closure.  Moreover, a large trace-free strain may be isotropic on
a strongly expanding two-plane.  Such a partially degenerate plane supplies
damping toward the plane but does not select a coherent line inside it.
Controlling those intervals requires a projector/cluster formulation or a
genuine time-integrated cancellation, and remains part of the dynamical pin.

\section{Status and scope}
\label{sec:scope}

We state the scope of this finding exactly.

\paragraph{What is established from the original audit.} Hypothesis~(H1) of
v7 --- a uniform
$\Hm\to\Hm$ operator bound \eqref{eq:H1} on the adjoint / push-forward $\Ad_g$
over an $\Hm$ chart-neighborhood of the identity --- is \emph{false} in the
normalized Fourier-mode shear model of Section~\ref{sec:lean}. The refutation is
machine-checked (no \texttt{sorry}, elementary) in
\texttt{Mettapedia.FluidDynamics.NavierStokes.BenH1Break}, and the underlying
norms are independently derived from the Sobolev-weight structure in the
companion numeric lab. The model faithfully captures the mechanism that defeats
v7's local-boundedness argument: the adjoint \eqref{eq:adg} loses one derivative
($g\in\Hm\Rightarrow Dg\in H^{m-1}$), so on a fixed-radius $\Hm$ chart the
operator norm is unbounded along high-wavenumber shears.

\paragraph{What is not established.} This is a refutation of (H1)
\emph{in a model} --- a single analytic Fourier-mode family together with the
linearized commutator action --- not a from-Mathlib-Sobolev-first-principles
theorem about $\SDiff^m(\mathbb{T}^3)$. The model uses the exact push-forward
formula \eqref{eq:adg} on an explicit shear family and the standard Fourier
$\Hm$ norm; it does not formalize the full diffeomorphism-group functional
analysis. The mechanism (derivative loss) is captured faithfully, but the
strength of the conclusion is ``checked in this model,'' not ``checked over the
entire chart from Mathlib's Sobolev theory.''

\paragraph{Consequence for v7.} Because every quantitative step of the
SG--Cole--Hopf program inherits the constant $\CAd$ from \eqref{eq:H1} ---
through \eqref{eq:prop65}, \eqref{eq:logbound}, and \eqref{eq:vort} --- the
falsity of~(H1) as stated means the route does \emph{not}, as written, establish
global regularity. v7 reduces 3D Navier--Stokes global regularity to~(H1)
(equivalently, to a uniform curvature / Bakry--\'Emery-type bound), and that
reduction target does not hold uniformly in the form required.

\paragraph{What the repair establishes.} The stochastic-Lagrangian repair is
not refuted by the H1 countermodel, and that evasion is itself a checked
theorem (Section~\ref{sec:evasion}).  Machine-checked without \texttt{sorry}
across the campaign: the one-form calculus with the exact frame-Laplacian
identity; the divergence-free non-exact witness forcing the gauge; the
pushdown theorem producing Navier--Stokes with the explicit pressure
$p=\partial_tW+\tfrac12\langle u,u\rangle+\langle u,\nabla W\rangle-\nu\Delta W$
from the averaged transport hypothesis; the gate consumers and the assembled
conditional route; nonzero exact inhabitants (decaying shear with balanced
gauge) and end-to-end rest canaries for both the coarse and the refined
route; the frozen-strain exact solution with its unbounded-amplification and

\texttt{stochasticLagrangian\_conditional\_route\_of\_pancakeBudget} records

\paragraph{Scope of the new pieces.}  The same honesty discipline as for the

\paragraph{Verification metadata.}  The named campaign modules contain no

theorems carry explicit machine axiom audits; the remaining newly added

\paragraph{What remains open.} We make no claim to have proved the Millennium

\texttt{ScaleLocalPancakeStrainBudget} (cross-referenced with the coarser

projector, identify the now-exact hard-block physical split with the route's

prove a dynamical,

\ref{sec:direction-dynamics} further shows that a pressure-Hessian route must
