\section{The mechanism and the two mathematical targets}
\label{sec:current-overview}

Goertzel and Fable's compositional idea is to explain how a planar map can
be built, simplified and coloured through a small collection of structural
laws. A piece has an ordered interface. Its possible colourings retain the
choices shared with neighbouring pieces. A reduction makes a smaller map;
a reconstruction transports a compatible colouring back to the original.
The intended architecture is
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{embedded map}
\ \longrightarrow\ \text{recognize a reducible piece}
\ \longrightarrow\ \text{smaller embedded map}\\
\longrightarrow\ \text{colour the smaller map}
\ \longrightarrow\ \text{reconstruct the original colouring}.
\end{gathered}
\]
The recognition step is mathematical: it must be proved for every map in
the intended class. Naming a constructor or an interface does not guarantee
that such a piece occurs.

There are two distinct targets. The classical Four Colour theorem is
already available in the audited Lean assembly. For a finite simple graph
$G$ supplied with a noncrossing path drawing in the real plane,
\[
                  G\text{ admits a proper colouring by four colours}.
\]
The theorem includes disconnected graphs, isolated vertices and the empty
graph. Its declaration is
\path{FourColor.CompositionalComplete.colorable_of_planeDrawing}.
The same assembly supplies compatible GSLT construction histories for
every bridgeless spherical cubic source map, retaining its vertices,
edges and cyclic order. Parallel source edges are not silently removed.

The classical coverage and reducibility mathematics is reused from
Robertson, Sanders, Seymour and Thomas, through the Gonthier--Werner
formalization and Chris Emery's Lean port. The new reconstruction and shared
arguments modify this assembly; they do not make its classical foundations
new. Ben's stronger explanatory target remains open: replace extensive
independent obligations by a few uniform family laws, with proved geometric
supply and termination.

\section{What composition already proves}
\label{sec:current-witnesses}

\subsection{Compatible solutions really stitch together}

Write $W(P;x,y)$ for actual colouring witnesses of a piece $P$ with boundary
values $x,y$. Serial gluing has the witness equivalence
\[
 W(P;Q;x,z)\ \simeq\
       \coprod_y W(P;x,y)\times W(Q;y,z).
\]
A colouring of the composite is obtained from two witnesses agreeing on
the \emph{same} seam $y$. Conversely, restriction recovers that pair.
Nonemptiness gives relational composition; finite cardinality gives the
boundary-matrix multiplication law. Existence, counting and a recorded
recolouring history are different statements.

This is also the central constraint on interacting insertions. A joint
relation $R\subseteq C^I$ must retain its common choices. Replacing it by the
product of its coordinate projections can admit tuples with no joint
witness. The coupled diamond repair keeps both insertion choices together.
Its finite core certificate and a general boundary-split transfer supply
configurations 1--4 in the complete classical assembly. Four original
certificate dependencies are absent from its endpoint; a shared finite
certificate remains.

These are instances of the proposed mechanism, not consequences of a

The complete source-construction theorem is existential. It guarantees
successful compatible choices along a valid complete sweep. It does not
say that every arbitrary partial colouring extends at every next step,
nor does it supply a new executable colouring algorithm.

\section{Geometric coverage is the decisive missing theorem}
\label{sec:current-coverage}

The classical proof supplies a reducible configuration by discharging:
charge is redistributed, and the catalogue closes the surviving local
cases. Replacing several repair certificates leaves this coverage theorem
in place. Fewer repair arguments and fewer enumerated coverage obligations
are separate achievements.

The 2026 paper by Inoue, Kawarabayashi, Miyashita, Mohar, Thomassen and
Thorup supplies a particularly relevant research target. Its
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24880v3}{near-linear algorithm} finds many
reductions or short obstructing cycles and uses an all-D-reducible
catalogue. Our experiments use the pinned catalogue revision
\texttt{c2ce7a96e35256201526e2abb51a39d04a588b53}, with its attribution and
licence retained. Its 8,200 files and 1,681 underlying internal graph
shapes are a reproducible inventory, not 1,681 certified repair families:
prescribed degrees and exterior attachments were omitted by that grouping.

In this work, flatness means \emph{zero final charge under specified
discharging rules}. It does not imply Euclidean flatness, bounded width or
a pumping theorem. Two overlapping neighbourhoods can jointly reveal a
reducible piece that neither local view supplies alone. The Lean work
retains their alignment and shared vertices instead of combining two
independent pictures.

The local coverage assembly is checked as an implication from named supply
laws. Its radius-twelve conclusion preserves the supplied occurrence and
observations when the centre changes. Actual consumers recognize several
diamond, chain, fan, tripod and exceptional geometries under stated facial
and degree hypotheses. The full supply laws are not thereby established.
In particular, a continuation to another degree-seven centre can return
to an earlier observed vertex. Such a return needs a certified reduction,
a separator argument, or a decreasing invariant.

An integrated uniform coverage result must therefore connect
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{ambient facial and degree data}
\ \longrightarrow\ \text{a complete embedded occurrence}\\
\longrightarrow\ \text{compatible repair}
\ \longrightarrow\ \text{strict reduction in the same graph class}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Vertex collisions, extra ambient edges, ordered ports and cyclic turns
cannot be discarded at the occurrence boundary. Local reducibility also
does not prove compatibility of simultaneous repairs sharing exterior
Kempe components. The newer algorithm's scheduling and cost bounds remain
separate obligations; its near-linear runtime is not claimed for this
formalization.

\section{Distinction calculus: retain what an operation can reveal}
\label{sec:current-distinctions}

\subsection{Two concrete failures of a static interface}

The decoded configuration D0099 has eleven ordered boundary vertices and
eight interior vertices. Its symbolic elimination uses six relations,
each retaining the \emph{same boundary as an argument}. A proper boundary
and an inhabited terminal relation reconstruct a colouring of the entire
nineteen-vertex source graph.

Evaluating those relations once and then forgetting the boundary is
insufficient for later updates. Two checked inputs have the same six
evaluated relations, yet the same accepted boundary operation makes one
extendible and the other nonextendible. If $S$ is this snapshot, $U$ the
operation and $E$ the extension answer, then
\[
 S(b_0)=S(b_1),\qquad E(Ub_0)\ne E(Ub_1).
\]
No function of $S(b)$ alone can predict $E(Ub)$. Every exact predictive
refinement must separate these two inputs. For crisp distinction observers,
the snapshot-to-updated-answer comparison requires defect at least one.
A preservation grade cannot recover information already forgotten.

A second checked example has identical visible diamond seams but
different full-source extension answers. After the boundary repair, the
source extends by choosing a different cap witness. Keeping the old cap
colouring frozen still fails. The larger piece must retain the option to
revise that hidden choice.

These examples refute the specified interfaces. They do not construct an
uncolourable planar graph, and do not prove that every compact interface
must fail. The snapshot inputs have their own abstract exterior
chromograms; this comparison constructs no common physical exterior.

\subsection{One exact interface for both operations}

Let $N(v)$ be the boundary neighbours of an interior vertex $v$, and let
$\rho:B\to K$ partition the boundary into regions. Record
\[
 P_b(v,k)=\{\,b(i):i\in N(v),\ \rho(i)=k\,\}.
\]
An admitted update applies one colour map $f_k$ throughout each region:
$b'(i)=f_{\rho(i)}(b(i))$. The general Lean theorem proves
\[
                         P_{b'}(v,k)=f_k[P_b(v,k)].
\]
The observation square commutes exactly. Set images compose, so the same
identity holds through every finite history of admitted updates, at
arbitrary boundary width. Coarsening takes unions of region reports and
commutes with maps uniform on the coarse regions. A common refinement
supports two operation partitions.

For D0099 that common refinement admits both the earlier interval update
and the hidden-vertex cap exchange. A completed report means
\[
\exists h:\text{interior}\to C,\quad
\begin{cases}
h\text{ satisfies every interior edge constraint},\\
h(v)\notin P_b(v,k)\quad\text{for every }v,k.
\end{cases}
\]
There is one shared $h$, not independently selected colours for different
constraints. With a proper final boundary, completion is equivalent to a
full source colouring fixing all its ports. Exact transport observes
supplied operations; it neither selects a useful Kempe component nor
guarantees that the completed report is inhabited.

\section{The remaining crux and ways the route could fail}
\label{sec:current-crux}

The central risk is compact coverage: useful local laws may accumulate
without a short reason why every map supplies one. A second risk is
interface growth: exact compatibility may require increasingly detailed
exterior information. A third is failure of progress: a sequence of
locally valid repairs can revisit an earlier state without reducing the
map. Renaming a catalogue, storing a large strategy table, or bounding
each isolated operation would not settle these issues.

The earlier audits already isolate specific false shortcuts. Finite
generation does not imply a finite exact counting image. Local Kempe
reachability does not automatically lift every move through a serial
composition. Recurrent states need not have a self-loop. None of these
counterexamples rules out a sound restricted family theorem or a different
coverage mechanism.

The next local obligation is concrete. Adjacent cap triangles route a
chosen component of the original noncrossing exterior matching. Removing
their adjacent pairs can join the partners of two different exterior
chords; this is more than deleting an already matched pair. The routing
law must preserve the original exterior and the relation between the
residual exit and hidden boundary updates. It must then reconstruct the
\emph{whole} source with compatible cap choices.

The bounded program test has three successful base forms, covering 33
original noncrossing matchings. Twelve related forms still need
intermediate states outside the tested family. These are discovery
results, not a Lean routing theorem or a proof that the family is
repair-closed. A useful next theorem explains the successful routes
structurally and identifies the extra invariant needed by the residual
forms. If that invariant grows into a per-state strategy table, the
decomposition or the operation domain needs revision.

Full closure requires all three global ingredients: geometric supply of
the admitted families, a valid well-founded reduction, and compatible
reconstruction. The completed classical theorem provides a reliable
baseline while these independent obligations remain open.

The complete endpoint and selected repair consumers have dependency audits

Reported frontend timings are local replay measurements, not runtime

The evidence has three levels. A Lean theorem proves its stated conclusion
under its actual hypotheses. A checked conditional assembly still requires
its named supply laws. An independent program census or finite repair game
is exploratory evidence unless a separate semantic and kernel bridge is
provided.

The concise source map is:
\begin{itemize}

source histories and classical dependency audit.

conditional supply assembly and its actual-map consumers.
\item \texttt{StripSourceExtension}, \texttt{CapJointTransport} and
\texttt{CapRechoice}: full-source reconstruction, compatible cap updates
and the need to revise hidden witnesses.
\item \texttt{PartitionedBoundary}, \texttt{BoundaryDistinction} and
