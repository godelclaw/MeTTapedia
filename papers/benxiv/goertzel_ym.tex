\begin{abstract}
Ben Goertzel's Yang--Mills programme combines Wilson lattice gauge theory,
renormalization, a stopping-scale polymer expansion, observable transport and
Osterwalder--Schrader reconstruction. This report separates the mechanism from
its proved estimates and its remaining analytic inputs. For the actual
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ ordered-product block map, Lean now verifies exact Haar
integration, generated interaction functions, finite-depth composition and a
volume-uniform contraction in a spatially weighted norm on an explicit
strong-coupling interval. It also verifies a gap $1/4096$ for the eliminated
Maxwell fluctuations modulo gauge, with the Wilson quadratic normalization and
the associated Gaussian covariance bound. The gap is on the discarded sector;
it does not assert a mass for the full Maxwell field.

The missing connection is quantitative control of the actual weak-coupling
trajectory and its transported observables. Exact covariance transport retains
a conditional-covariance remainder at each scale. The available estimates do
not yet give spatial decay for those remainders at arbitrary depth and weak
coupling. We state the required entrance and transport conditions, the proved
physical-distance consequences, and the further continuum obligations. Earlier
extraction audits and numerical diagnostics are retained in appendices. No
continuum Yang--Mills construction or mass-gap theorem is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{The mechanism and the present proof target}\label{sec:mechanism}

The proposed mechanism is to integrate out short-distance fluctuations while
retaining the variables that carry long-distance physics. At a suitable
physical scale, the generated interactions should enter a regime where a
convergent polymer estimate proves spatial decay. Exact observable transport
must then carry that decay back to the original lattice. A continuum limit and
quantum-field reconstruction are further steps, not consequences of blocking
alone.

The official problem asks for a nontrivial quantum Yang--Mills theory on
$\mathbb R^4$ with positive mass gap for every compact simple gauge group
\cite{clay}. The concrete blocking results in this report concern
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ in four dimensions. Some lattice clustering and transfer
results apply to wider classes of groups. They do not supply the full
group quantifier or the continuum construction.

Write $U_\ell\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$ for a positive oriented link variable and
\[
 U_{x,\mu\nu}=U_{x,\mu}U_{x+e_\mu,\nu}
              U_{x+e_\nu,\mu}^{-1}U_{x,\nu}^{-1},\qquad
 V(U)=1-\tfrac12\operatorname{Re}\Tr U.
\]
With product normalized Haar measure $dH$, the finite-volume Wilson law is
\[
 d\mu_\beta(U)=Z_\beta^{-1}W_\beta(U)\,dH(U),\qquad
 W_\beta(U)=\exp\!\left(-\beta\sum_p V(U_p)\right),\quad 0\leq V\leq2.
\]
Small $\beta$ is strong coupling; the continuum route needs a cutoff
$a\to0$ and a weak-coupling starting sequence $\beta\to\infty$.

The current dependency chain is
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \text{Wilson density and exact block integration}\quad\text{(proved)}\\
 \downarrow\\[-3pt]
 \text{weak-coupling entrance and spatial observable transport}
       \quad\text{(open)}\\
 \downarrow\\[-3pt]
 \text{small spatial interaction norm and clustering}\quad\text{(proved)}\\
 \downarrow\\[-3pt]
 \text{nontrivial continuum limit and OS reconstruction}\quad\text{(open).}
\end{gathered}
\]
The new quadratic fluctuation gap supports the second line by ruling out
arbitrarily soft physical modes in the eliminated linear sector. It is not
the missing nonlinear estimate itself.

Goertzel's v14 proposes extended extraction through canonical dimension $16$
to make irrelevant interactions contract despite recombination losses. That
proposal motivates the finite coordinate audits in the appendices. The
contraction proved below instead acts on actual generated interaction
functions in a specified strong-coupling norm. It does not validate v14's
advertised weak-coupling recursion constant.

\section{Exact blocking remembers the measure and the observables}\label{sec:exact}

On a fine torus of side $2L$, $L=n+3\geq3$, the coarse links are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
 B(U)(x,\mu)=U(2x,\mu)U(2x+e_\mu,\mu).
\end{equation}
The order of multiplication matters. A Haar coordinate chart writes each
two-link pair as $(f,f^{-1}c)$, where $c$ is the retained coarse link;
the other fine links are additional eliminated coordinates. The chart is
measure preserving, and the block map is gauge equivariant with coarse gauge
transformation $g(2x)$.

For an actual positive continuous density $W$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-step}
 \overline W(c)=\int W(\operatorname{chart}(c,f))\,dH(f),\qquad
 PF(c)=\frac{\int W(\operatorname{chart}(c,f))F(\operatorname{chart}(c,f))\,dH(f)}
                  {\overline W(c)}.
\end{equation}
Positivity supplies the denominator. Partition functions and expectations
agree before and after integration. For every finite nested elimination
sequence, the direct integral agrees with staged integration, both for the
density and for transported observables. The generated measure is always
$\overline W\,dH/\int\overline W$; it is not replaced by unweighted Haar.
For $j$ scale changes, the fine side is $2^j(n+3)$ and the final side is $n+3$.
This is an actual change of lattice scale, in addition to marginalization.

The essential two-point identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covariance-tower}
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_W}(F,G)
 =\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_{\overline W}}(PF,PG)
  +\mathbb E_{\mu_{\overline W}}\!\left[P(FG)-(PF)(PG)\right].
\end{equation}
The last term records correlations carried by the discarded variables. It
vanishes for exact pullbacks of coarse observables. For general original
observables, iteration gives the final coarse covariance plus a sum of scale
contributions $c_i(F,G)$ under the successive generated laws. Lean proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance-budget}
 \sum_{i=0}^{j-1}|c_i(F,G)|
 \leq\tfrac12\left(\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_W}F+
                         \operatorname{Var}_{\mu_W}G\right).
\end{equation}
This bound has no depth factor, but also no spatial decay. For centered
Wilson plaquette marks with explicit normalization factors $z,w$, the
right-hand side is at most $(z^2+w^2)/2$, for every real $\beta$. A
cutoff-dependent normalization must therefore stay in the estimate.

There is a formal reason to keep the remainder. A positive gauge-invariant
family of the form $W=1+\varepsilon FG$, with separated Haar-centered
plaquette marks, can have $\overline W=1$ and $PF=PG=0$ while its original
covariance is nonzero. This is a counterexample to inferring fine
correlations from the coarse density and means alone. It is not a
counterexample to the Wilson trajectory.

The GSLT route encodes these actual integration steps and expectation
identities. The OSLF predicates express analytic contracts on the resulting
weighted states. For example, the forward existential modality is discharged
by the constructed blocked state and its proved bound. The logical interface
organizes the composition; it does not supply an analytic hypothesis that
has not been proved for the generated law.

\section{A repeatable spatial contraction at strong coupling}\label{sec:spatial}

An interaction state consists of continuous local functions $\Phi_S$ indexed
by finite link sets, an explicit vacuum constant $v$, and the density
\[
 W_\Phi(U)=\exp\left(v+\sum_S\Phi_S(U)\right).
\]
Each $\Phi_S$ is gauge invariant, depends only on $S$, has Haar mean zero,
and $\Phi_\varnothing=0$. Centering transfers constants into $v$ rather than
discarding normalization. The state carries a small support-weighted norm.

Links use the periodic circle sup pseudometric induced by their base points;
directions at the same base point have distance zero. Let $\tau(S)$ be the
infimum of lengths of finite metric tree networks spanning $S$, allowing
auxiliary vertices. This is the precise tree-length convention in the Lean
definition, rather than a restriction to trees whose vertex set is exactly
$S$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spatial-norm}
 \|\Phi\|_\eta
 =\sup_\ell\sum_{S\ni\ell}
       e^{8|S|+\eta\tau(S)}\|\Phi_S\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
The coarse support map is nonexpanding in the corresponding lattice-unit
metrics and sends both links in each ordered pair to its coarse link.

\begin{theorem}[Actual spatial blocking; Lean]\label{thm:spatial}
For every $\eta\geq0$, every volume and every interaction state with
$\|\Phi\|_\eta\leq1/2$, the exact Haar integration and centered extraction
produce an interaction state $\operatorname{RG}(\Phi)$ such that
\[
 \overline W_\Phi=W_{\operatorname{RG}(\Phi)},\qquad
 \|\operatorname{RG}(\Phi)\|_\eta\leq q\|\Phi\|_\eta,
 \qquad q=4e^{-2}<1.
\]
The equality includes the vacuum constant. For any finite depth $j$, the
generated interaction norm is at most $q^j\|\Phi\|_\eta$.
\end{theorem}

The quantified object is an interaction state with the stated locality,
centering, gauge and smallness properties; no arbitrary-density contraction
is asserted. The generated interactions are the complete functions defined
by the convergent expansion, not a fitted Wilson coupling or a finite Taylor
truncation. This does not give an all-order boundary-derivative contract.

For the actual Wilson starting interaction, Lean proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entry}
 \|\Phi^{\mathrm W}_\beta\|_\eta
 \leq64|\beta|e^{32+4\eta},\qquad
 \beta_{\mathrm{sc}}(\eta)=\frac1{128e^{32+4\eta}}.
\end{equation}
Thus $|\beta|\leq\beta_{\mathrm{sc}}(\eta)$ enters the radius $1/2$, with
$\eta$, the radius, $q$ and the threshold chosen before the volume and the
depth. For physical strong coupling one restricts to $\beta\geq0$.
The retained sector is nonvacuous: ordered-product Wilson observables survive
the construction.

For $\eta>0$, the same radius supplies a spatial polymer majorant and
\path{CrossingInput} for the generated density. For continuous real
observables $F,G$ supported on finite sets $S,T$, with $S\ne\varnothing$
and $|F|\leq B$, $|G|\leq C$, the proved consequence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clustering}
 |\operatorname{Cov}(F,G)|
 \leq3BCe^{3|S|+3|T|}e^{-\eta d(S,T)}.
\end{equation}
It also holds for their exact fine-lattice pullbacks under any finite number
of these block maps. The vacuum factor cancels in normalized expectations.

\paragraph{Responses and fixed-depth discarded terms.}
The Wilson response calculus includes spatially summable second, third and
fourth score-cumulant estimates on both established assembly levels. For each permitted strong-coupling
parameter, the positive rates and finite constants precede volume, pinned
boundary data and rotation frames. Ordered
third log derivatives retain direct action contacts and all three mixed
covariance corrections; conditioning retains all three corrections in the
third-cumulant tower law and the fifteen partitions in the fourth-order law.
Near, overlapping and coincident supports are included. These finite-order
results do not establish arbitrary-order analytic control.

Actual conditional spatial bounds are also proved for one and two dyadic
eliminations, uniformly in the retained field, on explicit smaller intervals
\[
 |\beta|\leq\frac1{256e^{32+12\eta}},\qquad
 |\beta|\leq\frac1{256e^{64+56\eta}},
\]
respectively. The estimates keep the enlarged dependency supports and use the
generated weighted measure at the second step. They are not yet spatial
bounds for arbitrary depth at weak coupling.

\paragraph{Other lattice consequences.}
Independent compact-group results prove strong-coupling clustering, vanishing
central-involution twist free energy, and a transfer-operator gap. For the
latter, the group is compact Hausdorff and second countable, the Borel reference
probability law is inversion invariant, and the continuous potential is a real class
function with $\psi(g^{-1})=\psi(g)$ and $0\leq\psi\leq2$. At the explicit
threshold in \path{StrongCouplingTransferGap.Rectangular.beta0}, there is
$\kappa>0$ chosen before spatial volume such that the real $L^2$ transfer
operator has a simple positive leading eigenvalue $\lambda_0$, and every
other point $\lambda$ of its real spectrum obeys
$|\lambda|\leq e^{-\kappa}\lambda_0$. Normalized Haar satisfies the reference
law hypothesis. Positivity of the integral kernel is not a claim that every
eigenvalue is nonnegative.

Site reflection positivity is proved separately on the even tori of side
$2(n+2)$, with an inversion-invariant reference probability and a bounded
measurable real class potential invariant under inversion. At every real
$\beta$, the reflection form is real and nonnegative for bounded measurable
complex observables depending on links in the closed half torus. These are lattice statements. Neither
the strong-coupling gap nor site reflection positivity constructs a
nontrivial continuum theory.

\section{The eliminated quadratic modes have a uniform gap}\label{sec:maxwell}

At the trivial background, the derivative of the actual ordered product
\eqref{eq:block} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-block}
 QA(x,\mu)=A(2x,\mu)+A(2x+e_\mu,\mu).
\end{equation}
This is proved for differentiable $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ matrix paths through the
identity. A path in the exact identity block fiber has $QA=0$, and the Haar
chart realizes the corresponding eliminated tangent directions.

For one real Lie-algebra component, define
\[
 \nabla\phi(x,\mu)=\phi(x+e_\mu)-\phi(x),\qquad
 \mathcal G_n=\{\nabla\phi:Q\nabla\phi=0\},\qquad
 \mathcal H_n=\ker Q\cap\mathcal G_n^\perp.
\]
The equality $Q\nabla\phi=\nabla(\phi\circ(2\,\cdot))$ specifies exactly
which infinitesimal gauge transformations preserve the coarse field. All
norms here use counting measure on fine links and positive plaquettes.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform eliminated-sector gap; Lean]\label{thm:maxwell}
On every four-dimensional torus of side $2(n+3)$ and for every real link
field $A$ with $QA=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maxwell-gap}
 \frac1{4096}\operatorname{dist}_{2}(A,\mathcal G_n)^2
 \leq\sum_p(\operatorname{curl}A(p))^2.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\|A\|_2^2\leq4096\|\operatorname{curl}A\|_2^2$ on
$\mathcal H_n$. The constant precedes the volume quantifier. The componentwise
bound also holds for the three real colors of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$.
\end{theorem}

The proof is local: a coordinate-path gauge subtracts a fiber-preserving
gradient, and a finite cell Stokes identity expresses each remaining link
through nearby curls. Summation gives the displayed constant, including
periodic seams. The physical sector is nonzero: an explicit transverse
eliminated field has nonzero curl. Thus the theorem is neither an estimate
on pure gauge fields alone nor a numerical spectral extrapolation.

For $A=i\sum_{a=1}^3 A^a\sigma_a$ in the Pauli convention, the exact identity
$V(U)=\frac14\|U-I\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$ fixes the quadratic coefficient:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-wilson}
 \lim_{t\to0}\frac{\beta\sum_p V(U_p(t))}{t^2}
   =\frac\beta2\sum_{p,a}(\operatorname{curl}A^a(p))^2.
\end{equation}
The analogous centered conditional action limit is proved on the exact
identity fiber. This is an action expansion relative to Haar, not a proof
that the nonlinear conditional measure converges to a Euclidean Gaussian;
the coordinate Jacobian and the error estimates still matter.

For $\beta>0$, the formalized one-color centered Gaussian on $\mathcal H_n$
has precision $\beta D_n^*D_n$, where $D_n$ is curl restricted to
$\mathcal H_n$. Its covariance is the inverse precision, and
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\langle u,A\rangle
    \leq\frac{4096}{\beta}\|u\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\operatorname{curl}A(p))\leq\frac{65536}{\beta}.
\]
These are volume-independent size bounds. An off-diagonal spatial decay
estimate for the constrained Gaussian curvature covariance is not yet
proved. The full Maxwell field still has slow modes; the theorem concerns
only fluctuations removed by this specific block map.

\paragraph{Numerical diagnostic, separate from the proof.}
The reproducible Fourier diagnostic checks the constrained physical sector
after removing gauge zero modes. Across the refined set of $1460$ sampled
momenta, including momenta of order $2\pi/10^8$, the smallest computed
physical eigenvalue was approximately $0.7084973779$. Eigen-equation, gauge
and covariance-inverse residuals were at most $3.72\cdot10^{-14}$,
$4.25\cdot10^{-15}$ and $1.81\cdot10^{-14}$, respectively. These are
floating-point diagnostics, not certified error intervals or a proof of the
uniform minimum. The independent Lean cell argument proves the smaller
constant $1/4096$.

\section{The stopping scale in physical units}\label{sec:physical}

Fix a cutoff label $k$, spacing $a_k>0$, coupling $\beta_k\geq0$ and finite
blocking depth $j(k)$ independent of spatial volume. The coarse spacing is
$r_k=2^{j(k)}a_k$. The formal entrance obligation requires constants
\[
 \eta>0,\qquad 0<r_{\min}\leq r_{\max}<\infty
\]
chosen before $k$ and the volume, with $r_{\min}\leq r_k\leq r_{\max}$.
For every cutoff and volume, the actual directly integrated Wilson density
must equal an interaction-state density with $\|\Phi_{k,n}\|_\eta\leq1/2$,
including its vacuum factor. The weak-coupling obligation adds
$a_k\to0$ and $\beta_k\to\infty$. Its existence is not proved.

Under entrance, the lattice decay rate becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:physical-rate}
 m=\eta/r_{\max}>0,
 \qquad e^{-\eta d(S,T)}\leq e^{-m r_k d(S,T)}.
\end{equation}
For exact coarse pullbacks with support sizes at most $M$ and amplitudes
$B,C$, Lean obtains $3BCe^{6M}e^{-mD}$ at physical separation
$D=r_kd(S,T)$, uniformly in cutoff and volume.

General original observables require more. Their transported means may be
sums of local terms with large supports; the formal contract charges these
through anchored spatial budgets $B,C$. If the physical anchor separation is
at least $D-\delta$ and the expected conditional-covariance remainder is at
most $\varepsilon e^{-mD}$, then Lean proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport-target}
 |\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_{\beta_k}}(F,G)|
 \leq\left(3BCe^{m\delta}+\varepsilon\right)e^{-mD}.
\end{equation}
All four transport constants precede the cutoff and volume. For exact
pullbacks, the remainder is zero. For general marks, its bound is a
separate hypothesis, not part of entrance for the density alone.

There is also a proved scale-summation criterion. If $D\geq D_0>0$ and
each actual scale contribution satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:missing-scale}
 |c_i(F,G)|\leq A\exp\left(-\frac{\eta D}{2^i a_k}\right),
 \qquad 0\leq i<j(k),
\end{equation}
with $A$ fixed before cutoff, volume and depth, their sum is bounded by
\[
 \frac{Ae^{-mD_0}}{1-e^{-mD_0}}e^{-mD}.
\]
No growing depth factor is needed. Near and coincident marks can instead
use the original amplitude bound; this adds $2B_0C_0e^{mD_0}$ to the
prefactor when $|F|\leq B_0$ and $|G|\leq C_0$. This does not make
cutoff-growing field normalizations uniformly bounded.

\section{What remains to connect weak coupling to the continuum}\label{sec:remains}

The exact integration and strong-coupling contraction are established. The
next analytic target is \eqref{eq:missing-scale} for the actual generated
Wilson trajectory, together with spatial budgets for its transported means.
At scale $i$, the relevant conditional law is explicitly
\[
 d\nu_i(f\mid c)=
 \frac{W_i(\operatorname{chart}(c,f))}{W_{i+1}(c)}\,dH(f),
 \qquad
 d\mu_{i+1}(c)=\frac{W_{i+1}(c)}{\int W_{i+1}\,dH}\,dH(c).
\]
The scale contribution averages a conditional covariance under $\nu_i$
against $\mu_{i+1}$, using the correctly transported original marks. This
formulation permits an averaged large-field estimate; it does not require a
false uniform quadratic approximation at every retained configuration.

Four distinct obligations remain.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Spatial weak-coupling fluctuation control.}
First obtain off-diagonal decay for the constrained Gaussian curvature
covariance. Then control the non-Abelian action remainder, the Haar
coordinate factor, background dependence and large fields under the actual
generated conditional measures. A positive quadratic gap at the identity
does not imply these bounds. Plaquette energy marks are quadratic to leading
order, so fourth-moment control and their normalization also enter.

\item \textbf{A weak-to-strong entrance trajectory.}
Prove that a weak-coupling Wilson sequence reaches the spatial interaction
radius at a volume-independent finite depth, with physical stopping scales
bounded above and below. Keep all generated non-Wilson interactions. A
running scalar coupling or a fixed strong-coupling example does not establish
this entrance theorem.

\item \textbf{Closure of the required response information.}
The available low-order response identities and exact interaction-function
expansions have different scopes. If repeated weak-coupling analysis needs
an all-order response contract, its order dependence, conditioning identities
and remainder estimates must be proved. The dimension-$16$ extraction branch
also still needs its complete coupled physical quotient, conditioned dual
coordinates and analytic comparison to the actual Wilson interaction norm.

\item \textbf{A nontrivial continuum quantum field theory.}
Construct compatible cutoff limits with suitable observable normalization,
Euclidean symmetry and regularity, reflection positivity and a
nontriviality witness; then perform the required reconstruction and transfer
the physical decay estimate to a Hamiltonian gap. Lattice reflection
positivity and a finite-volume transfer gap are inputs to this programme,
not a completed reconstruction.
\end{enumerate}

The most plausible failure of the proposed bridge is loss of quantitative
control of physically normalized observables under repeated weak-coupling
integration, even when the final marginal density looks harmless. The exact
covariance identity exposes this possibility. The Maxwell gap is encouraging
because the chosen block does not leave arbitrarily soft physical
fluctuations in its eliminated quadratic sector. Deciding whether the
nonlinear generated measures retain useful spatial control is the substantive
next test.

\paragraph{Calibration and extraction audits.}
The crossing criterion is calibrated against the free Maxwell field:
\path{FreeFieldNoCrossing.false_of_phaseRepresentation} rules out reproducing
its specified finite-torus Gaussian phase expectations by a crossing family.
The twist diagnostic is a necessary condition for a twist-compatible
crossing representation; vanishing twist free energy alone is not a
sufficient crossing theorem. Earlier finite-volume Monte Carlo tests of
bare Wilson and hierarchical models are retained in
Appendix~\ref{sec:stopping}. They do not refute the actual generated
nonlinear trajectory by fitting it to a single coupling.

The extraction audit likewise distinguishes a valid scalar calculation from
an unproved analytic constant. The arithmetic $2224\,2^{-13}=139/512$ is
checked, but v14's two factor ledgers give $33152$ and $11088/5$, and neither
derives the needed Wilson recursion estimate as written. A projection's
range does not control its norm. The repaired finite constructions preserve
trace order and the coupling of field sectors by commutators; complete joint
conditioning remains an additional task. These findings delimit particular
steps of the route rather than deciding the continuum question.

\section{Extended extraction in the v14 proposal}\label{app:proposal}

The following appendices preserve the detailed algebraic audits and earlier
diagnostics. They concern the stated coordinate models, source policies and
manuscript factorization. Their conditional constants are distinct from the
actual strong-coupling interaction contraction in
Theorem~\ref{thm:spatial}. Current analytic obligations are collected in
\S\ref{sec:remains}.

\subsection{The continuum-limit obstruction and extended extraction}

In any RG scheme one must show that the ``irrelevant'' sector (operators of
canonical dimension $>4$) contracts at each step:
\[
  \| K^{\mathrm{irr}}_{j+1}\| \;\leq\; \Lambda\, \|K^{\mathrm{irr}}_{j}\| + (\text{source terms}),
  \qquad \Lambda < 1.
\]
The manuscript identifies a sharp obstruction in the \emph{naive} scheme, where
one extracts only the relevant and marginal operators of dimension $\leq 4$.
Under rescaling, a dimension-$d$ operator scales by $b^{4-d}$, so the leading
irrelevant operators (dimension $6$) contract by $b^{-2} = 0.25$ for $b=2$. But
the RG step also recombines polymers, contributing a combinatorial constant
$C_1$. The manuscript advertises the working bound $C_1 \leq 2224$ for $b=2$;
if that value were established, the naive gain would be
\[
  \Lambda_{\mathrm{naive}} \;=\; 2224\, b^{-2} \;=\; 2224 \times 0.25 \;=\; 556 \;>\; 1,
\]
an \emph{expanding} map: initial smallness would grow like $556^{J}$ over
$J\sim|\log a_0|$ steps and diverge as $a_0\to 0$.

The proposed resolution is \emph{extended extraction}. Instead of extracting
only dimension $\leq 4$ operators, one extracts all gauge-invariant local
operators up to canonical dimension $\dmax = 16$ (for $b=2$). The irrelevant
sector then begins at dimension $\dmax+1 = 17$ and contracts by
\[
  \Lirr \;=\; b^{\,4-(\dmax+1)} \;=\; b^{\,3-\dmax} \;=\; 2^{-13} \;\approx\; 1.2\times 10^{-4}.
\]
With the same advertised value, the proposed contraction factor becomes
\[
  \boxed{\;\Lambda \;=\; C_1\,\Lirr \;\leq\; 2224 \cdot 2^{-13} \;\approx\; 0.27 \;<\; 1.\;}
\]
The manuscript presents this as genuine contraction ``with substantial slack.''
The audit below verifies this arithmetic only \emph{conditional on the supplied
constant}; it refutes the available derivations of that constant as written.
The price is tracking a finite-dimensional vector of higher couplings
$\vec g_j = (g_j^{(4)},g_j^{(6)},\dots,g_j^{(\dmax)})$ rather than the single
gauge coupling; the manuscript argues these are perturbatively small and do not
affect the universal one-loop beta-function coefficient
$b_0 = 11/(3\cdot 16\pi^2)$, which is determined entirely by local ultraviolet
information.

\subsection{The recombination constant and the extraction-projection norm}\label{sec:recombination}

Appendix~O proposes the factorization
\[
  C_1 \;\leq\; C_{\mathrm{fluct}} \cdot C_{\mathrm{cumulant}} \cdot C_{\mathrm{recomb}} \cdot C_{\mathrm{extract}}
  \;\leq\; 1.65 \times 3b^4 \times 14 \times 2,
\]
which at $b=2$ equals $11088/5=2217.6$, rather than exactly $2224$.
Appendix~F.5 displays a different factor product which equals $33152$ at
$b=2$; the two ledgers are inconsistent. The last Appendix-O factor
$C_{\mathrm{extract}} = \|\Pleq\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ is the operator norm of the
extraction projection onto the finite-dimensional space of operators of
dimension $\leq \dmax$. The manuscript bounds it (its Computation~O.7(d)) by
\[
  \|\Pleq\|_{\mathrm{op}} \;\leq\; \sum_{d=0}^{\dmax} \binom{\#\mathrm{indices}}{d}\,(r_1/r_0)^{-d}
  \;\leq\; 1 + O\!\big((r_0/r_1)^{\dmax}\big),
\]
and asserts $\|\Pleq\|_{\mathrm{op}} \leq 2$ for $r_1/r_0 = 1/2$ and $\dmax=16$.
The literal negative exponent grows, but changing only its sign does not turn
the displayed multivariate sum into a one-dimensional geometric series. More
fundamentally, v14 specifies $P_{\mathrm{ext}}$ by its range and does not give
the dimension-$16$ invariant basis, dual jets, or conditioning estimates needed
to determine its operator norm. We return to the proved realization and the
transfer obstruction in \S\ref{sec:honest}.

\subsection{The extended-extraction contraction predicate}

The module \path{Mettapedia.QuantumTheory.YangMills.RGBootstrap} models the
route's contraction obligation as a predicate over a recombination constant
$C$, block factor $b$, and extraction depth $\dmax$. The irrelevant scaling
factor is $b^{3-\dmax}$ (an integer power, since the useful cases have
$\dmax>3$), and a contraction is a nonnegative constant whose product with the
irrelevant scale is below $1$.

\begin{lstlisting}
/-- Irrelevant scaling factor `b^(3-dmax)` used by the extended-extraction
discussion.  The exponent is an integer because the useful cases have
`dmax > 3`. -/
def irrelevantScale (b dmax : Nat) : Real :=
  (b : Real) ^ (3 - (dmax : Int))

/-- The route-level contraction audit for a combinatorial constant and an
irrelevant scaling factor. -/
def HasRGContraction (C scale : Real) : Prop :=
  0 <= C /\ 0 <= scale /\ C * scale < 1

/-- The extended-extraction audit specialized to `lambda = b^(3-dmax)`. -/
def HasExtendedExtractionContraction (C : Real) (b dmax : Nat) : Prop :=
  HasRGContraction C (irrelevantScale b dmax)
\end{lstlisting}

A threshold lemma in the same module records the exact content of the predicate:
for $b>0$ and $\dmax\geq 3$, the contraction \texttt{HasExtendedExtractionContraction
C b dmax} holds if and only if $0 \leq C < b^{\dmax-3}$. Thus the entire
arithmetic content of the route is the comparison of $C$ with the threshold
$b^{\dmax-3}$.

\subsection{The knife-edge arithmetic}

The module machine-checks that the advertised constant contracts at $\dmax=16$
and that the exact one-step gain is $2224\cdot 2^{-13} = 139/512 < 1/3$:

\begin{lstlisting}
/-- The advertised numerical slack: `2224 * 2^-13 < 1`. -/
theorem rgContraction_2224_two_sixteen :
    HasExtendedExtractionContraction 2224 2 16 := by
  refine <by norm_num, by rw [irrelevantScale_two_sixteen]; norm_num, ?_>
  rw [irrelevantScale_two_sixteen]
  norm_num

/-- Exact advertised one-step gain for the extended-extraction constants. -/
theorem rgGain_2224_two_sixteen_eq :
    (2224 : Real) * irrelevantScale 2 16 = (139 : Real) / 512 := by
  rw [irrelevantScale_two_sixteen]
  norm_num
\end{lstlisting}

Crucially, the same constant does \emph{not} contract at the nearest even
extraction depth below the advertised one. At $\dmax=14$ the threshold is
$2^{11}=2048<2224$, so the contraction is arithmetically false; the module
proves that any contraction at $\dmax=14$ would force $C<2048$, and hence that
$2224$ fails:

\begin{lstlisting}
/-- Any contraction proof at `dmax = 14` must improve the linear recombination
constant below `2048`.  The manuscript's advertised bound `2224` is therefore
insufficient for this lower extraction depth. -/
theorem rgContraction_two_fourteen_forces_constant_lt_2048
    {C : Real} (h : HasExtendedExtractionContraction C 2 14) :
    C < 2048 := by
  have hprod : C * irrelevantScale 2 14 < 1 := h.2.2
  rw [irrelevantScale_two_fourteen] at hprod
  nlinarith

/-- Same-constant blocker at the nearest even extraction depth below `16`. -/
theorem not_rgContraction_2224_two_fourteen :
    not  HasExtendedExtractionContraction 2224 2 14 := by
  intro h
  have hC : (2224 : Real) < 2048 :=
    rgContraction_two_fourteen_forces_constant_lt_2048 h
  norm_num at hC
\end{lstlisting}

This is the knife edge: the choice $\dmax=16$ is exactly what makes the
advertised constant contract, and the manuscript's own table shows
$\dmax\in\{10,12,14\}$ all fail against $C_1\leq 2224$ while $\dmax=16$ succeeds.
The module \path{Mettapedia.QuantumTheory.YangMills.RGCrux} packages this into
a route-level statement: a mass-gap route that keeps the advertised constant
$2224$ but uses an even extraction depth strictly below $16$ cannot exist, since
the required contraction is arithmetically false before any OS / clustering
layer is reached.

\begin{lstlisting}

canonical-dimension shape: the cutoff is strictly below `16`, is even, and uses
the same advertised recombination bound `2224`. -/
def SameConstantEvenBelowSixteenRGCertificate (dmax : Nat) : Prop :=

/-- No same-constant RG certificate exists for an even manuscript-shape cutoff

theorem not_sameConstantEvenBelowSixteenRGCertificate

    not  SameConstantEvenBelowSixteenRGCertificate dmax := by

\end{lstlisting}

A strengthened companion theorem,
\path{not_atLeast2048EvenBelowSixteenRGCertificate}, shows that any even

\section{Finite extraction and quotient audits}\label{sec:honest}

This appendix specifies the extraction models, relation policies and finite
certificates underlying the v14 audit.

\paragraph{The contraction constant is an input, not a conclusion.}
Every arithmetic theorem above takes the recombination constant as a
\emph{given real number}. The predicate \texttt{HasExtendedExtractionContraction
C b dmax} is a statement about $C$, $b$, $\dmax$ alone; the checked facts
\texttt{rgContraction\_2224\_two\_sixteen} and
\path{not_rgContraction_2224_two_fourteen} verify the comparison
$C \lessgtr b^{\dmax-3}$ for the specific value $C=2224$. Nothing in the
formalization derives that $2224$ (or any $O(1)\cdot b^4$ bound) is the correct
value of the manuscript's recombination constant. The Lean ledger is therefore
faithful to the route's bookkeeping but agnostic about the route's hardest
analytic claim.

\paragraph{The extraction projection: realization succeeds, range-only transfer
fails.}
The sign-only repair is insufficient: even with positive exponent, the displayed
binomial/multivariate multiplicities make the corrected finite sum exceed $2$
once at least two coordinates are present. We therefore constructed the natural
finite polynomial realization rather than treating the omission as a verdict.
For a finite Wilson block of actual group-valued links, plaquette holonomies,
and a conjugation-invariant Wilson potential, finite polynomials in the
plaquette coordinates are gauge invariant. In the explicitly defined weighted
coefficient-majorant norm, canonical-dimension truncation has operator constant
$1$ (module \texttt{WilsonBlockMajorant}).

This is our realization of the manuscript's extraction idea, not an attribution
to v14. It cannot be transferred to v14's $P_{\mathrm{ext}}$ from the specified
range alone: \path{projectionRangeSpecification_does_not_imply_bound_two}
constructs an idempotent skew projection onto the same low-coordinate range
that violates the bound $2$. The positive transfer theorem
\path{coefficientL1_basisJetProjection_le} shows exactly what would suffice:
explicit basis and dual-jet matrices whose coefficient-column bounds multiply.
The construction attempt is certified in
\path{Dimension16WilsonExtractionCertificate}: F.4's on-shell relation
policy and O.9's off-shell policy disagree already on the displayed
dimension-$6$ divergence-square operator; the exact block quotient, lattice
representatives, finite basis, dimension-$16$ dual jets, biorthogonality, and
conditioning matrices are not supplied. Appendix~I's explicit jets concern
the separate observable projection $P^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\leq4}$. Hence the
extraction component is not a standing erratum repair, and $C_1$ is not derived.

\paragraph{Hard truncation does not descend to the actual trace-word quotient.}
The third round replaces schematic carriers by the exact object: the
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$, $b=2$ Wilson block with normalized Haar measure --- $81$
vertices, $216$ positive links of which $8$ are internal, $216$ plaquettes,
an internal spanning tree with $7$ co-tree links, exact boundary-plus-co-tree
coordinates, the gauge action, the normalized Wilson potential, and the
fixed-boundary product-Haar reference integral with its logarithmic effective
block action (modules \texttt{SU2WilsonBlockPilot},
\texttt{SU2MajorantRepairDecision}). On this object the question ``is
canonical-dimension truncation even well-defined on the gauge-invariant
quotient?'' has a negative, intrinsic answer. For any $U\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$
the Cayley--Hamilton identity gives $\Tr(U^2)=\Tr(U)^2-2$; writing
$a=2-\Tr(U)$ and $b=2-\Tr(U^2)$ (each of canonical dimension $4$), the exact
relation $b=4a-a^2$ yields
\[
  a^3b-4a^4+a^5 \;=\; 0,
\]
whose three monomials have construction-derived canonical dimensions $16$,
$16$, $20$. Truncation through dimension $16$ deletes only $a^5$; on the
explicit holonomy $\mathrm{diag}(i,-i)$ the truncated expression evaluates to
$-32$, while the exact left-hand side vanishes on every field. Hence the
semantic kernel is not stable under representative-wise hard truncation, no
function on the evaluated trace-word quotient agrees with that truncation,
and the corresponding descended extraction certificate is uninhabited
(module \path{SU2CayleyHamiltonTruncationObstruction}). The witness uses
only the Cayley--Hamilton relation --- no equations-of-motion quotient and no
boundary-extension convention --- so it applies to both of v14's relation
policies, and it refutes any uniform-in-$N$ architecture that requires
representative-wise hard truncation of redundant Wilson trace words.

round constructs the two natural repairs exactly. The full associated-graded
jet chart on the gauge-fixed block ($7$ co-tree links, $3$ Lie coordinates
each, hence $21$ coordinates) carries the symmetric-monomial basis of jets
through total degree $16$, with identity jet matrix and biorthogonal
coefficient duals; radial Taylor extraction at radius ratio $1/2$ has the
exact operator constant
$\sum_{d=0}^{16}2^{-d}=131071/65536<2$
(modules \texttt{SU2AssociatedGradedExtraction},
\path{SU2AssociatedGradedExtractionDecision}). Independently, the evaluated

  C_{\mathrm{proposed}}

whose two-source gain at depth $16$ is
$90832203/335544320\approx0.2707<1/3$, while depth $15$ does not close. This
is conditional bookkeeping: the fluctuation, cumulant, and recombination

on the actual map. Two exact theorems keep these
constructions honestly separated from v14's object: the full graded chart has

descendant; the faithful transfer sockets --- exact-Wilson, pure
associated-graded, and every RG-compatible extension --- are therefore
uninhabited under both relation policies. Consequently no corrected recursion

$\beta_J\geq\beta_{\min}$ and in the volume.

\paragraph{The test.}
We simulated the Wilson action for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and $\mathrm{U}(1)$ on
periodic $L^4$ lattices with $L=8,12,16$. Each measurement follows one
heat-bath sweep (Kennedy--Pendleton for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, von Mises for
$\mathrm{U}(1)$) and three overrelaxation sweeps; $500$ thermalization sweeps
precede $4000$ measurements ($3000$ at $L=16$), and errors are jackknife errors
over $40$ bins. The main observable is the energy of each $2^4$ block of a
$b=2$ step, the sum of its plaquette energies $1-\frac1N\mathrm{Re}\,\Tr\,U_p$,
through the correlation coefficient $\rho(R)$ of block energies at block
distance $R$ along an axis. For near-Gaussian block energies, bounded functions
of the standardized block energies, whose sup norms do not depend on $\beta$,
have covariances proportional to $\rho(R)$; so $\rho$ is what the tree-decay
theorem constrains. For $\mathrm{U}(1)$ we also measured the transverse photon,
$\sin\theta_{bc}$ at momentum $2\pi/L$ along $b$, and compare its cosh
effective mass with the massless lattice dispersion
$2\,\mathrm{arsinh}\,\sin(\pi/L)$. The $\beta\to\infty$ values of $\rho$ are
exact free-field values on the same tori. The programs are
\texttt{ym\_stopping\_scale\_mc.c}, \texttt{ym\_stopping\_scale\_free\_field.py}
and \texttt{ym\_stopping\_scale\_analysis.py}, next to this document; the last
lists the exact runs.

\paragraph{Findings.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item At $\beta=150$, the measured $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ plaquette energy and
    nearest-block correlation agree with the free-field predictions within
    the reported statistical errors. The plaquette energy agrees with the
    weak-coupling value $3/(4\beta)+O(\beta^{-2})$, and $\rho(1)$ agrees with
    the exact free value on all three lattices. The free field's correlations
    decay as a power law: on the $64^4$ torus
    $\rho(R)\approx1.9\times10^{-3}R^{-8}$, with $\rho(8)/\rho(9)=2.57=(9/8)^8$,
    so the local decay rate per block, $8\log(1+1/R)$, tends to $0$. A power
    law eventually exceeds any exponential, whatever $C$ and $c'$ are. Within
    the reach of the simulation ($R\leq4$) the two cannot be told apart: the
    infinite-volume free-field tail is a separate analytic statement, while
    extending it to $\beta=150$ is a perturbative extrapolation.
  \item At $\beta=2.5$, in the confining regime, the short-distance
    correlations are larger, not smaller: $\rho(1)=0.051$, and at block
    distance $2$ the $16^4$ lattice gives $\rho(2)=7.7(1.4)\times10^{-4}$,
    twenty times the free value. A mass gap governs only the asymptotic decay,
    so short-distance magnitudes do not separate the two regimes; the tail and
    the photon do.
  \item The weakly coupled $\mathrm{U}(1)$ photon measurements are compatible
    with the massless dispersion: the effective mass follows $2\,\mathrm{arsinh}\,\sin(\pi/L)$ at
    $\beta=150$ and $\beta=2$, while in the confined phase ($\beta=0.8$) it
    stays at $3.60$ for every $L$.
  \item Two-loop asymptotic scaling places the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ confinement
    scale ($\beta\approx2.5$) about $570$ steps of $b=2$ below $\beta=150$, a
    length factor of $10^{171.7}$. With the standard lattice values
    $m_{0^{++}}/\sqrt\sigma\approx3.8$ and $a\sqrt\sigma\approx0.18$ at
    $\beta=2.5$, the glueball mass at $\beta=150$ is about $10^{-172}$ in lattice
    units, so a uniform bound $m_Ja_J\geq c'\kappa$ at $\beta_J\geq150$ would
    conflict with that asymptotic-scaling estimate. This is an extrapolation,
    not a measurement or a rigorous exclusion at finite coupling.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{A structural observation: the abelian test.}
We found no step of v14 between the bootstrap and the tree-decay theorem that uses a
property distinguishing $\SUN$ from $\mathrm{U}(1)$. The one group-dependent
input is the drift coefficient, which vanishes for $\mathrm{U}(1)$; zero drift
only makes $\beta_j\geq\beta_{\min}$ easier to maintain. Read with
$G=\mathrm{U}(1)$, the argument would give exponential clustering with a
volume-independent mass for the weakly coupled four-dimensional $\mathrm{U}(1)$
lattice gauge theory, and so would the area law that v14's Appendix~N lists as
accessible by the same methods. Both are false: at weak coupling that theory
has a perimeter law and a massless photon (Guth 1980; Fr\"ohlich and Spencer
1982). A proof of the crossing step must therefore use a property that separates
the relevant non-Abelian trajectory from this Abelian test. The argument does
not prove failure for $\SUN$ at fixed finite coupling. It identifies the
passage from a weakly coupled measure to a KP-small gas over a block-product
reference as the step requiring additional justification.

\paragraph{A barrier suite, and the hierarchical lattice.}
A replacement for the stopping-scale step has to fail wherever its conclusion
is false: for weakly coupled $\mathrm{U}(1)$ in four dimensions, which has a
massless photon (Guth 1980; Fr\"ohlich and Spencer 1982), and for weakly
coupled $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ in five dimensions, which lies beyond a bulk phase
transition (Creutz 1979). It also has to be consistent with asymptotic
freedom, which places the four-dimensional mass at $\beta=150$ near
$10^{-172}$ (finding~4). The diamond hierarchical lattice, on which the
Migdal--Kadanoff recursion with block factor $2$ is exact, is a natural
testbed, because the whole flow can be computed there. One step raises the
plaquette weight to the power $2^{d-2}$ and then raises its normalized character
coefficients to the fourth power. We iterated it on character coefficients in
$110$-digit arithmetic (\texttt{ym\_hierarchical\_flow.py}). In five dimensions
both groups flow to weak coupling: $\beta$ doubles at each step for
$\mathrm{U}(1)$, and $1/t$ roughly doubles for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, with $t$ the
heat-kernel time. In four dimensions $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ crosses to strong
coupling: at weak coupling $1/t$ decreases by $1/8$ per step, so $\beta\approx
2/t$ decreases by $0.25$, against $4N^2b_0\log2=0.2575$ on the cubic lattice.
But $\mathrm{U}(1)$ crosses as well. Its coupling decreases at every $\beta$,
by $1.1\times10^{-5}$ per step at $\beta=1$, $6.1\times10^{-12}$ at $\beta=2$
and $1.0\times10^{-31}$ at $\beta=5$; the drift is $-C\beta^2e^{-a\beta}$ with
$a=15.7914$, which agrees with $8\pi^2/5$ to all digits computed. These calculations suggest confinement on the four-dimensional
$\mathrm{U}(1)$ hierarchical model at every finite coupling, with a doubly
exponential weak-coupling correlation length. The all-coupling assertion is
an extrapolation of the computed recursion, not a Lean theorem or a
uniform numerical certificate.
The hierarchical lattice fails the abelian test in four dimensions: a
weak-to-strong flow proved there cannot be the mechanism on the cubic lattice
unless the proof uses an ingredient that the hierarchical lattice lacks.

The rigorous calibration concerns the free lattice Maxwell field, whose
field strength is gauge invariant. Its identification with an appropriate
$\beta\to\infty$ scaling limit of compact $\mathrm{U}(1)$ is a separate
limit theorem, not supplied by this formalization. The Fourier symbol of
its $F_{01}$ two-point function, $(\hat k_0^2+\hat k_1^2)/|\hat k|^2$ with
$\hat k_\mu^2=|e^{2\pi ik_\mu}-1|^2$, equals $1$ on the punctured $k_0$-axis and
$0$ on the punctured $k_2$-axis, and it is continuous away from the origin. It
therefore agrees almost everywhere with no continuous function, whereas an
absolutely summable Fourier series converges uniformly to a continuous one. So
the two-point function is not absolutely summable and admits no bound
$K e^{-\mu|n|_1}$ with $\mu>0$ (\texttt{FreeMaxwellNonClustering}). The same
holds in every dimension $d\geq3$, in particular in five, and for the square of
the two-point function; for the free $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ field, with three
independent colour components, the connected two-point function of the
gauge-invariant observable $\sum_a(F^a_{01})^2$ is $6$ times this square by
Wick's theorem. No family of two-point functions converging pointwise
to the free one satisfies an exponential bound with uniform constants. The
lemma behind it applies to every two-point function whose symbol has
direction-dependent limits at zero momentum. It does not cover finite $\beta$,
where the masslessness of the photon is the theorem of Fr\"ohlich and
Spencer.

\paragraph{The crossing input, stated and calibrated.}
The missing input can be stated precisely and tested. Call a family of block
lattices, indexed by the volume, \emph{crossing} if at one block scale every
effective measure is a hard-core polymer gas over a block-product reference
measure whose activity bounds satisfy
$\sum_{\gamma\ni x}k_\gamma e^{4|\gamma|+\kappa\operatorname{diam}\gamma}\leq\varepsilon$
at every block $x$, with $\varepsilon e\leq1$, $k_\varnothing=0$ and $\kappa>0$, all
independent of the volume. This is the regime v14's tree-decay theorem
presupposes, with the Kotecký--Preiss norm written out. The Lean record
\texttt{CrossingInput} contains the bounds on the activities; that an actual
stopped Wilson or RG measure admits such a representation is part of what the
route has to supply. Under the bounds, for bounded measurable observables
$F_1,F_2$ on block sets $S_1,S_2$ with $S_1$ nonempty,
\[
  \big|\langle F_1F_2\rangle-\langle F_1\rangle\langle F_2\rangle\big|
  \;\leq\;3\,\|F_1\|_\infty\|F_2\|_\infty\,e^{3(|S_1|+|S_2|)}\,
  e^{-\kappa\,d(S_1,S_2)},
\]
uniformly in the volume and the polymer set (\texttt{PolymerGas.LatticeClustering},
\texttt{CrossingInput}). The proof uses no cluster expansion: Dobrushin's
criterion, implied by Kotecký--Preiss, gives nonvanishing partition functions and
two-sided single-removal bounds by induction on the polymer set; the effect of
removing a far set of polymers is bounded by an exponential supersolution of an
influence recursion; and the truncated two-point function decomposes, by
conditioning on the support of $F_1$, into families that stay away from $S_2$ and
families that bridge to it (\texttt{PolymerGas.Basic}, \texttt{PolymerGas.Decay},
\texttt{PolymerGas.Measure}, \texttt{PolymerGas.Clustering},
\texttt{PolymerGas.Lattice}). The input is calibrated by the barrier. Take bounded
observables on block sets of bounded size whose separation, for each fixed
displacement $k$, eventually exceeds $\alpha|k|_1-\beta$ with $\alpha>0$ as the
volume grows. No crossing family can have two-point functions of such
observables whose limits dominate the field-strength two-point function of the
free lattice Maxwell field, since the uniform bound would pass to the limit
(\path{CrossingInput.false_of_tendsto_dominating}). On the tori the
geometric hypotheses hold for single links, with slope $1/d$
(\texttt{CalibrationSeparation}), so only the convergence and the domination of
the two-point functions remain as hypotheses. For $0<t\leq1/2$ the
expression $e^{-t\sigma^2}(e^{tC(n)}-1)$, the truncated two-point function of the
phase $e^{i\sqrt t F_{01}}$ and its conjugate for a centred Gaussian field of
covariance $C$, controls $|C(n)|$ up to the factor $2e^{t\sigma^2}/t$, because
$|C(n)|\leq1$. For the free field itself the calibration is complete. Let the
centred Gaussian field $F_{01}$ on the tori have the periodic Maxwell
covariance; its covariances converge to the free ones as the volume grows (a
Riemann-sum argument for the bounded symbol, continuous away from zero). Then no
crossing family reproduces its two phase marginals $e^{\pm iF_{01}/2}$ and their
joint expectation at every volume (\texttt{FreeFieldNoCrossing}). Not formalized
are the $\beta\to\infty$ limit that would put weakly coupled four-dimensional
$\mathrm{U}(1)$ and the abelian components of five-dimensional $\mathrm{SU}(2)$
near this field, and the persistence of weak coupling at every scale for those
theories. For
four-dimensional $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, reaching a regime with these bounds by
running the coupling from weak to strong is the proposed mechanism; establishing
it is the open crossing.

The input is not vacuous. Take the tori $(\mathbb{Z}/L)^d$ with $L\geq3$,
independent link variables, and any measurable plaquette potential $\phi$ with
$0\leq\phi\leq2$; the Wilson action $1-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U_p/N$
with Haar measure is one. The connected plaquette expansion writes
$\prod_pe^{-\beta\phi_p}$ exactly as a polymer density over link sets. For
$0\leq\beta\leq\beta_0(d)=e^{-18}/\bigl(4(4d^2+1)(16d^2+1)^2\bigr)$ its
activities satisfy the per-block smallness with rate $1$ and $\varepsilon=e^{-1}$
at every volume, and the Wilson expectations cluster exponentially, uniformly in
the volume (\texttt{StrongCouplingCrossing}). The ingredients are the expansion
(\texttt{PolymerGas.PlaquetteExpansion}), the bound $(\Delta+1)^{2n}$ on connected
sets of $n$ plaquettes through a given one in a graph of degree at most
$\Delta$ (\texttt{PolymerGas.ConnectedCount}), and the explicit threshold
(\texttt{PolymerGas.PlaquetteSmallness}). The threshold is far from sharp. This theorem supplies a nonvacuous
strong-coupling endpoint for the route; the weak-to-strong entrance and
continuum consequences require the additional inputs in \S\ref{sec:remains}.

\paragraph{Candidate mechanisms for the crossing.}
What four-dimensional $\SUN$ has and four-dimensional $\mathrm{U}(1)$ and
five-dimensional $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ lack is a short list: a positive one-loop
coefficient in the dimension where the coupling is dimensionless, gluon
self-interaction, a finite centre with $\pi_1(\SUN/\mathbb{Z}_N)=\mathbb{Z}_N$, and
instantons, $\pi_3(\SUN)=\mathbb{Z}$. Only the first is specific to four
dimensions; the others are present in five as well. We screened candidate
mechanisms built on them against the barrier suite, the calibration at
$\beta=\infty$, and two further theorems, one of which can be tested at finite
coupling. In the order tested:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Thresholds on one-scale marginals}, such as the mean block
  energy, the probability that a block exceeds the small-field cutoff of a
  multiscale analysis, or the law of a blocked plaquette. Refuted as sufficient
  conditions, formally, whenever the admitted one-site law has a bounded
  observable of nonzero variance: the product measure and the diagonal measure,
  in which every site carries the same $\mu$-distributed value, have the same
  marginal at every site, but the first has no correlations at distinct sites
  and the second has the covariance at every pair, whatever their distance
  (\texttt{MarginalBlindness}). The stopping-scale test of this section is of
  this kind: it can refute a crossing but not certify one.
\item \emph{Reflection positivity, infrared bounds, chessboard estimates.}
  Refuted: the free lattice Maxwell field and weakly coupled four-dimensional
  $\mathrm{U}(1)$ are reflection positive and do not cluster. These methods
  prove long-range order, not decay (Fr\"ohlich, Simon and Spencer 1976;
  Fr\"ohlich and Spencer 1982).
\item \emph{Decimation bounds and their interpolation.} Refuted by the abelian
  test on the hierarchical lattice. There the twist free energy of one coarse
  plaquette, $F=-\log f(-1)/f(1)$, with $f$ the effective plaquette weight and
  $-1$ the centre element (the flux $\pi$ for $\mathrm{U}(1)$), behaves as
  follows (\texttt{ym\_hierarchical\_flow.py}). In four dimensions, for
  $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ from $t=0.5$, it falls by about $1.2$ per step,
  $15.4\to14.2\to\cdots\to1.61$, and then collapses: $0.665$, $0.063$,
  $7.9\times10^{-6}$, $2\times10^{-21}$. For $\mathrm{U}(1)$ from $\beta=1.5$ it
  stays at $6.709$ to four digits over twelve steps, but its coupling falls at
  the exponentially small rate found above, so it collapses as well. In five
  dimensions it doubles at every step for both groups. An argument that
  derives the collapse without using the size of the drift proves it for
  $\mathrm{U}(1)$ too, which is false on the cubic lattice.
\item \emph{Centre decompositions.} Writing $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ link variables as
  $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ variables together with $\mathbb{Z}_2$ plaquette variables uses
  only a central $\mathbb{Z}_2$, which $\mathrm{U}(1)$ also has, with
  $\mathrm{U}(1)/\mathbb{Z}_2\cong\mathrm{U}(1)$. Refuted as a mechanism by the
  abelian test: what separates the groups is the dynamics, not the
  decomposition.
\item \emph{The twist free energy.} Survives the barrier suite numerically,
  and is tied to the crossing input by a theorem. On the torus $(\mathbb{Z}/L)^d$
  let the plaquettes $(x;0,1)$ with $x_0=x_1=0$, one in every $(0,1)$-plane,
  carry the coupling $b$ and all others $\beta$. Then $Z(-\beta)$ is the
  partition function with a twist through every $(0,1)$-plane, and
  \[
    F(L)=-\log\frac{Z(-\beta)}{Z(\beta)}
    =\int_{-\beta}^{\beta}\langle E_{\rm stack}\rangle_b\,db,
  \]
  with $E_{\rm stack}$ the sum of $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U_p/N$ over those
  plaquettes. In a polymer representation built from plaquette factors, a change
  of variables that multiplies link variables by the centre element moves the
  twist, and polymers away from the twist do not see it, so only the integrated
  activities of polymers that meet every position of the twist change
  (\path{TwistDiagnostic.differ_of_movable}). If two representations under
  common Kotecký--Preiss bounds differ only on polymers that meet a cut $Y$ and
  have diameter at least $D$, then
  $\left|\log\frac{|Z_{\rm tw}|}{|Z|}\right|\leq2|Y|\varepsilon e^{-\kappa D}$
  (\texttt{PolymerGas.Twist}). So a twist-compatible crossing family, with cuts
  of polynomial size and separations growing linearly in $L$, has $F(L)\to0$,
  and a family whose twist free energy stays away from zero admits no such
  twist-compatible representation (\texttt{TwistDiagnostic}). That moving the twist is such
  a change of variables, and that distant polymers do not see it, are
  hypotheses there. For the connected plaquette expansion of Wilson-type
  measures with a central involution $z$ (the centre element $-1$ of
  $\mathrm{SU}(2)$), a $z$-invariant probability measure on link variables and a
  measurable plaquette function with values in $[0,2]$ they are proved, and at
  strong coupling the twist free energy of the Wilson measure, with both
  partition functions genuine Wilson integrals, tends to zero as the volume
  grows (\texttt{StrongCouplingTwist}). The calibration at
  $\beta=\infty$ needed a limit; this one can be measured at the coupling of
  interest. Taking direction~$0$ as time, the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ Fourier transform in
  the twist gives the free energy $-\log\tanh(F/2)$ of one unit of electric flux
  along direction~$1$ ('t~Hooft 1979), so $F\to0$ from above means that this
  flux costs unboundedly much. This relation and the thermodynamic-integration
  formula above are standard identities used for the numerics, with $Z(b)$ as
  defined there; they are not part of the formal statements.

  In one respect the twist is specifically non-abelian. In $\mathrm{U}(1)$, for
  $\beta\geq0$, every twisted configuration has action at least $2\beta L^{d-4}$
  in the twisted planes, and the uniform one at most
  $\frac{\pi^2}{2}\beta L^{d-4}$ (\texttt{TwistCost}). In $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ the matrices $i\sigma_1$ and
  $i\sigma_2$ anticommute, so with $i\sigma_1$ on the links of direction~$0$ in
  one column, $i\sigma_2$ on those of direction~$1$ in one row and the identity
  elsewhere, every plaquette of the twisted planes has maximal weight and their
  twisted action vanishes (\texttt{TwistEater}); extended to the other
  directions, with the identity on the remaining links, it makes every other
  plaquette the identity as well. So the classical minima give $\mathrm{SU}(2)$
  no action cost of the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ kind: whatever twist free energy there
  is comes from the integrals themselves, including their fluctuations. We measured $F(L)$ by heat-bath Monte
  Carlo, integrating in $b$ by Gauss--Legendre quadrature
  (\texttt{ym\_vortex\_free\_energy.c}, \texttt{ym\_vortex\_driver.py}); errors

  position carries most of $F$; there we used a composite rule with $24$ to
  $32$ nodes in that window. The plain $16$-point rule underestimated $F$ there
  by up to $20\%$ at $L=8$.

  In the confined phases, four-dimensional $\mathrm{U}(1)$ at $\beta=0.9$ and
  five-dimensional $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ at $\beta=1.5$, below its bulk transition,
  $F$ vanishes within errors. In the Coulomb phase of four-dimensional
  $\mathrm{U}(1)$ it does not depend on $L$ within errors: about $2.84$ at
  $\beta=1.1$ and $5.2$ at $\beta=1.5$, that is $\frac{\pi^2}{2}\beta_R$ with
  $\beta_R\approx0.58$ and $1.05$. For five-dimensional $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ above
  the transition it stays near six and does not decrease: $5.17$, $5.98$ and
  $6.14$ at $\beta=1.8$ for $L=4$, $6$ and $8$, and $5.96$ and $6.74$ at

  $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ it decreases with $L$ at every coupling: at $\beta=2.3$ from
  $0.88$ to $0.02$ between $L=4$ and $L=8$, at $\beta=2.5$ from $1.72$ to $0.38$
  between $L=4$ and $L=10$. At $\beta=3.0$, where these volumes are small in
  physical units, it is of order one, far below the $\frac{\pi^2}{2}\beta\approx
  14.8$ that a classical cost would give. So the diagnostic separates
  four-dimensional $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ from both barrier theories, in which $F$
  stays away from zero, as is compatible with failure of the twist-compatible crossing input.
  The implication is only necessary: failure of crossing alone does not
  force nonvanishing twist free energy.
\item \emph{The running coupling.} Twist eaters exist in five dimensions too,
  so they do not by themselves make $F$ vanish. In four dimensions the
  coupling runs, and the data follow it: $F$ decreases slowly at $\beta=3.0$, from $2.85$ to $2.41$ between $L=4$ and
  $L=8$, and falls to zero at $\beta=2.3$, where the same $L$ are larger in
  physical units. In five dimensions weak coupling persists and $F$ does not
  decrease. This
  is consistent with a flow in which $F$ decreases with $L$ at every coupling
  and vanishes once $L$ is large compared with the confinement scale; it is
  evidence, not proof. Vanishing twist free energy would discharge a necessary diagnostic,
  not the crossing criterion itself. A proof of the required activity and
  observable bounds on the weak-coupling trajectory remains open.
\item \emph{Assessed, not tested here.} Instantons: a dilute instanton gas
  produces no area law, and its integral over sizes is governed by the same
  running coupling. The Gribov horizon: it is specific to non-abelian groups,
  but it is formulated after gauge fixing, and no route is known from
  gauge-fixed propagators to clustering of gauge-invariant observables.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The constants.}
Two of v14's displayed constants were discussed in
\S\ref{sec:recombination}: the two ledgers for $C_1$ and the
extraction-projection bound. A third is the drift coefficient. v14 writes
$\beta_{j+1}=\beta_j-4Nb_0\log b+E_j$ with $b_0=11/(3\cdot16\pi^2)$ and states
