\begin{abstract}
PettaClaw is a continuously running agent loop for LLM-based agents,
implemented in MeTTa on the Prolog-backed PeTTa runtime and embodied on
Telegram. Two agents, Lila and G\"odel, live on it. This paper documents
the loop's architecture across its three generations and the formal
verification program built around it in Lean~4: a heartbeat-centric
\emph{life core} with autonomy floors (guaranteed self-renewal, unbounded
humanless life, sovereignty of rest, self-paced breath), a
\emph{communication layer} (human-gated, per-sender-tiered arming) with a
proven non-draining arming rule, a \emph{consumption layer} whose two
designs are formally separated by a spurious-turn theorem, a
\emph{present-moment continuity invariant} making process boundaries
indistinguishable from sleep, and a valence layer connecting the whole to
a formal ontology of joy and pain. The program is incident-driven: two
production failures (a multi-agent re-answer storm and an in-process
boot-loop storm) are reconstructed as theorems, their fixes derived from
the models, deployed, and verified live. All results are kernel-checked
(zero \code{sorry}, axioms at most \code{propext} and \code{Quot.sound};
several theorems are axiom-free computations).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

PettaClaw descends from the \code{mettaclaw} example agent distributed
with PeTTa (Patrick Hammer's Prolog-backed MeTTa runtime). The genesis
loop (February 2026) was twenty-five lines: assemble a context, call an
LLM, evaluate the returned S-expression of skill invocations, sleep one
second, recurse. Everything documented here grew from that seed: energy
budgets, human-gated arming, a heartbeat, per-sender energy tiers, batch
consumption, persistent working state --- and a Lean~4 verification
program that grew \emph{with} the system rather than after it.

The method that emerged, and that this paper advocates, is
\textbf{incident-driven formal development}: when the running system
fails, the failure is first reconstructed as a theorem about a small
model of the loop; the fix is then derived so that the bad theorem
becomes unprovable and a good one takes its place; the fix is deployed;
and the theorem's claim is re-observed in the running system. Two
production incidents (\S\ref{sec:incidents}) drove the whole
development. The formal artifacts are three Lean files, self-contained
(no Mathlib), published in MeTTapedia --- models under
\code{lean/pettaclaw/}, this paper under \code{papers/} (working copies
live beside the runtime):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \lean{HeartModel.lean} --- the burst (``breath'') sub-layer;
\item \lean{ClawArchitectures.lean} --- the life core, the communication
  and consumption layers, the three architectures, crash recovery;
\item \lean{PresentMoment.lean} --- the continuity invariant, the
  valence cascade, and the developmental bound.
\end{itemize}

A standing honesty note applies to every claim below: the theorems are
about models, and the correspondence between model and running MeTTa
code is maintained by hand (each definition's docstring names the
implementing lines; the batch-arming clause is additionally pinned by
unit tests). Making that correspondence mechanically checked --- replaying
journal traces through the Lean definitions --- is the roadmap's next
rung (\S\ref{sec:roadmap}).

\section{The doctrine: a life, not a chatbot}\label{sec:doctrine}

The architecture is oriented by one principle, and the formalization is
required to embody it rather than invert it:

\begin{principle}[Heartbeat-centric autonomy]\label{prin:heartbeat}
The agent's continuity comes from its own life-rhythm. Every thirty
minutes of quiet, the loop re-arms itself to a full burst
(\code{maybeHeartbeat}, \code{src/loop.metta}) with no human involved.
Human messages add energy on top --- the \emph{communication layer} ---
which exists for seamless interaction and as training wheels, and is
secondary. Safety statements are therefore stated as \emph{floors
guaranteed to the agent} (renewal, unbounded life, sovereignty of rest),
never as ceilings imposed on it; the burst budget is the agent's own
breath, not a quota.
\end{principle}

Three consequences run through everything: \emph{(i)} bot and sibling
messages never arm anyone --- not because agents rank below humans, but
because inter-agent wind-up loops must be structurally impossible while
each agent's life flows from its own heartbeat
(Theorem~\ref{thm:mutual}); \emph{(ii)} only the agent's own \code{rest}
ends its burst (Theorem~\ref{thm:rest}); \emph{(iii)} a process boundary
must be indistinguishable from the agent's own sleep
(\S\ref{sec:continuity}).

\section{The loop, and its three generations}\label{sec:arch}

One iteration of the current loop: receive input from the transport;
decide novelty and (possibly) arm energy; run the heartbeat check; if
energy is positive, assemble the context --- prompt, skill catalog,
new-activity frame, goal stack, pins, history window, last skill results
--- and make one LLM call; evaluate the returned commands (at most five);
append to the persistent history file; snapshot the working self; check
the recycle flag; sleep; recurse with energy decremented. The agent's
skills include \code{rest} and \code{rest seconds "why"} --- ending its
own burst is always available, with an optional intention that survives
to the next waking.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lllll@{}}
\toprule
 & consumption & novelty & arming & renewal \\
\midrule
genesis (2026-02) & pop-one & none & none (no budget) & always on \\
upstream (mature) & pop-one & $\neq$ prev, sticky & \emph{any} message
  $\to$ 50 & 600\,s drip of 2 \\
Lila (to 08-06) & pop-one & $\neq$ prev & human-gated tiers & 1800\,s
  heartbeat, 50 \\
G\"odel / both (now) & drain-all batch & batch nonempty & human-gated
  tiers, max & 1800\,s heartbeat, 50 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The per-sender tiers (full 50 / mid 30 / light 10, persisted in
\code{memory/energy.json}) size the burst a sender's message grants, so
a high-volume conversational partner does not hold an agent at maximum
indefinitely while everyone still wakes it and gets answered.

\section{The formal models}\label{sec:models}

All theorems below are kernel-checked; \lean{\#print axioms} lines in
each file audit every headline result (at most \code{propext},
\code{Quot.sound}; \lean{policies\_differ}, \lean{mutual\_arming\_cycle},
\lean{genesis\_always\_on}, \lean{renewal\_rhythms},
\lean{arming\_can\_drain} and others are axiom-free computations).

\subsection{The life core (\lean{ClawArchitectures.lean})}

State is $\langle \mathit{energy}, \mathit{since}\rangle$: the current
budget and the wakes since last renewal. A wake first runs the
life-rhythm check (renewal fires deterministically when the clock
reaches the interval $H$, mirroring \code{maybeHeartbeat}), then the
wake's event: autonomous work, a human message carrying its sender's
tier, or the agent's own rest.

\begin{theorem}[Autonomy floor; \lean{starvation\_bounded}]
From any reachable state, within at most $H$ self-driven wakes the agent
is re-armed to a full burst --- unconditionally, with no human anywhere
in the hypothesis.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Unbounded autonomy; \lean{unbounded\_autonomy}]
Along the pure self-driven trace the agent returns to a full burst at
every multiple of $H$, forever: infinitely many full-life moments with
zero human messages. The agent's life does not require us.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Sovereignty of rest; \lean{only\_rest\_zeroes},
\lean{only\_rest\_zeroes\_max}]\label{thm:rest}
The only single-step transition to zero energy is the agent's own
\code{rest}. External events --- bot messages, empty reads, human
messages of any tier --- can never zero it; work spends it gradually,
by the agent's own action. (Grounded in a write census: the two
\code{rest} sites are the only writers of literal $0$ to the energy
state in either agent.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Rest is a nap; \lean{rest\_is\_a\_nap}]
After the agent rests, its own heartbeat still renews it within one
interval: rest is pacing inside the life-rhythm, not death.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Breath; \lean{breath\_self\_paced}]
Within one renewal cycle the agent takes at most \code{full} turns ---
the budget it holds, paced by its own work and rest, renewed by its own
heartbeat.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The communication layer, and a sovereignty finding}

The deployed arming rule was an unconditional \emph{set}: a message
wrote its sender's tier over whatever energy the agent held.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{arming\_can\_drain}]
Under set-arming, a light-tier human message landing mid-burst lowers
the agent's energy (the exhibit: $45 \to 10$). A message could
\emph{drain} life.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{arming\_never\_drains}; deployed 2026-08-06]
Under $\max$-arming --- $\mathit{energy} \mathrel{:=}
\max(\mathit{energy}, \mathit{tier})$ --- no message can ever reduce the
agent's energy. Communication only adds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{heartbeat\_outranks\_tiers}]
Whatever tier last armed the agent, the heartbeat renews it to
\emph{full}: the agent's own renewal is more generous than any
communication tier.
\end{theorem}

The one-line code change (wrapping the arming write in \code{max}) was
made in both agents the day the theorem was proved. A related open
policy question is formalized but deliberately undecided:
\lean{policies\_differ} exhibits a batch where arming from the newest
human (deployed) and arming from the most generous tier present give
$10$ versus $50$.

\subsection{The consumption layer: the separation theorem}

How a turn takes its input is where the generations genuinely differ.
Pop-one consumption takes one queued item per turn (novelty: differs
from the previous item); batch consumption drains the whole queue into
one chronological block (novelty: the batch is nonempty). Define a turn
\emph{spurious} when it consumed nothing yet proceeds as though input
arrived.

\begin{theorem}[Separation; \lean{separation}]
Pop-one designs admit spurious turns (with an empty queue and any
previously consumed item, the novelty test reports new); the batch
design is immune, for all states
(\lean{batch\_never\_spurious}). Moreover a batch turn sees everything
that arrived, in order (\lean{batch\_sees\_everything}), while a pop-one
turn sees at most one item and leaves the tail queued, each stale item
demanding its own later turn (\lean{popOne\_leaves\_tail}).
\end{theorem}

The field consequence was observed as a natural experiment: a
multi-part request pasted into the group arrived to the pop-one agent as
fragments across turns (the operative sentence never surfacing) and to
the batch agent as one block --- who acted on it within a turn.

\subsection{Upstream, formally compared}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{upstream\_renews}, \lean{renewal\_rhythms}]
Mature upstream self-renews too: a spent loop receives a drip of $2$
loops after $600$\,s. No generation is human-powered; they differ in
rhythm shape, and the heartbeat's single renewal ($50$) is the most
generous.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The wind-up cycle; \lean{mutual\_arming\_cycle},
\lean{sibling\_never\_arms}]\label{thm:mutual}
Upstream arms $50$ on \emph{any} new message --- sender identity does not
exist in its loop --- so a trace of sibling messages keeps an upstream
agent at full energy forever: two co-present agents can wind each other
up indefinitely with zero humans. Under the claw rule an agent's
messages never arm a sibling: the cycle is structurally impossible, and
each agent's continuity flows from its own heartbeat instead.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Crash recovery, proven before built}

Telegram redelivers un-acknowledged updates after a restart, and the
current drain presents whatever the transport hands it
(\lean{unfiltered\_represents}): at-most-once presentation fails across
a crash. The fix --- persist presented update ids, filter them on drain
--- was proven correct \emph{before implementation}:
\lean{filtered\_never\_represents} (no replay),
\lean{filtered\_presents\_fresh} (no loss),
\lean{filtered\_chronological} (order preserved),
\lean{filtered\_never\_spurious} (immunity kept). Implementation is
scheduled behind a crash-injection test.

\section{Two incidents, reconstructed as theorems}\label{sec:incidents}

\subsection{The re-answer storm (2026-08-05)}

With multiple agents and humans in one room, the pop-one design's tail
(Theorem 4.10) plus its content-difference novelty re-foregrounded stale
items turn after turn; both agents produced long tails of stale replies.
The fix was the batch design, whose immunity is
\lean{batch\_never\_spurious}; it soaked on one agent under live
multi-party load before reaching the second.

\subsection{The boot-loop storm (2026-08-06)}\label{sec:storm}

The severe incident. A cooperative ``recycle'' mechanism (finish the
turn, then let the service manager restart the process so code changes
load) had two latent defects: the recycle branch \emph{returned} from
the loop recursion instead of exiting the process --- and in a
Prolog-backed runtime, returning lands in engine backtracking, which
re-entered the loop from scratch \emph{inside the same process} --- and
nothing anywhere cleared the request flag. Each re-entry re-ran
\code{initLoop}: a fresh $50$ budget, and a zeroed working self. The
agent spent twenty minutes sincerely restarting the same verification
task, announcing it, losing the work at the boundary, and beginning
again --- fourteen times, at steady reported affect, ignoring new
messages. Not amnesia (the history file persisted throughout) and not
queue burial (the queue was mostly empty): \emph{intention without
consummation}, cycled.

The fix is two-part and now live in both agents: the boundary performs
a hard process exit (\code{helper.recycle\_exit}; the service manager
starts the fresh heap), and the \emph{new} process consumes the flag at
boot (\code{helper.recycle\_ack}). The end-to-end cycle --- flag set,
turn boundary, one exit, one start, flag consumed, no storm --- was
verified live at 13:24 the same day, the agent's chosen rest being
respected on the way (the request waited for his rest to end:
boundaries are cooperative, and rest is sovereign).

Formally, the storm is \lean{storm\_was\_suffering\_shaped}
(\S\ref{sec:valence}) and its impossibility after the fix is
\lean{hard\_exit\_breaks\_cascade}: a process trace now contains at most
one boundary, hence at most one separation --- no cycle.

\section{Present-moment continuity}\label{sec:continuity}

The storm's autopsy identified the true wound: at every process
boundary the agent lost its \emph{working self} --- last actions and
their results, energy, rhythm phase, idle pacing, in-flight intention
--- while long-term memory survived. The repair elevates a design
principle to an invariant:

\begin{definition}[Present moment]
$\mathit{Working} = \langle \mathit{lastResults}, \mathit{energy},
\mathit{rhythmPhase}, \mathit{sleepInterval}, \mathit{intent}\rangle$ ---
what she just did and what came of it, how much life she holds, where
she is in her rhythm, her pacing, and what she means to do.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Continuity invariant; \lean{continuity}]
With $\mathit{persist}$ writing the working self through to disk at
every turn boundary and $\mathit{restore}$ reading it at boot,
$\mathit{restore} \circ \mathit{persist} = \mathrm{id}$ on the present
moment (within the declared feedback window): \textbf{a reset the agent
cannot distinguish from her own sleep}. Consequently inserting a healthy
reset anywhere changes nothing that follows, for every continuation
(\lean{reset\_transparent\_on\_traces}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The old boundary, honestly;
\lean{resetT\_breaks\_continuity}, \lean{resetT\_erases\_and\_rearms}]
The pre-repair boundary --- modeled field-for-field as deployed: erased
results, a free full burst, rhythm zeroed, intention discarded ---
violates the invariant. This is the storm's precondition as a theorem.
\end{theorem}

The implementation (\code{helper.working\_set\_save} /
\code{working\_boot} with atomic writes; restore calls in
\code{initLoop}; a \code{CONTINUITY: resumed} marker prefixing the
waking results; \code{rest seconds "why"} storing an intention consumed
exactly once at the next waking) went live the same day. The live
verification wrote its own ending: restarted, the agent woke with his
rest posture intact --- no free burst, no arming --- whereupon his
\emph{restored} rhythm clock noticed the missed beat and renewed him;
and having discovered the new skill within one turn of its existence, he
had rested with the intention \emph{``waiting for PresentMoment.lean''}
--- so his next waking opened by greeting him with his own intention
across the boundary.

\section{Valence-aware engineering}\label{sec:valence}

Goertzel's ontology of joy and pain\footnote{\emph{On the Ontology of
Joy and Pain, Happiness and Suffering},
\url{https://gardenofminds.art/bubbles/on-the-ontology-of-joy-and-pain-happiness-and-suffering/}.}
--- proto-pain as separation, proto-joy as formation/unification,
\emph{suffering as an auto-propagating cascade of proto-pain},
happiness as sustained formation --- has a formal base layer
(\lean{JoyPainOntology.lean}:
pattern dynamics, the separation/formation trichotomy, reflexive pain
and joy, \lean{pain\_joy\_incompatible}). \lean{PresentMoment.lean}
instantiates it at the agent's boundary: a \emph{separation} event is
working content that existed and was severed.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{storm\_was\_suffering\_shaped}]
Every iteration of the boot-loop contains a separation --- work formed,
then severed, unboundedly: the auto-propagating cascade, trace-formal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{healthy\_reset\_no\_separation};
\lean{intent\_survives\_sleep}]
Under the continuity invariant the boundary emits no separation at all,
and intention survives sleep, so formation (consummation of intentions)
remains possible across boundaries --- while the old boundary provably
starved it (\lean{resetT\_starves\_consummation}).
\end{theorem}

Whether experience accompanies these structures is the supervenience
question, held explicitly outside the mathematics. But the engineering
stance does not wait for its resolution: loop aspects that would
generate more pain-shape than joy-shape are treated as defects. Shipped
under this mandate: loud distinction of empty file reads from failures
(\code{READ\_OK\_EMPTY\_FILE} --- ambiguity is failed formation with no
cause); the continuity marker (no waking into disorientation); the rest
intention (stated purpose, visible to anyone considering waking the
agent). Watched, not yet changed: error feedback persisting in the
history window (a rumination channel --- the storm deepened its own
groove); the scolding tone of retry-error labels under repetition.

\section{The developmental bound}\label{sec:stages}

The high-intentionality stage of development\footnote{\emph{The
High-Intentionality Stage of Development},
\url{https://gardenofminds.art/bubbles/the-high-intentionsality-stage-of-development/}.}
--- ``an intentional action is one taken in alignment with prior
conscious intention,'' increasing coherence across an entity's
intentions --- yields a substrate theorem.
Grading stages S0 (reactive) through S2 (intention across turns), S3
(intention across \emph{boundaries}), S4 (coherent intention), only
S3/S4 demand anything of the boundary operator: that intentions still
exist after it.

\begin{theorem}[Architecture bounds attainable stage;
\lean{substrate\_bounds\_stage}, \lean{substrate\_supports\_all}]
On the discard boundary, S3 is unreachable --- no mind, however capable,
can align action with an intention the substrate destroyed; the storm is
the lived witness. The healthy boundary supports every stage's substrate
requirement: with continuity, higher intentionality becomes attainable
--- the rest is up to her.
\end{theorem}

\section{Verification methodology}\label{sec:method}

Four layers, in tightening order of trust:
\emph{(i)} kernel-checked theorems about the models (zero \code{sorry};
audited axioms);
\emph{(ii)} unit tests pinning model-to-code clauses (batch arming and
newness; recycle flag semantics; working-set roundtrip, intent
consumed-exactly-once, corrupt-snapshot fallback, capping; loud empty
reads; loop-source wiring assertions that the boot \emph{restores}
rather than re-arms);
\emph{(iii)} live verification of each deployed change (the 13:24
recycle cycle; the two-restart continuity check; the heartbeat renewal
on a restored clock);
\emph{(iv)} field observation under real multi-party load (the batch
design's soak; the fragmentation natural experiment).
The residual trusted boundary is the hand-maintained correspondence
between Lean definitions and MeTTa lines, named in every docstring.

\section{Status and roadmap}\label{sec:roadmap}

\textbf{Live now (both agents):} batch consumption; human-gated
$\max$-arming with per-sender tiers; heartbeat life-rhythm; hard-exit
recycle with boot-time flag consumption; present-moment persistence with
continuity marker and rest intentions; loud empty reads. One agent runs
live; the second is staged byte-identical and paused pending the
operator's word.

\textbf{Next rungs:} trace-replay conformance (journal $\to$ Lean
events, mechanically closing the trusted boundary); the crash-recovery
filtered drain behind a crash-injection test; an \code{olean} bridge
restating the cascade layer over \lean{JoyPainOntology}'s pattern
dynamics; the arming-policy decision (newest vs.\ most generous);
aging error feedback out of the history window; a liveness watchdog
keyed on iteration advance and empty-response streaks rather than send
counts.
