\begin{abstract}
A proof framework can host another faithfully while changing the size of its
certificates, the work of its checker, and the representation of its models.
We separate these obligations and describe their composition. The proposed
organization is a resource-sensitive graph of configured proof systems,
whose simulations induce preorders after choosing a resource class. MM0
and HOL Light supply explicit-certificate reference systems; Lean supplies
an important computational comparison. Binding, theory composition,
operational observations, and resource-bounded reasoning are connected
without identifying proof adequacy with semantic completeness. These notes
state a theory to develop, distinguish existing Lean results from remaining
joins, and give positive examples and boundaries.
\end{abstract}

\section{Four costs and three kinds of correctness}
Consider a theorem with a short Lean proof using a substantial definitional
computation. The stored proof can be small because the checker performs that
computation. Expanding it into explicit elementary justifications can move
work from the checker into the certificate and its producer. This is a
possible trade-off, not a universal compression theorem. Explicit equality
proofs can also be short, and a different encoding may share the work.
Deduction modulo provides precise examples of proof-length speed-up
through computation~\cite{burel}; results about arithmetic proof speed-up
depend on their specified theories and proof measures~\cite{buss}.

There are four separate accounts:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Discovery:} search for a certificate, including tactic work.
\item \emph{Storage:} physical bytes of statements, certificates, signatures,
  dependency tables, and shared libraries.
\item \emph{Translation:} execution of an actual certificate compiler.
\item \emph{Checking:} execution of the receiving checker.
\end{enumerate}
Undecidable search is compatible with decidable checking and short proofs
of some instances. This is already true in first-order logic; higher-order
syntax is not the explanation for undecidability by itself. Nor does any
of these facts give an algorithm for finding all short proofs quickly.

Three correctness statements must likewise remain distinct:
\emph{certificate preservation}, \emph{derivability reflection}, and
\emph{semantic soundness or completeness}. Their different quantifiers are
the central issue of the next two sections.

\section{Primitive discipline and executed resource class}
A kernel profile has two distinct coordinates. Its \emph{primitive discipline}
records the operations admitted at the selected observation: rule
instantiation, syntax comparison, scope and dependency checks, substitution,
explicit equality inference, or implicit conversion. Its \emph{resource class}
records proved bounds on the implemented checker under a specified physical
encoding. The observations may be rule-level calls or smaller machine
instructions, but the level must be stated.

The source-derived HOL Light inference interface supplies a concrete first
case. \code{HOLLight.CheckingDiscipline} records a rule-level operation view
of its ten existing inference requests. The required basis is minimal for
this fixed view: a basis covers all ten requests exactly when it contains
the six required operations. A basis excluding term substitution or explicit
BETA admission does not cover the interface. BETA admission is an explicit
rule request, not an implicit normalization service; none of the ten
requests exposes implicit normalization. This classification excludes the
separate axiom and declaration-history interfaces, and proves no instruction
lower bound or running-time theorem.

\paragraph{Minimal inventories and generating bases.}
The word ``minimal'' has several distinct meanings. An inventory is least
for an observer when every covering inventory contains it. A generating
basis is inclusion-minimal when removing a rule destroys its ability to
derive the required rules. A least generating basis would additionally have
to be contained in every sufficient basis. Logical weakness, measured by
assumptions or model classes, and resource efficiency are further orders;
none follows from counting primitive names.

Rule generation is defined in \code{Logic.InferenceBasis} using derivations
from each rule's premises. Cut substitutes derivations for those premises.
Generation is equivalent to preservation of consequences under all open
hypotheses, and composes. A finite candidate set has a minimal generating
subset; irredundancy characterizes minimality.

Two concrete bases are given in \code{InferenceBasisControls}. One uses zero
and successor; the other uses one, predecessor and addition by two. Both are minimal within their common union and have
the same consequences, but there is no least sufficient subset under literal
rule inclusion. These are constructed controls, not minimality theorems for
MM0 or HOL Light.

\code{InferenceBasisBoundary} proves that primitive elimination is not
computable in general. One rule asserts zero; the other requires checked
finite evidence that a supplied program halts. Both local witness checks
are decidable. The assertion rule can be removed exactly when the program
halts. The positive control is a terminating zero program; the negative
control searches forever for zero from a constant-one program. Finite
existence of a minimal basis therefore supplies no general algorithm for
finding it.

\code{Logic.InferenceBasisCompiler} provides explicit proof templates with
indexed premise holes. Filling the templates with compiled children gives
accepted target certificates with the original conclusions. Its interface
states functional correctness; computability and executed clocks require
realizations of its template function. The concrete control compiles
zero/successor proofs into one/predecessor/add-two proofs and exactly doubles
the tree-node count. Repeated holes charge repeated child occurrences;
this construction makes no implicit graph-sharing claim. Node counts,
serialized bytes and machine work remain different quantities.

\paragraph{Actual binding guards.}
The MM0 and Metamath profiles observe their original witness interfaces.
MM0 includes simultaneous substitution, sorts, dependency preservation,
explicit conversion witnesses and fresh dummy checks. Metamath includes
substitution, typecodes and distinct-variable constraints. In both actual
checkers, concrete substitutions pass the typing checks but violate the
independence guard. Distinct images supply positive controls; coincident
images and missing declared independence supply negative ones. Thus these
guards do substantive work. Their necessity under removal does not prove
that the corresponding whole kernels are semantically irredundant or
physically linear-time.

\code{KernelCheckingClasses} independently defines linear checking from the
original executed clock on complete encoded packets. Linear checking implies
polynomial checking. A linear exact translation followed by a linear checker
also supplies linear checking, including refused inputs. The derived factor
charges the source machine's syntactic push bound, the written target packet,
and the physical handoff. With only a polynomial translation bound, the
corresponding conclusion is polynomial checking. Mutual exact polynomial
simulation preserves that checking class without identifying exact costs,
primitive disciplines, axioms or proof-search strategies.

Exact authority alone does not give polynomial equivalence. The existing
unary and binary evenness presentations have identical Boolean acceptance
and exact authority for the same predicate, but translation to the unary
certificate format cannot have a polynomial output-size bound. The new
\code{KernelComparisonBoundary} exports this as a counterexample to collapsing
all exact checkers with polynomial declared time accounts into one size
class. These accounts are not silently promoted to original-machine clocks.
A universal least kernel therefore needs a stated class and comparison
relation; it cannot follow merely from effective checking or expressive reach.

\paragraph{Searching partial checkers.}
The input to \code{EffectiveKernelSearch} is a finite code for a partial
checking program. Acceptance means that evaluation of the exact claim/certificate pair
returns the designated value $1$. A finite bounded-evaluation witness makes
checking a submitted candidate primitive recursive. Fair search enumerates
both candidate certificates and their bounds, so a divergent candidate cannot
trap the search. Its successful domain is exactly the set of claims accepted
by that program. Soundness transfers found evidence to semantic truth;
semantic completeness is a separate hypothesis. A non-recursively-enumerable
semantic validity predicate has no effective checker exact for all its claims.
The supplied evaluation bound is not an original-machine time bound.
The concrete \code{NonEffectiveKernelBoundary} case supplies effective finite
evidence for halting but excludes a sound and complete effective finite
evidence language for all nonhalting claims, even with a partial checker.
This does not exclude sound proofs of particular nonhalting claims or regular
infinitary certificates within an effectively checked scope.

\code{FiniteCertificateSearch} enumerates all binary words of length at most
$n$, with exactly $2^{n+1}-1$ candidate entries. Exhaustion excludes this
finite collection. A separately proved sufficient proof-length bound turns
exhaustion into underivability. A size simulation transports a target search
radius, but this does not make exhaustive search polynomial or synthesize an
efficient proof compressor.

\code{HOLLight.EffectiveKernelSearchBridge} connects successful partial-checker
search to the existing executable evidence extractor and shared HOL graph
compiler. The same fixed finite hosting theory accepts exactly the quoted
checking-code evaluations. Its reflection theorem covers arbitrary accepted
HOL certificates, not only this compiler's output. Source derivability and
model truth still require independent code-to-source authority. The executable
controls include a successful $761$-receipt graph and refusal of a changed
root, wrong source result and insufficient evidence. No polynomial expansion
in the original source proof is asserted.

\paragraph{Heuristics and standard truth.}
\code{HeuristicEvidence} screens arbitrary submitted certificates with the
original checker. Soundness requires no correctness or completeness of the
producer. Screening composes over batches while retaining each accepted
occurrence. Empty results exclude only those submissions. For every fixed
sound effective checker of nonhalting, a true nonhalting claim has no
accepted certificate; even an abstract oracle proposing arbitrary finite
certificates cannot close that gap without changing the checker. The
quantifier is ``for each checker, some missing truth,'' not ``one truth
unprovable in every sound theory.''

\code{HOLLight.KernelAtlas} joins screened HOL graphs, the actual HOL-to-MM0
certificate compiler, its established structural size bound, and truth in
the constructed full-domain model. The individual carrier is explicitly
equivalent to the ordinary natural numbers. No new executed translation
clock is supplied. Eta provides a concrete model-valid sentence without a
certificate in the \emph{axiom-free} configuration; adjoining ETA changes
that configuration. The example distinguishes exact hosting from semantic
completeness without questioning sound proofs about standard models.

\paragraph{Finite budgets and explicit conversion evidence.}
\code{FuelledConversionEvidence} observes the actual finite-code evaluator
with three outcomes: accepted, completed refusal, and exhausted. Completed
results persist at larger bounds. Every result of the original partial
program eventually appears at a finite bound, including refusals. This is
completeness for that program's evaluation relation; it does not establish
termination or semantic completeness. Evaluator depth is not an executed
machine clock or a Lean heartbeat count.

The modeled Mathdata normalizer has its own monotonicity and completion
theorems in \code{FuelledConversionProfile}. Successful
comparisons produce articles accepted by the original explicit conversion
checker. Its fixed-pass successful comparison is transitive. By contrast,
\code{FuelledConversionWitness} supplies a selected definition-unfolding
search whose one-step recognizer accepts $a\sim b$ and $b\sim c$ but cannot
find $a\sim c$ until its budget is enlarged. The checked paths themselves
compose with additive length budgets and reverse without losing their
original witnesses. A completed failed search excludes that selected route;
it is not a proof of inequivalence under every conversion rule.

A concrete raw environment with a self-referential definition exhausts every
unfolding bound. This is a boundary for arbitrary supplied environments, not
an admitted acyclic theory history or a counterexample to termination of a
typed fragment. The source converter's counters also differ from modeled
pass fuel. The inspected Megalodon source uses a lazy converter and resource
checks; Proofgold's pass loop has a distinct zero-budget convention, for which
\code{strictPass_offset} proves the exact offset for the modeled pass
operation~\cite{megalodonsource,mathdatapfgsource}.

\paragraph{Digest identifiers and certificates.}
\code{CertificateDigestBoundary} proves that exact replay based solely on a
digest exists set-theoretically precisely when original acceptance is constant
on each digest fibre, separately for every claim. An accepted and rejected
certificate with the same digest prevents such replay. The converse uses
classical representative selection and gives no efficient algorithm. A
certificate for computation, an authenticated cache and a cryptographic
argument consequently need their own correctness and trust contracts.

\section{Configured checkers and faithful hosting}
\begin{definition}[Configured effective checker]
A configuration consists of an effectively represented signature and theory
$\Theta$, effectively represented claims $J_\Theta$ and finite certificates
$P_\Theta$, and an implemented Boolean checking algorithm
$\proofCheck_\Theta(j,p)$. Unless explicitly relaxed, the algorithm terminates on
\emph{all} inputs, including malformed certificates. Define
\[
 \Prov_\Theta(j) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists p\in P_\Theta,\ \proofCheck_\Theta(j,p)=\mathsf{true}.
\]
The implementation's termination and correspondence to a mathematical
checker are additional verification obligations.
\end{definition}
For a partial implementation, use the relation ``there is a finite
accepting run'' instead. Enumeration then dovetails certificates and
execution bounds; an unfinished run is not a rejection. We must use this
variant whenever total termination of the chosen implementation is not
established. This distinction is relevant to computational conversion,
not just to search.

An MM0 theory is a configuration of a framework. A HOL theory configures a
fixed HOL kernel. OpenTheory supplies an exchange and theory-composition
layer for the HOL family. Lean also includes a computational conversion
discipline. Comparisons of these systems therefore quantify over declared
configurations; they do not compare unqualified bare kernels with arbitrary
axioms silently supplied to one side.
For a uniform framework translation, also give a computable map
$\Phi$ from admitted source configurations to admitted target
configurations. Its initialization time and emitted declaration size
are charged separately or included in the variable input. A collection
of fixed-theory proof bounds is not automatically a uniform bound for
compiling arbitrary \code{.module} declarations.

\begin{definition}[Hosting data and obligations]
For configured systems $S,T$, give a claim translation
$\tau:J_S\to J_T$ and certificate compiler
$E:J_S\times P_S\to P_T$. The following are independent obligations:
\begin{align*}
 \proofCheck_S(j,p)=\mathsf{true}
   &\ \Longrightarrow\ \proofCheck_T(\tau j,E(j,p))=\mathsf{true},
     &&\text{certificate preservation;}\\
 \Prov_T(\tau j)&\ \Longrightarrow\ \Prov_S(j),
     &&\text{global derivability reflection.}
\end{align*}
Faithful hosting satisfies both, so
$\Prov_S(j)\Longleftrightarrow\Prov_T(\tau j)$.
\end{definition}

The first statement concerns outputs of $E$. The second concerns
\emph{every} target proof of an encoded source claim, including proofs
that $E$ could never produce. For example, interpreting an authored
calculus's patterns as data and its predicate $\mathsf{holds}$ as source
derivability proves reflection by a model argument. It does not supply an
efficient algorithm extracting a source proof from an arbitrary HOL proof.

A further, useful certificate condition is
\[
 \proofCheck_S(j,p)=\mathsf{true}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \proofCheck_T(\tau j,E(j,p))=\mathsf{true}.
\]
This catches accepted translations of rejected source inputs. It still
does not replace global reflection: a target might prove an encoded claim
by some other certificate. Conversely, faithful hosting alone does not
force $E$ to preserve rejection of every malformed input. We require
both conditions where a public translator promises exact rejection.

\section{Resource grades and composition}
Fix physical encodings and let $n=\size{j}+\size{p}$ count input bytes or
bits consistently. Resource assertions include the configuration's size
when it is variable input; fixed-theory bounds may put it in constants.
Do not charge a variable module library as a free oracle.

A resource grade $(a,f,g)$ bounds the translated claim size, certificate
size, and \emph{executed} translation work:
\[
 \size{\tau j}\le a(n),\qquad
 \size{E(j,p)}\le f(n),\qquad
 \mathsf{steps}_{E}(j,p)\le g(n).
\]
The third inequality must follow from a machine executing $\tau$ and $E$
on the same physical representations. A separately declared function
named ``translation time'' establishes no such connection. All bounds
state whether their input domain includes refusal paths.

\begin{proposition}[Composition of resource bounds]
Let $E_1:S\to T$ and $E_2:T\to U$ be simulations with nondecreasing grades
$(a_1,f_1,g_1)$ and $(a_2,f_2,g_2)$. Suppose their physical handoff has a
proved cost bound $h$. Put $m(n)=a_1(n)+f_1(n)$. The composite has bounds
\[
 a_2(m(n)),\qquad f_2(m(n)),\qquad
 g_1(n)+h(m(n))+g_2(m(n)).
\]
Certificate preservation and global reflection each compose.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The second machine receives at most $m(n)$ units of data. Monotonicity
gives its two size bounds and running bound; the first run and handoff
are also charged. Acceptance preservation composes by implication.
Reflection applies first to $T\to U$, then to $S\to T$.
\end{proof}

Thus polynomial size and executed-time simulations compose when the
machine handoff is polynomial. Identity copying is an actual linear-cost
implementation, not a zero-cost axiom. Suitable grades form a preorder
after quantifying over available translations. Quotienting by simulations
in both directions gives equivalence classes \emph{at that resource grade}.
Multiple incomparable grades or trust policies give a graph rather than a
single numerical ranking. The richer proof-relevant category retains the
chosen translations; its preorder forgets that evidence.

For the implemented stack-machine model, polynomial output size can be
derived from executed polynomial time. Let $c_M$ be the maximum number of
syntactic pushes along a command branch, over the machine's finite labels.
For a run on input $x$ taking at most $t$ transitions,
\[
 \size{\mathsf{output}}\le\mathsf{peakCells}\le\size{x}+c_Mt.
\]
The refined peak follows primitive mutations inside commands and all stacks,
including scratch cells released before the next transition. A macrostep may
contain several pushes; the checked three-push control rules out silently
setting $c_M=1$. A second control pushes and pops within one transition:
its transition-state peak is zero, while its refined peak is one.
Finite internal alphabets preclude hiding an arbitrary term in one cell.
Native byte allocation and representation overhead require further refinements.

The original sequential-composition machine also has an exact storage law.
Both component executions end with their scratch arenas empty, and each
transfer step moves a symbol from one stack to another. Consequently its
transition-state peak is
\[
 S_{\mathrm{sequence}}=\max(S_{\mathrm{first}},S_{\mathrm{second}}).
\]
The handoff takes exactly $2\size{\mathsf{middle}}+2$ transitions and keeps
exactly $\size{\mathsf{middle}}$ cells throughout those transition states.
A checked doubling-then-erasure run returns an empty word in thirty-four
transitions but reaches four cells. Its peak is four, whereas the sum of
component peaks is eight. This equality concerns transition states;
the intra-command refinement remains a distinct measure.

Cook--Reckhow p-simulation conventionally compares proof systems for a
fixed language such as propositional tautologies and requires a
polynomial-time proof translator~\cite{cook}. Our cross-logic comparisons
also need the claim and theory translations. Reaching every recursively
enumerable predicate with a suitably configured MM0 theory is not a proof
that MM0 is a p-optimal proof system for tautologies. Different configured
theories can assert different claims. Likewise, polynomial equivalence
permits large constants and degrees; it does not imply equally short
certificates or practical interchangeability.

\section{Coupling proof search to proof checking}
Fix a configured checker $S$, its physical certificate encoding, and a
costed machine. A proof producer $A$ is a partial algorithm which, on
input $j$, may produce a certificate $p$. Its discovery time $D_A(j)$
includes failed candidates, intermediate checks, indexing, scheduling,
and the final validation. Soundness requires that every reported result
is accepted by $S$. Completeness means that $A$ eventually reports a
result for every $j$ with $\Prov_S(j)$. These properties do not require
termination on other inputs. A finite budget returning no proof therefore
returns a pending search state; it is not evidence of nonderivability.

\paragraph{Certificate and policy measures.}
For each claim, distinguish the least certificate length
\[
 L_S(j)=\min\{\size{p}:\proofCheck_S(j,p)=\mathsf{true}\}
\]
from the checking work of a particular certificate, $V_S(j,p)$, and
from $D_A(j)$ for a particular producer. An empty minimum is $\infty$.
The nondominated pairs $(\size{p},V_S(j,p))$ describe the certificate
trade-off for $j$. They need not be computably identifiable: finding a
better certificate does not certify the absence of an even better one.
Code length of a \emph{producer} is a fourth measure; it is not the length
of the certificate that the producer eventually writes.

\begin{proposition}[A bound which would decide derivability]
Suppose certificates are finite words over a finite alphabet and checking
terminates effectively on all inputs. If $\Prov_S$ is undecidable, there
is no total computable function $b(j)$ such that every derivable $j$
has an accepted certificate of length at most $b(j)$. Likewise, a sound
and complete producer cannot have a total computable time bound $t(j)$
for all its successful searches.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With the first bound, enumerate the finitely many words of length at
most $b(j)$ and check them all. An accepted word decides the positive
case, and their exhaustive rejection decides the negative case. All
checks terminate. With the second bound, simulate the producer for
$t(j)$ steps; a validated result decides the positive case, and the
absence of one decides the negative case. Each construction contradicts
undecidability. Neither argument asserts that every individual proof is
long, or that every individual successful search is slow.
\end{proof}
Total checking is essential here. A partial verifier could accept a
fixed one-bit certificate only after simulating a machine to termination.
The certificate is short while the supposed checking operation carries
the unresolved halting search. This is precisely why finite accepting
runs and total checkers must be distinguished.
The formal partial-recursive producer result uses the original
\code{Code.evaln} evaluation budget. It proves that a computable budget
sufficient for every positive query would decide the original meaning,
and that increasing the budget preserves an accepted observation. This
budget is not identified with a TM2 step count. Separate executed-search
theorems use the original TM2 runs and charge their actual steps.

\paragraph{Bounded search and complexity classes.}
A declared length bound $\ell$ gives a finite exhaustive search with
at most $\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}q^i$ candidates for an alphabet of size $q$.
If every such check costs at most $v(j,\ell)$, its time is bounded by
the number of candidates times that cost, plus enumeration and copying.
This is an upper bound for that search policy, not a lower bound for
every possible producer. A fixed claim language with polynomially short
certificates and polynomial-time checking lies in NP; this hypothesis
does not hold for unrestricted undecidable theoremhood. Checking a
supplied proof in polynomial time is polynomial in \emph{its input},
which includes the proof, not automatically in the claim length alone.

SAT makes the distinction concrete: one satisfying assignment witnesses
satisfiability, whereas a tautology asserts truth under every Boolean
valuation. Propositional completeness does not imply short proofs of every
tautology. A polynomial-time proof system with polynomial-size proofs of
all tautologies exists exactly when $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$~\cite{cook}.
For an explicitly fixed finite domain, quantifiers can be expanded into
finite conjunctions and disjunctions, potentially with large growth. This
does not identify first-order or higher-order model expressiveness with
propositional expressiveness on arbitrary or infinite domains.

\paragraph{Search transport requires executable witnesses.}
If a source producer returns $p$, certificate preservation gives a
workflow producing a target proof of $\tau j$: retain the source query
$j$, run its producer, compile $p$, and check $E(j,p)$. On a common
sequential cost model, with accounted handoff cost $h$, its time is bounded by
\[
 D_A(j)+g(\size{j}+\size{p})+h(j,p)
       +V_T(\tau j,E(j,p)).
\]
Separate machine models require additional proved simulation overhead.
This constructs one source-guided target policy. A native target producer
receiving only $\tau j$ additionally needs an effective query decoder
and its accounted cost; neither follows from a computable forward map.
It does not bound the fastest target policy or the shortest target proof.
Conversely, global reflection
$\Prov_T(\tau j)\Rightarrow\Prov_S(j)$ supplies no efficient source
producer. A reverse search bound requires an executable certificate
extractor, its acceptance theorem, and its cost. Enumerating source
proofs until one appears gives eventual extraction under reflection,
but no useful bound follows from that argument alone. Thus two
derivability-equivalent presentations may retain very different proof
search geometry.
The implemented transport theorem also derives the produced-packet size
from input size and discovery time by the stack-growth inequality. A
producer cannot emit an enormous physical certificate within a small
executed time bound. This implication does not assert that discovering a
short certificate is cheap.

\paragraph{An observed budget is not a decision procedure.}
The finite-budget observer distinguishes a completed accepting run, a
completed refusing run, and a still-live configuration. Its exact threshold
is the number of original machine transitions, established from the
original step function. For a witnessed producer, compiler and checker,
the joined route has the executed upper bound
\[
 D+2\size{\mathsf{sourcePacket}}+2+C+
   2\size{\mathsf{targetPacket}}+2+K.
\]
The two transfers are performed by the same physical composition used in
the translation theory. Packet lengths can additionally be bounded from
input length and the programs' actual writing capacity. A checked
nonidentity route finishes in exactly seventeen transitions: a budget of
sixteen leaves it live. An independent control accepts one certificate and
refuses another for the same positive claim. Neither refusal nor a live
observation refutes theoremhood. These observer theorems count simulated
original transitions; an executed universal interpreter requires its own
overhead theorem. Candidate enumeration is accounted for separately below.

The binary finite-search instance now supplies an actual bounded enumeration
loop. Its depth budget is unary physical input; the current path, pending
branches and every emitted candidate remain on finite-alphabet stacks.
The enumerator follows the original finite certificate order, then transfers
the full framed enumeration to the original checker loop. Its executed bound
includes generation, both passes of the complete handoff, every candidate
check, field copying and the final verdict map:
\[
 E(b)+2\size{\mathsf{enumeration}(b)}+
 \sum_{p\in\mathsf{wordsUpTo}(2,b)}(K(p)+2\size{p}+4)+14.
\]
The result agrees with bounded certificate existence, and its finite-budget
observation distinguishes completion from expiry. This construction charges
refused candidates and the potentially exponential enumeration storage.
It does not avoid materializing the enumeration or give polynomial time in
a binary-coded depth bound.

A second executed route returns the original bounded search's first
accepted certificate. It retains each actual candidate while running the
original checker, frames the decision together with that candidate, and
then scans the resulting word for the first success. Its output tag
distinguishes an accepted empty certificate from exhaustion. The selector
drains the remaining input, so the clock charges every candidate check,
the retained words, both physical transfers and final selection. Finite
budget observation agrees with actual completion; readout recovers the
original certificate and hence the configured checker's source meaning.
This route still checks all candidates before selection. It establishes
neither early stopping nor a universal scheduling bound.

A third executed route stops checker calls at the first accepted candidate.
For rejected prefix $P$, first success $p$, and unread suffix $U$, its
singleton-response caller clock is
\[
 \sum_{q\in P}(K(q)+6\size q+9)
 +K(p)+7\size p+\size{\mathsf{frame}(U)}+11.
\]
The suffix is physically discarded, so it still costs bytes to clean up,
but its checker executions need not terminate or even be supplied.
An exhausted vector costs one final transition. A separate theorem joins
this caller to the existing enumerator and charges $E(b)$, the complete
handoff, and the caller clock. Thus early checking does not eliminate eager
candidate-generation cost. Independent readout of any completed run returns
the original bounded search result and inherits only the configured
checker's supplied soundness. Eleven physical caller controls and their
one-transition-short observations pass, including success before a
later divergent candidate; divergence before success remains pending.
The joined enumerator passes twelve complete executions and twelve
one-transition-short observations. At depth five, the positive example
finds the same one-bit certificate after 33 checker calls; exhaustive
refusal takes 63. This preserves the existing depth-first order and makes
its discovery cost visible even when the accepted certificate is short.

\paragraph{Universal scheduling links the two costs.}
Levin-style search schedules candidate \emph{programs}, not just
certificate strings. In a suitable prefix-coded machine and scheduling
model, a candidate producer $a$ receives weight comparable to
$2^{-\size{a}}$. The search pays for generating its proposed witness
\emph{and verifying it}; its comparison bound has the characteristic
factor $2^{\size{a}}$ times their combined work, with the scheduler and
simulation overhead specified. A slow verifier can therefore dominate
even when a candidate producer is fast. This does not decide negative
instances or eliminate enormous constants~\cite{hutter}.

Hutter's proof-guided algorithm selection is a further connection:
it searches for proofs that programs compute the required function and
that their execution has a stated time bound. Its guarantee is relative
to the programs and bounds provable in the chosen background theory,
and includes the time to compute those bounds and large proof-dependent
additive terms~\cite{hutter}. This is not a uniform solution to theorem
search and not a claim that there is a best unrestricted algorithm.
The proofs authorizing a faster policy are themselves products of
search, checking, and a declared logical authority.

\paragraph{Reuse and amortization.}
For a produced proof reused $k$ times, write the whole cost as
\[
 \underbrace{D_A(j)}_{\text{discover once}}+
 \underbrace{T_E(j,p)}_{\text{compile once}}+
 \sum_{i=1}^{k}\bigl(H_i+V_i\bigr).
\]
Initialization, reading, cache validation, and effectful work belong in
the relevant terms. With unchanged authority and snapshot, a compile-once
route with per-use checking cost $v_T$ beats direct checking cost $v_S$
only if its additional setup and translation cost is less than
$k(v_S-v_T)$. If $v_T\ge v_S$, this checking-time argument gives no
break-even point. Storage and peak space remain separate comparisons.

\paragraph{Positive and negative controls.}
Explicit equality and substitution witnesses can replace a checker's
repeated normalization, provided a correctness and cost theorem justifies
the replacement. Verified caching can reuse a checked shared lemma.
In contrast, merely delaying a producer or verifier by a computable
amount changes discovery or checking time without changing accepted
claims or certificate lengths. A one-off translation can also cost more
than the checking work it saves. These examples separate the resource
measures; they establish no performance claim for a particular backend.

\section{The explicit base and computational compression}
An explicit base checks supplied proof-relevant steps. It still parses,
compares terms, follows references, performs substitution, and validates
scope. MM0's explicit design does not mean that its implementation does
no computation~\cite{mm0}. HOL Light's equality rules similarly expose
inference steps while implementing term transformations.

For a deterministic effective checker, a finite accepting execution can
be supplied as a trace. A checker of fully written consecutive
configurations can validate the trace without rediscovering the run.
This motivates the general explicitization theorem: host a finite
presentation of the machine's transition relation, prove its trace
judgment adequate, and compile the trace certificate. Such a theorem
must include the accepting configuration and exact statement linkage;
merely proving a machine can halt is insufficient.

The resulting certificate can be polynomial in the \emph{written trace}
and nevertheless enormous relative to the original proof. This is how a
trace-explicit base can be operationally universal without erasing resource
differences. Composition of elaborators also collapses arbitrary counts
of intermediate layers; raw elaboration depth cannot alone define a
strict hierarchy.

The executed interface also admits non-polynomial per-packet clocks.
These translations compose through the same physical handoff and can
feed an exact target checker. A proved polynomial clock recovers the
polynomial interface; its output-size bound follows from actual writing
work. Both relations are preorders under the same replay/reflection policy,
and every polynomial route is an executed route. A loose upper bound is
not a machine lower bound: separation needs an independent argument such
as unavoidable output growth.

\paragraph{A checked normalization separation.}
In the existing Mathdata model, set $u_0=a$ and
$u_{n+1}=(\lambda x. f\,x\,x)\,u_n$, with fixed well-typed primitives.
The bottom-up normalizer produces the full binary tree $v_n$ in one
changing pass, followed by a stable pass. The original instrumented
elaborator records exactly $n$ beta contractions. Nevertheless, the
existing finite-alphabet term codec gives the exact equations
\[
 |\mathsf{encode}(u_n)|=1244n+305,\qquad
 |\mathsf{encode}(v_n)|+429=734\cdot2^n.
\]
The executed output-growth law therefore excludes every polynomial-time
machine emitting this full expanded representation from these inputs.
Pass fuel and contraction count do not measure the cost of traversing,
copying, or writing its payload. This is a theorem about expanded output,
not an inherent lower bound for Boolean conversion checking or a shared
output representation, and not a verification of source Megalodon.

The same family now has a checked shared representation using the original
chronological Pattern table. Four initial nodes and two application nodes
per level suffice: there are exactly $2n+4$ nodes, and complete binary
serialization, including reference addresses, has length at most
$512(n+1)^2$. The existing materializer reconstructs precisely the original
encoded $v_n$. Future children, future roots, and trailing symbols are
refused. At $n=8$, the expanded wire contains 187,475 symbols and the
shared wire 1,684 symbols. Their size ratio is unbounded. Emitting the
full tree serialization from these shared packets still admits no
polynomial-time machine. This lower bound concerns physical expanded
output; it does not assert that the materializer's heap representation,
graph checking, or Boolean conversion must expand that tree. The executed
source-to-shared compiler remains a separate obligation.

\paragraph{Positive example.}
A saved lemma referenced twice needs one checked initializer and two
references. A graph-preserving compiler retains that sharing.
\paragraph{Negative example.}
Inlining a shared proof recursively can produce an exponential tree.
A bound in graph nodes is also insufficient if each node carries a
large term, a large binary reference, or a large context.

\paragraph{Equations.}
An explicit equality certificate can justify reflexivity, congruence,
transitivity, substitution, and individual definition-unfolding steps at
the base. Implicit normalization shifts proof-relevant work to checking.
Whether the result admits a polynomial explicitization is a theorem about
that conversion procedure, not a consequence of calling it conversion.
Unrestricted normalization can be costly~\cite{statman}; sharing and the
chosen reduction strategy matter to a reasonable cost model~\cite{accattoli}.

Adding arbitrary equations as axioms can change the derivable claims,
and can make a theory inconsistent. Replacing a derivable explicit
equality by a proved equivalent conversion mechanism need not add claims.
Both theories can remain recursively enumerable while proving different
statements. Recursive enumerability is not sameness of logical authority.

\section{Models, higher-order syntax, and binders}
The semantic comparison should use independently defined models and
satisfaction. For a model translation $R:\mathsf{Mod}(T)\to\mathsf{Mod}(S)$,
the institution-style condition is
\[
 M\models_T\tau(j) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R(M)\models_S j.
\]
Signatures, sentence translations, and contravariant model reducts must
commute with renaming and imports~\cite{institutions}. A certificate
simulation alone says none of this.

Henkin models restrict function domains while maintaining the closures
required by the chosen typed language and its terms. Full models use full
function spaces. Henkin completeness is a semantic theorem for a specified
calculus~\cite{henkin}; it does not follow just because another checker
accepts that calculus's proofs. Sufficiently expressive full second-order
validity is not recursively enumerable, so no effective checker is both
sound and complete for it~\cite{vaananen}. An effective calculus can still
be sound for full semantics. This restriction concerns full validity,
not every restricted fragment or every individual full-model theorem.

HOL's kernel can therefore have a first-order \emph{presentation of its
checking judgments}, while the object logic has higher-order types and
quantification. Explicit substitutions provide direct precedent for such
presentations~\cite{holls}. That is not a claim that full HOL semantics
has become first-order semantics.

Binding alone does not require a computational tier. Names, de Bruijn
indices, nominal syntax, or a scoped binding signature can be finite data.
The substantive obligation is adequacy of typing, alpha-equivalence,
freshness, capture avoidance, and substitution under that representation.
Generic binding signatures and second-order metavariables offer reusable
abstractions~\cite{fpt,fiore2022}; declaring a binder does not automatically
prove the adequacy laws. Higher-order unification or implicit equality
solving introduces a further algorithmic obligation.

For HOL-to-MM0, one may encode binders as first-order data or use MM0's
native binder discipline. For calculus-to-HOL, one may encode syntax as
data or use host lambda binding. These choices require distinct adequacy
theorems. No ABT substitution node or particular runtime representation
is imposed as the definition of the intended general framework.

\paragraph{Conservative extensions.}
Lifting a definition into an explicitly listed axiom records an honest
theory dependency. It is not a proof that the enlarged theory is
conservative. Model expansion, consistency, and preservation of old
derivable judgments differ; model conservativity does not alone imply
proof conservativity without appropriate completeness~\cite{arthan}.
Whole polymorphic histories of definitions and type definitions need
their own transport and extension theorem~\cite{kumar}.

\paragraph{Finite evidence for standard infinite meanings.}
The atlas now has two exact, deliberately scoped examples.
\code{PresburgerUniversal} structurally normalizes Mathlib's existing
Presburger terms to affine forms. It decides universally quantified,
hypothesis-free affine inequalities over the ordinary natural numbers.
Acceptance is equivalent to truth for every natural valuation; refusal is
equivalent to existence of an actual countervaluation. This does not decide
arbitrary Boolean combinations or quantifier alternation.

\code{CertificateGSLTRootBuchiAuthority} checks a finite invariant region
and progress ranks for a fixed controller on a finite carrier. Existence of
accepted evidence is equivalent to legal admitted execution from the
selected root with accepting states visited beyond every finite prefix.
The invariant region excludes unreachable bad components, which would
otherwise obstruct completeness for a root-specific claim.
\code{StandardSemanticAuthorityProfiles} places the arithmetic and recurrent
claims under one exact authority family and projects checked edge witnesses
to genuine infinite GSLT execution. Its negative controls separate a winning
selected controller from a nonrecurrent alternative path, and a real path
from invalid supplied evidence. These are not S1S, S2S or real-closed-field
decision procedures.

The unrestricted $\omega$-rule instead requires the whole infinite family
of premises. Finite induction or a certified cyclic argument may establish
particular universal claims, but does not validate every purported infinite
proof. For the recursive proof encodings studied by Frittaion, recognizing
valid $\omega$-proof codes is $\Pi^1_1$-complete~\cite{omegarule}. This lies
beyond the finite accepting-run boundary of ordinary partial checking; the
finite recurrence certificates above certify a restricted infinite meaning.

\section{Lean and further computational kernels}
Lean is not merely HOL with faster proof compression. Its dependent
types, universes, inductive families, quotient machinery, and configured
axioms also change the language and its foundational commitments. To
compare logical authority, first state the claim interpretation and the
assumptions under which it is sound. To compare compression, keep that
interpretation fixed and then compare certificates and checker work.

The Lean reference describes beta, delta, iota, zeta, and quotient
reduction, eta principles, and definitional proof irrelevance~\cite{leanref}.
These should become explicit, separately admitted conversion profiles in
a hosting construction. Reduction rules, the implemented conversion
algorithm, and axioms such as classical choice must not be amalgamated
into one unexplained ``higher tier.'' The independent Lean4Lean checker
and its partial correctness development are relevant inputs, not a
finished verification of the entire current kernel~\cite{lean4lean}.

The implemented algorithm and its resource limits require versioned source
claims. In Lean~4.33.1, \code{CoreM.maxHeartbeats} counts small allocations in
thousands; it does not count beta reductions. The kernel type checker has
system and recursion-depth guards in its inference and reduction
paths~\cite{leansource}. The generic bounded-evaluation results above do not
identify their bounds with these counters or verify this entire C++ kernel.
Undecidability of an ideal equality relation, incompleteness of an implemented
recognizer, exhaustion, and actual divergence must be established separately.

There are two possible statements with different acceptance criteria.
A \emph{run-replay theorem} turns an actual accepting run of the specified
implementation into an explicit accepted certificate. A \emph{declarative
adequacy theorem} identifies the accepted judgments with the intended
dependent type theory. Replay alone cannot exclude implementation bugs
in the algorithm being replayed. Conversely, declarative soundness does
not bound the runtime of a particular implementation.

Normalization and decidable conversion also need careful hypotheses.
Proof irrelevance does not by itself determine either result: closely
related systems have negative normalization results and positive
reduction-free normalization arguments~\cite{abelcoquand,coquand}.
We should isolate the precise fragment, equality principles, universe
rules, and algorithm rather than transfer a result by resemblance to Lean.

The Lean typed-boundary control accepts a raw beta step in an environment

\code{Lean4LessCheckerBoundary} structures state obligations; they are not

There are three distinct directions ``beyond Lean.'' First, a configured
checker may perform additional deterministic computation, certified decision
procedures, or reflection while preserving a specified judgment relation.
The questions are its executed time/space and the cost of writing an explicit
certificate. Second, additional universes, axioms, or reflection principles
can enlarge logical authority; this requires an interpretation and its
metatheoretic assumptions, independently of certificate compression. Third,
full higher-order validity can serve as a semantic specification beyond
effective complete checking. It is not another executable tier. These
choices do not entail a strict ordering of particular implemented systems.

protocol and error model, outside the present deterministic interfaces.

Further kernels, including rewriting frameworks and verified reflective
checkers, can be added as configurations and arrows in the same graph.
Each arrow retains its authority, representation, and cost conditions.
There is no requirement that every pair be directly comparable, or that
stronger assumptions always buy smaller proofs. Bootstrapping then means
checking particular implementation-correctness claims under explicit
metatheoretic assumptions, not making a system prove its own soundness
without an external justification.

\section{Modules and operational native types}
A \code{.module}/LanguageDef surface can be the single authoring source
for syntax, rules, named imports, and interpretations. Backend emissions
then present views of the same admitted theory. An MM0 printer and an
OpenTheory exporter should share declaration identities, dependency
ordering, scope, and checked module interpretations rather than create
parallel theory authorities. LF's adequacy discipline remains useful
when describing these representations~\cite{lf}.

There are two separate composition laws to prove: translation of a
compatible module join agrees with joining translated modules up to an
explicit renaming; and interpretation transport preserves the declared
derivations. Meet, replacement, definitions, and recursive imports do
not inherit these laws merely from notation. Conflict rejection and
future-reference refusal remain observable behavior.

For GSLT-to-MeTTa execution, the compared objects are operational systems
as well as checkers. The source and target property languages and evidence
families must be defined independently. Matching rule occurrences is
only the first obligation: premises, typed substitution, permissions,
contexts, equation classes, effects, and revisioned providers enter
the source-visible execution relation.

Finite reachability transport can use a source step represented by a
nonempty target path. A source-visible modal contract hides administrative
target steps at declared boundaries. Universal, normal-form, and
divergence-sensitive properties require more: internal progress or
termination, treatment of branching, and the appropriate backward
coverage law. These are stronger than acceptance preservation.

Truth-valued observations can miss two identical results carried by two
different rule occurrences. Evidence families retain positions,
bindings, provenance, order, and multiplicity. An effectful failed branch
can also retain output. A proof of ``at most one answer'' does not license
discarding an executed printing branch. A bounded execution should return
remaining obligations rather than assert that no result exists.

\subsection{Presentations, formation, and conversion}
A local native-type cube is indexed by a presentation and a displayed
operational site. A machine presentation can hide equation lookup behind
a premise, whereas a program presentation exposes a callable head inside
a context. The two interfaces can consequently have different slots.
An interpretation between these presentations must earn its operational
and native-type transport laws; neither an equal cube nor an equal set
of accepted claims supplies those laws. Conversely, faithful semantic
transport need not identify the slot sets or select one uniquely
canonical presentation.

The proved slot-count comparison in \code{SiteInterfaces} counts index variables
in addition to the rely-and-output slots of \code{Slots.modalFamily}.
For the displayed MM0 application site, these are distinct interface
descriptions. Their vertex counts must not be substituted for one
another. Target premises, output carriers, and the treatment of equations
also require a stated interface convention. The bare completion's lack
of closed classifiers is a property of that completion; it does not mean
that the object language lacks meaning or that its proof checker is
unsound. Constructor classifiers, closed classifier inhabitation, and
inhabitants of a classifier are separate questions.

The source module \code{TypeTheory.HypercubeBubbles} independently
parameterizes product formation and conversion. Its separation results
exhibit a polymorphic judgment requiring a formation rule that no added
conversion supplies, and a conversion judgment that increasing the
formation vertex alone does not supply. These results concern their
explicit pure-type-system presentation. An NIK profile therefore records
its presentation, formation rules, conversion policy, logical authority,
and executed resource grade separately; a vertex is not a numeric
verification tier.

For a proposed presentation bridge, the remaining obligations include
the source and target carriers, legal substitutions, administrative-step
boundaries, correspondence of indexed sites, preservation and reflection
of the chosen observations, evidence transport, and the execution cost
of these maps. Forward possibility, the predecessor-quantifying right
adjoint of OSLF's diamond, and the forward universal-successor modality
must have distinct contracts. A finite search's silence licenses none
of their negative claims without the corresponding completeness witness.
The cited interface and formation/conversion sources have been inspected;
their recent changes are not included in this paper's joined resource
checkpoint or silently imported into its qualification claim.

\subsection{Independent source checkers and optional typed representations}
The foundation here consists of independently specified source judgments and
checkers for a guarded atom-rule calculus, the GSLT-metalanguage command

Their mathematical validity does not depend on choosing a Prime candidate.
A representation in another calculus is a separate comparison result:
it neither defines the source checker nor completes its implementation.
Prime remains an unsettled family of design candidates.

\paragraph{Weakness and efficiency as different objectives.}
Bennett's weakness criterion concerns the generalization of hypotheses under
an explicitly specified task model; it is not a theorem identifying a
smallest proof kernel~\cite{bennettweakness}. Goertzel's efficient pragmatic
intelligence explicitly accounts for computational resources relative to
environments and goals~\cite{goertzelefficient}. These motivate separate
questions here: which assumptions suffice, which inference operations suffice,
and what discovery, compilation and checking cost on the intended workload.
A proof of one optimum does not identify the others.

\section{Resource-bounded reasoning as a consumer}
Goertzel's treatment of pragmatic intelligence explicitly accounts for
resource use and discusses multiple objectives~\cite{goertzel}. The
connection here is a proposed engineering use of the preceding theory:
a reasoning agent chooses among certified presentations, proof producers,
and backends while keeping the logical contract fixed.

For a workload $w$, consider the vector
\[
 (\mathsf{searchTime},\mathsf{translateTime},\mathsf{checkTime},
   \mathsf{bytes},\mathsf{peakSpace},\mathsf{authorityAssumptions}).
\]
An admissible choice supplies the required preservation, reflection, and
observation evidence. Pareto comparison can then discard choices dominated
on that workload; a declared policy chooses among the remainder. Actual
cost observations and proved bounds play different roles. This defines
a possible decision discipline, not a theorem of general intelligence or
a universally optimal proof strategy. Discovery costs can dominate even
when certificates and checking are small.
The implemented finite-route frontier is deliberately narrower. It starts
with one source packet, an already discovered certificate, and a finite
family of exact executed compilation/checking routes. Its coordinates are
complete target packet symbols, proved translation and checking clocks,
and the joined execution's actual peak cells. Every nonempty feasible
family has a nondominated route; the selected route retains its source
authority and satisfies the composed clock and peak bounds. Increasing
the budget retains feasible routes, although it may change the frontier.
A checked control has an accepted source theorem but no feasible route
under a zero translation budget. Another has two incomparable resource
vectors and no least vector. Neither resource infeasibility nor the lack
of a universally cheapest route is a logical refutation. These results
do not bound the cost of obtaining the profiles or implementing a scheduler.
For a declared workload distribution $\mu$ and randomized policy $A$,
an additional observable is
$F_{A,\mu}(B)=\Pr[\text{a valid certificate is returned within budget }B]$.
Budget exhaustion is recorded as pending. Averaging only successful
runs hides those cases and can reward a policy that solves few problems.
These workload-relative success curves complement worst-case bounds;
they do not replace semantic correctness or imply a universal ranking.
Authority assumptions are compared by a declared admissibility relation,
not assigned a universal numerical trust score. Selecting a fast backend
must not silently strengthen the axioms or weaken the required observer.

One useful contract-based optimization is to reuse a checked result at
the same provider revision. Another is to specialize a proved pure
decision operation. Reusing evidence after a changed snapshot, or treating
an external answer as a proved theorem without its stated authority,
violates the contract regardless of the apparent speed gain.

The resource interfaces above extend the original authority objects rather
than changing their semantics. Configurations, claims and certificates occur
in the encoded packet. Exact replay includes rejected certificates in the
compiler image, while arbitrary-target reflection is a separate property.
A checked counterexample has exact image replay but an additional host rule,
so it lacks global reflection and an all-proof extractor. A physical control
bounds both acceptance and refusal by nine steps: four for copying, four
for handoff, and one for the checker. The internal stack alphabets are required to be
finite in the operational authority interface.

The argument-vector join is a concrete instance of executed composition.
For fields with encoded lengths $n_i$, emitted lengths $m_i$ and original
scalar clock bounds $c_i$, its clock is
\[
  2+\sum_i(c_i+2n_i+2m_i+4).
\]
The caller physically transfers each input, collects each output and
reverses the complete accumulator. A refused scalar argument contributes
an empty framed field at its original index, distinct from an absent
field. The instantiated HOL renderer has a proved cubic upper bound in
the complete framed input length. Independent decoding of any completed
run recovers the original per-field accepted term or refusal. The decoder
also distinguishes malformed framing from a correctly framed refused field.

The list-level join begins with the original serialized Pattern list.
The existing list framer earns the declared arity before dispatching to the
vector renderer. A physically emitted final status distinguishes a wrong
arity from a successful empty vector, while inner refusal fields retain
their positions. For a fixed authored arity and original input length $N$,
the joined machine has the executed bound
\[
  \mathsf{steps}\le 3000200000(N+1)^3.
\]
The bound charges both intermediate transfers, scope admission, rendering,
status emission and cleanup. Its independent output decoder distinguishes
malformed framing, arity refusal and individual-field refusal. This is
still a qualified argument subroutine; generated axiom fields and the
complete certificate driver require their own joins.

The authored-rule join additionally starts from the original complete rule
instance. It executes first-match lookup, authored arity, every argument's
scope admission and rendering in one clock. For each fixed authored
calculus its bound is $3000300000(N+1)^3$; retaining the complete original
instance raises this to $3000500000(N+1)^3$ and charges the extra physical
copying. Missing rules, duplicate-name lookup, wrong arity and invalid unused
arguments are covered by the same execution law. Rule side conditions and
child judgments remain obligations of the full certificate driver.

The executable table is qualified separately from the mathematical finite
machine record. Structural instruction embeddings remove enumeration data
for finite control and register types from runtime arguments; checked
transition and initialization equalities preserve the original machine's
steps. This representation change does not turn the abstract step bound into
a wall-clock or native-allocation bound, and compiling the authored rule
table remains a separate uniformity obligation.

The SPEC field serializer now constructs all eighteen original primitive
occurrences for a fixed typed predicate and incoming theorem. Its three
independent dynamic fields contain the complete raw operand, its original
named representative and the occurrence origin. The output agrees byte for
byte with the existing checked chronological expansion, including its
intermediate claims, parent references, hypotheses and axiom dependencies.
For fixed template length $L$ and supplied field-packet length $N$, its
actual indexed machine runs in at most $4(L+1)(N+1)$ steps. The independent
certificate decoder recovers the checked block. This field serializer alone
assumes that the supplied named representative matches the raw operand.
A higher-order operand control produces 140,148 bytes in 318,520 counted
transitions and replays through the original checker. Future roots, a
self-parent, inconsistent raw/named fields and trailing bytes are refused.

The new naming machine derives the original named operand from its raw
applicative spine in at most $3n+4$ transitions. Constant names, type
annotations and provenance payloads are copied unchanged, including lambda
syntax stored inside opaque source data. Its domain includes every rendered
source argument. Five positive and six refusal execution controls cover
nested applications, repeated ordered children, Unicode, opaque binders and
definition provenance; arbitrary higher-order term spines are outside this
particular naming pass.

A single composed machine now starts with only the raw rendered argument
and origin. It derives the named field, retains and frames the two supplied
fields, and emits the original SPEC block. If $L$ is the fixed permuted
emission-template length and $N$ the two-field input length, the proved
clock is $(8(L+1)+64)(N+1)$, including both computations and every physical
handoff. Every terminating run on this input domain decodes to an original
checked eighteen-node block. This stage's input remains the raw operand;
the next composition adds source admission and rendering.
Two executions start with an opaque source binder and repeated ordered
arguments respectively. They take 424,819 and 849,587 transitions, emit
154,940 and 209,792 bytes, and replay as the original eighteen-node blocks
with clean auxiliary stacks. Eight mutations of references, trailing bytes
and argument fields are refused by the independent readout and checker.

The source-input composition now starts with the complete original Pattern
and origin. It checks scope and canonical binder metadata, renders and parses
the canonical HOL operand, computes its named form and emits SPEC, with every
physical handoff included. Its clock is
\[
  \bigl(3001030000 + 60000(L+1)\bigr)(N+1)^3,
\]
where $N$ measures the source packet and $L$ the fixed permuted emission
template. Rejected scope or metadata produces no decodable certificate.
Every accepted terminating readout earns source admission and the original
relative checker judgment, including all eighteen origins. Three compiled
readout controls pass original replay, four invalid source arguments refuse,
and six certificate mutations refuse. These thirteen controls exercise the
semantic readout; the general clock is established by the kernel-checked
execution theorem, rather than a measured physical run of this larger
composition. Arbitrary malformed-wire admission, changing-predicate control
generation and the whole translator driver remain further obligations.

The changing-predicate composition is now established for an original
authored row and a compiled specialization stage. Runtime input consists
only of the original Pattern argument list and occurrence origin. The
machine executes the original argument count and scope checks, renders and
names each operand, retains the complete ordered fields, selects the proper
prefix and current argument, and emits all eighteen original SPEC nodes.
The incoming theorem and predicate are generated from the row and earlier
arguments. An invalid argument that the rule body does not use still
refuses. Its cubic bound includes the actual compiled descriptor; generating
that descriptor from a runtime calculus remains a separate obligation.

Authored node dispatch now executes original rule-name selection and uses
that row's premise count. Its child preparation computes the original
instantiated premises, compares the complete requested conclusion, and
pairs the premises with submitted proofs in order. The quadratic clock
includes argument admission, repeated instantiation, copying and refusal
cleanup. An independently decoded successful output earns an original
source rule application and exact child occurrences. Child-proof validity,
graph insertion and chronological cache invariants are not inferred from
that preparation result.

Graph-address addition now reads two original natural-codec fields and
physically propagates binary carries before restoring the original output
codec. The $57N+64$ bound depends on encoded lengths, including numbers
beyond fixed machine integers. Twelve complete instruction runs check
original decoding, exact original transition counts and scratch cleanup.
A fixed offset vector of descriptor length $D$ has an actual composed
sequence machine, with bound $80(D+1)(N+1)$ on a runtime graph base of
encoded length $N$. Its independent readout preserves order and duplicates,
and its internal-parent addresses agree with the original block relocation.
Eleven complete sequence instruction runs check exact ordered readouts,
including eighteen- and fifty-eight-node offsets and 257-bit addresses.
External-root lookup authorization, whole block-relocation execution
qualification and chronological insertion remain further joins.

For a compiled block topology with admitted parent and root bounds, physical
relocation now selects the original runtime base, earns all internal
addresses, retains the original payload and external-root fields, and emits
the complete original certificate codec. The resulting word decodes to the
original block expansion. Original replay supplies the topology bounds;
checked earlier-root lookup and a checked local block supply original
checker acceptance after insertion. The step bound is
$128(D+1)(L+1)(N+1)$, charging both the address-plan length $D$ and
relocated-emission template length $L$. This composition has a checked
execution proof and polynomial realization. Whole instruction-run
qualification, earlier-root authorization, malformed packet admission and
chronological cache insertion remain separate joins.

The theory should extend these abstractions rather than replace them.
The name ``native inference kernel'' records a programme of parametric
native checking; it is neither a synonym for replay correctness nor a
theorem of universality. These notes do not resolve or redefine that
programme. Its historical account belongs with the existing library
documentation.

\paragraph{Remaining theorem packages.}
(1) Complete real-proof end-to-end qualification on the original checkers,
including shared claims and refusal paths.
(2) Assemble executed translation bounds for every arrow used in an
operational polynomial-equivalence claim, with physical handoffs.
(3) Extend hosting to binding rule schemas with typed adequacy and
explicit substitution evidence.
(4) Join signature/module interpretations and model transport, including
conservative definition histories.
(5) Complete source-visible whole-run GSLT observations and their native
type transport, including effects and provider revision.
(6) Prove the optimization contracts and measure whole costs.

Existing capacity theorems support this programme, but do not discharge
its remaining packages. A real HOL proof checked independently is a
valuable integration control; it does not replace an arbitrary-input
correctness theorem. Conversely, a polynomial certificate theorem does
not make a large emitted specification convenient. Verified extraction
to a costed intermediate language is a promising way to reduce repeated
hand-built machine proofs~\cite{forster}; its use requires an actual
verified connection to the current translator and chosen machine model.
The time-and-space invariance result for weak call-by-value lambda
calculus~\cite{reasonable} is another useful input, but its displayed
Boolean-output theorem does not by itself supply an output-sensitive
bound for writing arbitrary translated proof certificates.
