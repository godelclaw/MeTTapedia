# Constructive compositional colouring: lab tools

These are program checks behind the zigzag tube-ergodicity theorem and the pentagon-complement census. Nothing in this directory is kernel-checked. The Lean counterparts are the modules `ZigzagRingCursor`, `ZigzagRingDynamics`, `ZigzagRingErgodicity` and `GoertzelV24PentagonTubeSaturation`.

## Conventions

- **Colours** are the three nonzero elements of the Klein four-group. The C++ tools encode them as 1, 2, 3, so the third colour of two distinct colours is their xor. Text output writes 0, 1, 2.
- **Admissible words.** A boundary word of length k is admissible when every colour occurs a number of times with the parity of k. Every boundary word of a cubic piece is admissible (cut parity). There are (3^k + 3(−1)^k)/4 admissible words.
- **Support** of a piece: the admissible boundary words that extend to a proper 3-edge-colouring of the piece. Supports are invariant under colour permutations, so the tools decide one representative per orbit: the restricted growth string over 0, 1, 2.
- **Density**: support size divided by the number of admissible words. Gluing two pieces along the same ports is colourable iff their supports intersect. Densities summing to more than 1 force this.
- **Normal form**: cubic plane graphs with no cyclic edge cut of size at most 4 and all faces of length at least 5. These are the duals of 5-connected triangulations, generated by `plantri -c5m5 -d`.

## Building

```sh
g++ -O2 -std=c++17 -o ring_sectors ring_sectors.cpp
g++ -O2 -std=c++17 -o ring_swap_lemma ring_swap_lemma.cpp
g++ -O2 -std=c++17 -o piece_support piece_support.cpp
g++ -O2 -std=c++17 -o pentagon_census pentagon_census.cpp
```

The Python scripts need `networkx`. `replay_exceptions.py` needs a SAT solver; CaDiCaL is the default, and `--solver PATH` overrides it. The census needs plantri 5.5 and fullgen, from the plantri package at https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/plantri/.

## The zigzag ring

One ring with input word x and output word y is a choice of colours a_i ≠ x_i with y_i = a_i + x_{i+1} + a_{i+1} ≠ 0. Here a_i is the colour of the zigzag edge from input vertex i to output vertex i.

`./ring_sectors 5 12` reports, for each k:
- the number of admissible words, and the number of related pairs (`edges`);
- the strongly connected classes of the word graph, and whether every class is closed (no transient words);
- whether colour counts are conserved along every pair;
- class sizes (size x number) and periods (period: number);
- how the colour-count classes decompose.

| k | admissible | classes | class sizes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 60 | 3 | 20x3 |
| 6 | 183 | 22 | 1x3 3x6 6x12 90x1 |
| 7 | 546 | 6 | 42x3 140x3 |
| 8 | 1641 | 60 | 1x3 2x3 4x9 8x42 420x3 |
| 9 | 4920 | 10 | 72x3 504x6 1680x1 |
| 10 | 14763 | 171 | 1x3 5x18 10x144 1260x3 3150x3 |
| 11 | 44286 | 15 | 110x3 1320x6 2772x3 9240x3 |
| 12 | 132861 | 547 | 1x3 2x3 3x6 4x3 6x27 12x495 2970x3 13860x6 34650x1 |

For every k: all classes are closed, colour counts are conserved, every period is 1, and every count class using all three colours is a single class. Count classes missing a colour split into rotation orbits.

`./ring_swap_lemma 5 13` checks two statements for every admissible word:
- the pure shift, "shift(x) is reachable in two rings": no failures for any k;
- the swap, "shift(swap_j x) is reachable in two rings, for each j with x_j ≠ x_{j+1}": no failures for words using all three colours (3,454,386 tests at k = 13), and failure for every swap of a two-colour word.

`python3 ring_checks.py 5 8` checks that the relation equals the cursor process, and that every output from a word missing a colour is the word itself or its rotation. There are no mismatches and no violations.

- `maps`: maps read;

## The six exceptions

`exceptional_pentagons.json` holds the six non-full complements, one record per line of a JSON array. Each line has the map's plantri index, its rotation, the pentagon and the missing orbit representatives. It was produced by:

```sh
plantri -c5m5 -d -a 17 | ./pentagon_census --log x17.jsonl
plantri -c5m5 -d -a 22 | ./pentagon_census --log x22.jsonl
python3 -c 'import json,sys; r=[json.loads(l) for f in sys.argv[1:] for l in open(f)]; print("[\n" + ",\n".join(json.dumps(x) for x in r) + "\n]")' x17.jsonl x22.jsonl > exceptional_pentagons.json
```

`python3 replay_exceptions.py` rechecks each record without the census code:
- the normal form: sphericity, face lengths, brute-force absence of cyclic cuts of size at most 4, and dual enumeration of nontrivial cyclic 5-cuts;
- the support, deciding all 3^5 spoke words with the SAT solver.

| map (plantri index) | vertices | faces | missing orbit |
|---|---|---|---|
| n = 17 #1, first pentagon | 30 | 12 pentagons, 5 hexagons | 01222 |
| n = 17 #1, second pentagon | 30 | 12 pentagons, 5 hexagons | 01112 |
| n = 17 #2, first pentagon | 30 | 12 pentagons, 5 hexagons | 01222 |
| n = 17 #2, second pentagon | 30 | 12 pentagons, 5 hexagons | 00012 |
| n = 22 #116 | 40 | 15 pentagons, 4 hexagons, 3 heptagons | 01200 |
| n = 22 #285 | 40 | 16 pentagons, 3 hexagons, 2 heptagons, 1 octagon | 00012 |

Every exception has the same profile:
- it is in normal form, with no nontrivial cyclic 5-cut;
- it supports 54 of the 60 admissible words, and no inadmissible word extends;
- the six missing words form one colour-permutation orbit of good words (three cyclically consecutive spokes of equal colour);
- 24 of the pentagon's own 30 words remain, so each such map is still colourable through that pentagon.
